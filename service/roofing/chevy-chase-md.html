Chevy Chase's Six Permit Jurisdictions — The Most Important Thing to Confirm Before Any Roofing Work Begins

Chevy Chase is not a single municipality — it is a collection of independent villages and unincorporated county territory, each with its own permit authority. A contractor who does not know which jurisdiction applies to your specific parcel will file at the wrong office, fail to obtain a permit, and leave you with unpermitted roofing work regardless of what they represent. DMV General Contracting Group confirms the correct permit authority by parcel number at the free consultation — before any scope is discussed or any permit is prepared.

🏛️ Village of Chevy Chase

Oldest and largest Chevy Chase village. Own municipal permit office. All roofing permits for Village of Chevy Chase properties filed here — not MoCo DPS.

🏛️ Chevy Chase Section 3

Separate incorporated village with its own permit authority. Roofing permits filed with Section 3's municipal office — distinct from the Village of Chevy Chase.

🏛️ Chevy Chase Section 5

Another independent incorporated section. Own permit office for all construction including roofing. Not the same office as Section 3 or the Village.

🏛️ Village of Somerset

Small incorporated village within the Chevy Chase area. Own permit authority for roofing and all construction work performed within Somerset's limits.

🏛️ Village of Friendship Heights

Incorporated village bordering DC. Own permit office for roofing and construction. Not to be confused with the DC neighborhood of the same name.

🏛️ Unincorporated MoCo

Some Chevy Chase-area properties fall outside all village limits. These file roofing permits with MoCo DPS at 2425 Reedie Drive, Wheaton.

Why parcel confirmation matters — not neighborhood name: Two adjacent homes in Chevy Chase with identical mailing addresses may file roofing permits at different offices depending on which village boundary their parcels fall within. A contractor who files by neighborhood name rather than parcel location will get it wrong for a meaningful percentage of Chevy Chase properties. DMV General Contracting Group confirms jurisdiction by parcel at the free consultation — no assumptions, no guessing.

Chevy Chase's Historic Home Roofing Conditions — What We Assess

🏛️ Chevy Chase's 1910s–1940s Colonials, Tudors & Georgian Revivals — Specific Roofing Conditions

Chevy Chase's estate-level historic homes are among the most architecturally significant residential properties in Montgomery County — and their rooflines reflect the architectural ambitions of their era. The roofing conditions we assess in these homes are consistently different from those in standard mid-century suburban construction:

  • Slate roofing — still present on many Chevy Chase historic homes: A significant number of Chevy Chase's oldest colonials and tudors still have original or early-replacement slate — which may have 30–60+ years of remaining service life if assessed correctly and maintained. Slate assessment is a specialized evaluation: individual slate failure, deteriorated flashing, and ridge condition are each assessed separately to determine whether targeted repair or full replacement is warranted. DMV General Contracting Group performs slate assessment as a standard part of every Chevy Chase roof inspection on homes of the appropriate vintage.
  • Steep pitches and complex geometry: Chevy Chase's tudor revival homes — with their characteristic cross-gables, steep pitches, half-timbered dormers, and multiple roof planes — create significantly more flashing and transition points per square foot of roof than a simple gable. Each transition is a potential leak source. The steep pitch also increases installation complexity and labor relative to lower-slope residential roofing.
  • Original chimneys — mortar, crown, and flashing conditions: Chevy Chase's brick chimneys from the 1910s–1940s commonly have deteriorated mortar joints, cracked or absent chimney crowns, and failed step and counter-flashing — making the chimney the single most common active leak source in these homes. Every Chevy Chase roof inspection includes a dedicated chimney assessment.
  • Wood plank sheathing in oldest homes: The earliest Chevy Chase construction predates plywood — original 1×6 or 1×8 diagonal board sheathing is present beneath existing roofing on some of the town's oldest homes. Condition assessed after tear-off; gaps between boards affect new shingle fastening and may require overlay or replacement before new installation.
  • Period-appropriate material expectations: Chevy Chase's historic streetscapes have a visual consistency — dark slate tones, weathered wood profiles, and muted colors — that asphalt shingles in bright or commercial colors disrupt visually. Material and color selection that respects the home's architectural character is discussed at every Chevy Chase consultation.

Roof Repair vs. Roof Replacement in Chevy Chase, MD

Repair — When It Makes Sense

For Chevy Chase homes with slate roofing, targeted repair — replacing individual failed slates, repairing chimney flashing, resealing valleys — can extend a well-maintained slate roof's service life by decades at a fraction of full replacement cost. For homes with asphalt shingles under 15 years old, localized storm damage or a single flashing failure is typically repairable without full replacement.

Replacement — When It Makes Sense

Replacement is warranted when slate is deteriorated beyond targeted repair, when asphalt shingles are at end of service life with widespread granule loss, when multiple leak sources exist across the roof, or when the sheathing condition warrants full access. The complexity of Chevy Chase's historic rooflines means that a professional assessment — not appearance from the ground — is required to make this determination accurately.

Slate-Specific Assessment

Slate replacement decisions require specialized evaluation: individual slate failure rate, remaining slate thickness, flashing condition, and ridge deterioration are each assessed separately. Many Chevy Chase slate roofs that appear to need full replacement have 30+ years of remaining service life with targeted repair. Accurate slate assessment requires physically inspecting the roof — not estimating from the ground.
