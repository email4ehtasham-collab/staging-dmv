Rockville's Two Housing Eras — Different Roofing Profiles

🏚️ Twinbrook & West End (1950s–1960s) — Mid-Century Roofing Conditions

  • Multiple shingle layers likely: Homes reshingled once or twice since original construction may have 2–3 layers — which must be fully torn off before a new roof can be properly installed. Tear-off adds to project cost but is necessary for a durable result.
  • Original or aging flashing: Chimney step flashing, valley flashing, and roof-to-wall junctions in Twinbrook and West End homes are often original or from early updates — deteriorated flashing is a primary leak source assessed at every inspection.
  • Inadequate ventilation common: 1950s–1960s homes were built without modern ridge and soffit ventilation systems. Inadequate ventilation shortens shingle life significantly and is addressed as part of full replacement projects.
  • Wood sheathing in oldest homes: The earliest Twinbrook construction may have original wood board sheathing rather than plywood — condition assessed when existing roofing is removed.
  • Permit: City of Rockville, 111 Maryland Avenue

🏘️ King Farm, Congressional & Fallsgrove (1999–2015) — Modern Roofing, HOA Coordination

  • Approaching first replacement cycle: Homes built 1999–2015 with original 25–30 year architectural shingles are now 10–25 years old — many approaching or reaching end of service life, showing granule loss, curling edges, and isolated failures after storms.
  • Modern sheathing and ventilation: Plywood or OSB sheathing and code-compliant ventilation make replacement more straightforward than older Twinbrook properties — single layer tear-off, sound sheathing, and adequate ventilation already in place.
  • HOA material and color approval: King Farm, Congressional, and Fallsgrove HOA communities typically specify approved shingle colors and materials. Replacement outside approved specifications — even for the same original material — may require ARC approval before installation begins.
  • Permit: City of Rockville, 111 Maryland Ave (most); MoCo DPS (some edge parcels)

Roof Repair vs. Roof Replacement in Rockville, MD

Repair — When It Makes Sense

Repair is appropriate when damage is localized — isolated storm damage on a roof with sound shingles elsewhere, a single flashing failure, or minor chimney crown repair. For King Farm and Congressional homes with roofs under 15 years old and one isolated issue, repair extends useful roof life without full replacement cost.

Replacement — When It Makes Sense

Replacement is appropriate when shingles are at or past their service life, when multiple shingle layers must be torn off regardless, when flashing failures are widespread across multiple locations, or when granule loss is extensive. For Twinbrook and West End homes with 25+ year old roofs, replacement is typically more cost-effective than ongoing repair.

Professional Inspection First

An inspection identifies whether the issue is localized or systemic, whether multiple shingle layers are present, and the condition of flashing, sheathing, and ventilation — giving you the information needed to make a repair-versus-replacement decision based on actual conditions rather than guesswork.
