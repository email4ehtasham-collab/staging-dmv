Chevy Chase's Village Structure — What It Means for Your Basement Permit

The single most important thing to understand about renovation permitting in Chevy Chase is that “Chevy Chase, MD” is not a single municipality. It is a group of separately incorporated villages and unincorporated communities, each of which has its own permit authority — and filing with the wrong authority creates exactly the kind of permit problems that are expensive and time-consuming to unwind.

Chevy Chase Village

Village of Chevy Chase Permits

The largest incorporated village — bounded roughly by Connecticut Avenue, Western Avenue, and Jones Bridge Road. Has its own Village Manager and Village Hall at 5906 Connecticut Avenue. All residential permits for properties within the village limits are issued by the Village, not Montgomery County.

Martin's Additions

Town of Martin's Additions Permits

Small incorporated town bordered by Brookville Road and Quincy Street. Its own mayor and council — permits issued through the town's own process. Frequently confused with unincorporated Chevy Chase due to its small size.

Chevy Chase Section 3

Section 3 Village Permits

Incorporated village along Elm Street and adjacent streets. Has its own village council and permit authority separate from Chevy Chase Village. One of the most commonly misidentified jurisdictions in the Chevy Chase area.

Chevy Chase Section 5

Section 5 Village Permits

Small incorporated village in the Chevy Chase cluster. Residents here have their own municipal government and permit process — distinct from neighboring sections and from the larger Chevy Chase Village.

Chevy Chase View

Town of Chevy Chase View Permits

Incorporated town on the eastern edge of the Chevy Chase community. Own municipal government and permit authority. Properties here are frequently filed incorrectly with Montgomery County by contractors unfamiliar with the town's boundaries.

Unincorporated Chevy Chase

Montgomery County DPS Permits

Properties with a Chevy Chase, MD address that fall outside all incorporated villages use Montgomery County DPS for permits. This includes several blocks and streets that appear to be within the Chevy Chase community but are in unincorporated county territory.

⚠️ Why This Matters: Each Chevy Chase village has its own plan review process, fee schedule, and inspection staff. A contractor who files your permit with the wrong village — or with Montgomery County when a village permit is required — will receive a permit that is unenforceable in your actual jurisdiction. Inspections cannot be conducted by the wrong authority, leaving work uninspected and creating code compliance problems at resale. DMV-GC Group uses parcel boundary data to verify the exact permit jurisdiction for every Chevy Chase address — this verification happens before any other step in the project.

What to Expect in a Chevy Chase Colonial Revival Basement

Chevy Chase's most distinctive homes — the Colonial Revival houses along Grafton Street, Leland Street, Brookville Road, and Maple Avenue — were built primarily between 1910 and 1950. Their basements reflect the construction practices of that era and present specific renovation considerations that contractors unfamiliar with older construction handle poorly.

🏛️ Pre-Renovation Assessment for Chevy Chase's Older Homes

  • Foundation type: Homes built in the 1910s–1930s frequently have poured concrete foundations with older formulations — or in some cases, block foundations with mortar joints that have degraded over decades. Neither presents an insurmountable challenge, but both require different moisture management strategies than modern poured concrete.
  • Ceiling height: Chevy Chase's Colonial Revival homes typically have basement ceiling heights of 7'0"–8'0" — more generous than contemporaneous homes in Kensington or Garrett Park, reflecting the larger lot sizes and home footprints common in this market. Most Chevy Chase basements have the height to support full living-space standards without intervention.
  • Galvanized plumbing: Homes built before 1950 in Chevy Chase often have galvanized steel supply lines running through the basement. DMV-GC Group's licensed plumbers assess all visible supply runs during the consultation and advise on replacement where corrosion is present or flow restriction is evident.
  • Knob-and-tube wiring: Present in some of Chevy Chase's oldest homes — particularly those built before 1935 that haven't been fully rewired. Licensed electrician assessment is required before renovation where knob-and-tube is identified.
  • Existing coal or oil heating infrastructure: Older boiler rooms, abandoned coal chutes, and converted oil systems are common in Chevy Chase's pre-1950 homes. Mechanical equipment consolidation or relocation as part of a basement renovation requires coordination with HVAC, plumbing, and in some cases structural trades.
  • Laundry and utility rough-ins: Many Chevy Chase homes have existing laundry areas in the basement with utility sink plumbing already in place — these can often be incorporated into a new wet bar or kitchenette at reduced cost compared to new plumbing from scratch.

Custom Wine Cellars in Chevy Chase — Built to Proper Standards

Wine cellar design is one of the most technically specific elements of basement renovation — and one where the difference between a correctly built cellar and a poorly built one only becomes visible after the collection has been damaged. DMV-GC Group builds wine cellars to the same specification in Chevy Chase as in Potomac — with the same climate control standards, vapor management, and racking quality.

🍷 Chevy Chase Wine Cellar Construction Standards

  • Temperature target: 55°F ±2°F — maintained by a dedicated split cooling unit sized to the cellar's cubic footage and insulation value. Never use a standard HVAC system to cool a wine cellar — the humidity control is incompatible with wine storage requirements.
  • Humidity target: 60–70% relative humidity — maintained by the cooling unit and, where needed, a supplemental humidifier. Below 50% causes corks to dry out and allow oxidation; above 75% promotes mold on labels and racking.
  • Vapor barrier: Continuous 6-mil poly vapor barrier on all walls and ceiling before any insulation — preventing warm humid air from the conditioned space outside from entering the cellar envelope and condensing on cold surfaces.
  • Insulation: Minimum R-19 closed-cell spray foam on all walls and ceiling — provides both insulation value and a second vapor control layer in a single application.
  • Door: Solid-core insulated door with full perimeter compression seal — the door is the most common single point of failure in wine cellar climate control. Glass doors require insulated glass units, not standard single-pane.
  • Racking: Redwood or mahogany racking designed for the collection mix — standard Bordeaux, magnums, champagne, and case storage. These species are traditional for wine cellars because they don't off-gas at wine storage temperatures.
  • Lighting: UV-filtered LED only — standard LED or incandescent generates heat and UV light degrades wine through glass bottles over extended exposure periods.
  • Tasting area: Many Chevy Chase wine cellars incorporate an adjacent tasting room or wet bar — separate from the climate-controlled storage area but connected through a glass door or window to display the collection.

Moisture Management in Chevy Chase's Older Homes

Chevy Chase's older Colonial Revival and Tudor homes were built with foundation waterproofing approaches that predate modern standards. The good news: many of these homes have already had some waterproofing work done by previous owners over the decades — sump pits, drain tile systems, and exterior grading work are common. The assessment step determines what's already in place and what, if anything, still needs attention before finishing begins.

DMV-GC Group's pre-renovation moisture assessment in Chevy Chase covers: foundation wall type and condition, existing sump pit and pump status (including battery backup), grading and downspout discharge, window well condition and drainage, and any evidence of historical seepage or flooding. Assessment findings are provided in writing before any design or pricing is finalized.

💧 Chevy Chase Moisture Note: Homes in the lower-lying areas of Chevy Chase — particularly those on streets that slope toward Rock Creek or Coquelin Run — may have more active groundwater conditions than homes on higher ground. DMV-GC Group flags this during the consultation for properties in lower terrain areas, and designs the moisture management system accordingly.
