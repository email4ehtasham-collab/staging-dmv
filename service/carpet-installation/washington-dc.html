DC Carpet Projects — Two Distinct Contexts

🏛️ What Makes Washington DC Carpet Projects Different from Maryland

  • DLCP license required for all DC contractor work: Maryland MHIC license — the standard for Montgomery County contractors — is not valid in Washington DC. DC requires a separate DLCP (Department of Licensing and Consumer Protection) contractor license for all home improvement work performed in the District. DMV General Contracting Group holds DLCP license #410526000209, covering all DC carpet installation, flooring, and home improvement work. A Maryland-only contractor performing carpet installation in DC is working without the required DC license — Chevy Chase MD neighbors are a common source of referrals to unlicensed DC work that homeowners discover only later.
  • DC rowhouses — original hardwood very likely under old carpet: Capitol Hill Victorians, Georgetown Federals, Cleveland Park tudors, Petworth foursquares, and most DC rowhouses built before 1950 were constructed when oak, pine, and heart pine hardwood were the standard residential floor material. Wall-to-wall carpet came later — typically layered over original flooring in the 1950s through 1980s. Many DC rowhouse owners have never assessed what is beneath their current carpet. DMV General Contracting Group checks at every DC rowhouse estimate — and tells homeowners honestly when what we find is worth refinishing rather than covering again.
  • DC condo and co-op buildings — contractor requirements, not carpet approval: Most DC condo and co-op boards do not require board approval for carpet installation itself — carpet is generally considered owner-controlled interior finish work. What DC buildings do often require is that contractors working in the building meet specific insurance coverage levels, register with building management before the first installation day, and follow building protocols for elevator reservation and installation hours. DMV General Contracting Group carries the general liability and workers' compensation insurance levels required by DC condo buildings, navigates contractor registration with building management in-house, and confirms all building-specific requirements at the estimate appointment so nothing delays the scheduled installation day.
  • English basements — below-grade moisture management: DC rowhouses commonly have English basement units — separately rented or owner-occupied below-grade levels that function as independent living spaces. These below-grade units sit on concrete slabs and require the same moisture-barrier padding approach as any other below-grade living space: standard carpet padding directly on a below-grade concrete slab creates mold conditions within 2–5 years in DC's climate, regardless of how dry the English basement appears. Moisture-barrier padding is standard practice on every DC English basement carpet project we install.
  • DC DOB permit not required for carpet installation: DC Department of Buildings permits are required for structural work, electrical, plumbing, and significant interior alterations — carpet installation is interior finish work and falls entirely outside the DC DOB permit requirement. No DC DOB permit, no inspection, and no filing required for carpet installation anywhere in the District.

DC Condo & Co-op Buildings — What Requires Board Approval and What Does Not

🏢 DC Condo Carpet — Board Approval for Carpet Itself vs. Contractor Requirements

Most DC condo and co-op boards distinguish between modifications that require board approval — structural changes, exterior modifications, major alterations — and owner-controlled interior finish work that does not. Carpet installation generally falls in the latter category: you can replace your carpet without submitting an alteration request to the board. What DC buildings do require is that the contractor performing the work meet the building's insurance minimums, register with building management before commencing work, and follow the building's protocols for freight elevator reservation, installation hours, and debris removal. DMV General Contracting Group handles all of this in-house: we carry general liability and workers' compensation coverage meeting DC building requirements, contact building management at the estimate stage to confirm contractor requirements, complete any required contractor registration before scheduling installation, and reserve the freight elevator for material delivery. DC condo owners tell us this coordination — the part most contractors leave to the homeowner — is what separates a smooth installation day from a delayed or interrupted one. We ask about your building's specific requirements at the estimate appointment and manage the process from there.

English Basement Carpet — Moisture-Barrier Pad Is Non-Negotiable in DC

💧 DC rowhouse English basements and below-grade condo levels sit on concrete slabs that transmit ground moisture upward year-round — DC's mid-Atlantic climate and urban ground moisture conditions make this as consistent as anywhere in the region. Standard carpet padding on a below-grade concrete slab creates mold conditions within 2–5 years, producing the musty smell that English basement tenants and owners report without obvious flooding. Moisture-barrier padding — with its sealed bottom layer blocking slab moisture transmission — is standard practice on every DC English basement and below-grade carpet project we install. If a previous English basement carpet installation developed mustiness or mold without flooding, standard padding on a concrete slab is almost certainly the cause. DMV General Contracting Group installs moisture-barrier padding in every DC below-grade space without exception — not as an upgrade option, but as the correct installation for that substrate.
