Why Kensington Carpet Projects Need Local Expertise

🏛️ The Kensington Two-Era Housing Case — Explained Honestly

  • The Historic District's Victorian homes often hide original hardwood under old carpet: Kensington's 1890s–1920s Queen Anne, Georgian Revival, and Victorian Cottage homes were commonly built with wide-plank hardwood subfloors, since wall-to-wall carpet wasn't standard practice when these homes were constructed. Before recommending a full recarpet, we check what's actually underneath — some Kensington homeowners discover original flooring worth refinishing rather than covering again, which changes the scope of the project entirely.
  • Original staircases and trim need careful handling: Balloon-framed construction, hand-finished stair stringers, and original trim profiles are common in Kensington's older homes — the same features local architects specifically flag as worth preserving during any renovation work. We size and place tack strips and fasteners to avoid damaging this woodwork, and test-fit on older homes where the trim's condition is uncertain before committing to a full installation.
  • Mid-century neighborhoods have a different, more straightforward profile: Rock Creek Hills and Kensington Heights took their current form largely in the 1950s and 1960s postwar building boom, with standard mid-20th-century subfloor and stair construction. Carpet projects here move faster since there's less need for the extra care required in the historic core.
  • Historic District review does not extend to interior carpet: The Town of Kensington's preservation review governs exterior changes — siding, roofing, additions, and similar modifications visible from the street. Carpet material, color, and style inside your home, even inside a Historic District property, are entirely your choice with no review process to plan around.

Frequently Asked Questions — Carpet Installation in Kensington, MD

What is the best carpet for a Kensington Victorian-era home?
For Kensington's 1890s–1920s Victorians and foursquares in the Historic District — homes on streets like Prospect Street, Calvert Place, and Montgomery — solution-dyed nylon or SmartStrand triexta is our most frequent recommendation for secondary rooms, offering durability and stain resistance well suited to a family home. For a primary suite or formal room where feel matters most, wool remains the premium choice. What differs on Kensington's oldest homes is the subfloor underneath: original wide-plank flooring and balloon framing are common, and we assess what's actually there before installing rather than assuming a modern plywood subfloor. For Kensington's mid-century homes in Rock Creek Hills, Kensington Heights, and Parkwood, subfloor conditions are typically more straightforward, and the same fiber recommendations apply.
Will installing new carpet on my Kensington home's original staircase damage the historic trim?
Not when installed correctly, which is exactly the concern on Kensington's older Victorian homes. Original staircases in the Historic District often carry balloon-framed construction, hand-finished trim, and stair details worth preserving — the same features architects working in Kensington specifically flag as worth protecting during any renovation. We install carpet using tack strips and fasteners sized and placed to avoid damaging original stair stringers, risers, and trim profiles, and we test-fit on a small area first on any home where the original woodwork's condition is uncertain. On Kensington's mid-century homes in Rock Creek Hills and Kensington Heights, staircases are typically standard mid-20th-century construction and this is less of a concern.
Does Kensington's Historic District Commission need to approve interior carpet?
No. The Town of Kensington's historic preservation review, like every historic district review process, governs exterior changes — siding, roofing, windows, additions, and similar modifications visible from the street. Carpet material, color, and style inside your Kensington home, including inside a Historic District property, are entirely your choice and are not subject to that review. DMV General Contracting Group can confirm your specific property's historic status and any relevant considerations if you'd like certainty before ordering carpet, but the review process itself won't slow down an interior carpet project.
What's likely under my old carpet, and should I consider refinishing hardwood instead?
It depends on your home's era. In Kensington's Historic District, original wide-plank hardwood subflooring is common under old carpet, since wall-to-wall carpet wasn't standard when these 1890s–1920s homes were built — some homeowners find flooring in good enough condition to refinish rather than cover again, which changes both the scope and the look of the finished room. In the mid-century neighborhoods of Rock Creek Hills, Kensington Heights, and Parkwood, subfloor is more likely to be plywood without the same refinishing potential. DMV General Contracting Group checks what's actually underneath at the estimate appointment before recommending carpet as the default path, and can bring in our flooring team if refinishing is the better fit for your home.
