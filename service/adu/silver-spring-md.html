Types of ADUs We Build in Silver Spring, MD

Silver Spring's postwar housing stock — primarily split-levels, colonials, Cape Cods, and ranches built in the 1950s–1970s — offers practical ADU opportunities at every price point. Here is a realistic assessment of the ADU types that work best in Silver Spring:

Best for Split-Levels

Split-Level Lower-Level ADU

Silver Spring's dominant housing type — the postwar split-level — has a lower level that is partially above grade, naturally lit, and often already equipped with an exterior side door. This makes split-level lower-level ADU conversions the most natural and cost-effective ADU path in Silver Spring. Good ceiling heights (7'4"–8'2"), existing natural light, and often an existing separate entrance dramatically reduce the complexity and cost compared to a fully below-grade basement ADU. Includes egress window compliance, kitchen or kitchenette, bathroom, fire separation, and Montgomery County COO.

Typical Cost: $60,000 – $110,000
Full Basement ADU

Below-Grade Basement ADU Conversion

Silver Spring's colonials and Cape Cods have full below-grade basements — often 700–1,000+ sq ft — that are strong ADU conversion candidates. Egress window installation, separate entrance (side or rear), full kitchen, bathroom with ejector pump if needed, fire-rated ceiling separation, and Montgomery County COO. A full below-grade basement ADU in a Silver Spring colonial can be a genuinely spacious 1–2 bedroom unit — not a cramped studio.

Typical Cost: $65,000 – $120,000
Where Garage Exists

Garage Conversion ADU

Where a detached or attached garage exists on the property, conversion to an ADU is one of the faster and more cost-effective paths — particularly on Silver Spring's smaller suburban lots where a detached new-construction ADU would be difficult to site. Conversion includes insulation, HVAC, plumbing, egress, interior finishes, kitchen, and bathroom — all with Montgomery County permits and COO.

Typical Cost: $60,000 – $110,000
For Larger Lots

Attached In-Law Suite / Detached ADU

On Silver Spring's larger lots — particularly in Four Corners and Burnt Mills where homes sit on more generous parcels — an attached in-law suite addition or a small detached backyard cottage may be feasible. Silver Spring Split-Level ADUs — Why They're the Most Practical Option in the Market

Silver Spring has one of the highest concentrations of split-level homes in Montgomery County — built extensively throughout the 1950s–1970s when the split-level was the dominant suburban housing format. For ADU purposes, the split-level's lower level has specific advantages that make it the most practical — and often most cost-effective — ADU conversion in this market.

☀️

Natural Light Already Present

Split-level lower levels are 3–5 feet above grade on one side — allowing full-height windows that bring natural light into the ADU without egress window excavation. This dramatically improves the livability and rental appeal of the ADU compared to a fully dark below-grade basement apartment.

📏

Code-Compliant Ceiling Height

Split-level lower levels in Silver Spring typically measure 7'4"–8'2" — comfortably above the 7-foot minimum required for habitable ADU space. This eliminates one of the most common constraints in basement ADU projects and allows a standard finished ceiling that feels comfortable and open.

🚪

Existing Separate Entrance

Many Silver Spring split-levels have an original side or rear exterior door on the lower level — the door homeowners use daily. This existing entrance often satisfies the separate entrance requirement for an ADU without the cost of cutting a new opening in the foundation wall.

🔧

Existing Plumbing Rough-Ins

A significant percentage of Silver Spring split-levels were built with a half bath or laundry rough-in on the lower level. Completing an existing rough-in rather than cutting new drain trenches through the slab reduces the bathroom addition cost significantly — sometimes by $5,000–$10,000.

💰

Lower Total Project Cost

Because natural light, ceiling height, and often a separate entrance already exist, split-level ADU conversions in Silver Spring require less pre-ADU preparatory work than fully below-grade basement ADUs. The result is a faster, more cost-effective conversion that still generates the same rental income.

🏘️

Strong Rental Appeal

A split-level ADU with natural light feels dramatically more like a proper apartment than a below-grade basement unit. This appeals to a broader renter pool and supports higher rents — typically $100–$200/month more than a comparable below-grade unit in the same neighborhood.

Montgomery County ADU Requirements — What Silver Spring Homeowners Need to Know

Silver Spring is in unincorporated Montgomery County — all ADU permits are processed through Montgomery County's Department of Permitting Services (DPS). Here are the key requirements that apply to Silver Spring ADU projects:

📋 Montgomery County ADU Ordinance — Key Rules for Silver Spring

  • Lot size minimum: 6,000 sq ft — the vast majority of Silver Spring single-family properties qualify.
  • ADU size maximum: 1,200 sq ft or 50% of the main home's gross floor area, whichever is less. For most Silver Spring homes (1,200–2,200 sq ft), the 50% rule is the binding limit — meaning an ADU in a 1,400 sq ft Silver Spring Cape Cod cannot exceed 700 sq ft.
  • Egress in sleeping areas: Any room used as a bedroom in the ADU must have egress window compliance — minimum 5.7 sq ft opening, 24" height, 20" width, 44" max sill height. Split-level existing windows may already meet this requirement on the above-grade side wall.
  • Separate entrance: The ADU must have its own entrance — many Silver Spring split-levels already have this. For below-grade ADUs where a separate entrance doesn't exist, DMV-GC Group designs and builds one as part of the project.
  • Kitchen or kitchenette: Food preparation capability required — at minimum a sink, counter space, refrigerator, and cooking source. A full kitchen with range and dishwasher is preferred for rental appeal.
  • Full bathroom: Toilet, sink, and shower or tub — licensed plumbing with county inspection.
  • Fire-rated separation: The ceiling-floor assembly between the ADU and main dwelling must achieve 1-hour fire rating.
  • Owner-occupancy requirement: The property owner must reside in either the main home or the ADU. An investment property where neither unit is owner-occupied does not qualify for an ADU permit under the current Montgomery County ordinance.
  • Certificate of Occupancy: Required before the ADU can be legally occupied or rented. DMV-GC Group manages the COO application and all required Montgomery County inspections as a standard part of every Silver Spring ADU project.
