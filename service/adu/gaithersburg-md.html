City of Gaithersburg vs Montgomery County — Which Permit Authority Governs Your ADU?

This is the single most important question to answer correctly before any Gaithersburg ADU project begins — and the most commonly mishandled. Gaithersburg's mailing address covers both the incorporated city and surrounding unincorporated county land. The permit authority is determined by your property's parcel location, not your mailing address.

🏛️ City of Gaithersburg Permits

  • Issuing authority: City of Gaithersburg Department of Public Works and Planning
  • Covers: Properties within the incorporated city limits
  • Neighborhoods typically in City: Kentlands, Lakelands, Olde Towne, Lakeland, Diamond Farms, Flower Hill
  • City has its own plan review staff and inspection schedule
  • City ADU rules must comply with Montgomery County's ADU ordinance for zoning, plus City building code for construction
  • HOA ARC approval required for Kentlands and Lakelands before city permits are filed

📋 Montgomery County DPS Permits

  • Issuing authority: Montgomery County Department of Permitting Services
  • Covers: Properties in unincorporated Montgomery County with Gaithersburg mailing addresses
  • Neighborhoods typically in County: Crown Farm, Washingtonian Center area, Rio, parts of Quince Orchard area
  • County ADU ordinance and building code apply
  • Some county-jurisdiction Gaithersburg properties still have HOA requirements — verified separately from permit jurisdiction
  • Montgomery County COO required before ADU occupancy
⚠️ Common Contractor Mistake: Many contractors assume all Gaithersburg properties use the same permit authority. Filing a City permit application for a county-jurisdiction property — or a county application for a city-jurisdiction property — results in invalid permits, uninspected work, and a COO that cannot be issued. DMV-GC Group verifies jurisdiction using parcel boundary data at the start of every Gaithersburg project. This verification is free, takes less than a day, and eliminates the risk entirely.

HOA & ARC Coordination for Gaithersburg's Planned Communities

Several of Gaithersburg's most active ADU markets are governed by HOAs with Architectural Review Committees — adding a design approval step before permits can be filed. DMV-GC Group handles all HOA coordination as a standard part of every applicable Gaithersburg ADU project.

🏘️ Kentlands

Kentlands is a New Urbanist community designed by Andres Duany and Elizabeth Plater-Zyberk — one of the most architecturally cohesive planned communities in the DC area. The Kentlands Community Foundation's ARC maintains strict design standards for all exterior modifications. Any ADU work affecting the exterior — new entrance, new windows, addition — requires ARC review before City of Gaithersburg permits are filed. Basement ADUs with no exterior modification typically proceed with city permits only. DMV-GC Group designs all Kentlands ADU additions to ARC standards from the start, minimizing revision requests.

🏘️ Lakelands

Lakelands is a sister community to Kentlands — same New Urbanist design principles, adjacent location, and similar ARC process. Exterior ADU modifications require Lakelands HOA ARC approval before city permits. Interior basement ADUs with no exterior change typically do not. DMV-GC Group is familiar with Lakelands' ARC review requirements and designs all Lakelands ADU additions for architectural compatibility with the community's character.

🏘️ Crown Farm

Crown Farm is a newer mixed-use development in unincorporated Montgomery County — requiring county permits, not city permits. Crown Farm's HOA has its own design standards for modifications. ADU projects in Crown Farm must comply with both Montgomery County's ADU ordinance and Crown Farm's HOA guidelines. DMV-GC Group handles both layers of the approval process simultaneously.

🏘️ Other Gaithersburg HOA Communities

Many of Gaithersburg's established neighborhoods — Flower Hill, Diamond Farms, Quince Orchard area developments — also have HOAs with varying levels of ARC authority. DMV-GC Group identifies the applicable HOA and its specific ADU-relevant requirements for every Gaithersburg property during the free consultation, before any design or permit work begins.

Gaithersburg's Newer Construction — Why Basement ADUs Are Practical Here

One of Gaithersburg's underappreciated ADU advantages is its proportion of newer construction — Kentlands (1990s–2000s), Lakelands (2000s–2010s), and Crown Farm (2000s–2010s) all have homes built with basement specifications that are more favorable for ADU conversion than older stock. Here is why:

🏗️ Newer Construction Basement ADU Advantages in Gaithersburg

  • Higher standard ceiling heights: Homes built since the 1990s in Gaithersburg commonly have 8'0"–9'0" basement ceiling heights — well above the 7-foot habitable space minimum. This eliminates ceiling height as a constraint and creates comfortable, open-feeling ADU spaces.
  • Poured concrete foundations: Newer Gaithersburg construction uses poured concrete foundations rather than the concrete block found in older Wheaton and Silver Spring homes — meaning interior moisture management is more straightforward and less costly.
  • Rough-in plumbing: Many Kentlands, Lakelands, and Crown Farm homes were built with basement plumbing rough-ins — a drain stub-out in the slab for a future bathroom. Completing an existing rough-in rather than cutting new drain trenches saves $6,000–$12,000 on the bathroom addition. DMV-GC Group checks for rough-ins during every consultation for newer Gaithersburg homes.
  • Egress window rough openings: Some newer Gaithersburg homes have basement egress window openings already framed in — or at minimum, window wells already excavated — reducing or eliminating the egress window installation cost.
  • Modern electrical panels: 200-amp panels with adequate capacity for ADU circuits are standard in newer Gaithersburg construction — meaning a sub-panel for the ADU can typically be added without upgrading the main panel.
  • Wider staircase access: Newer construction basements often have wider stair configurations — improving the sense of entrance to the ADU and simplifying the separate entrance design compared to older narrow basement stairways.
