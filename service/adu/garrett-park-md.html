Garrett Park's National Register Historic District — What It Means for ADU Construction

The Town of Garrett Park was platted in 1887 as a Victorian-era railroad suburb along the B&O Railroad's Metropolitan Branch — today's MARC Brunswick Line. The town's remarkably intact collection of late Victorian, Queen Anne, Folk Victorian, and early Craftsman homes earned it a listing on the National Register of Historic Places as a historic district. This designation is one of the most significant in Montgomery County and shapes how ADU construction should be approached here.

🏠 What the National Register Does NOT Restrict

The National Register of Historic Places listing does not give the federal government authority over private property renovations. Interior modifications — finishing a basement, converting a garage interior, adding a bathroom — are not subject to federal historic review and proceed with Town of Garrett Park building permits like any other construction. The National Register designation alone does not restrict what Garrett Park homeowners can do inside their own homes.

🏛️ What Requires Careful Consideration

Exterior modifications that affect the historic character of a Garrett Park home — new openings in primary street-facing facades, changes to historic porch or entry features, new additions visible from the street — should be designed with sensitivity to the historic character. For any exterior scope that could be considered an "adverse effect" on a listed historic property, consultation with Maryland's State Historic Preservation Office (SHPO) may be appropriate. DMV-GC Group assesses every Garrett Park exterior ADU scope for historic review implications before design begins.

🪟 Egress Windows — Front vs Rear

Egress window installation for basement ADUs requires cutting a new opening in the foundation wall. For Garrett Park homes, the location matters: a new egress window on the rear or side of the home — not visible from the public street — has minimal historic impact and typically proceeds with Town permits only. A new opening in the front foundation wall — visible from the street — requires more careful design and may warrant SHPO review. DMV-GC Group designs all Garrett Park egress window installations to minimize historic impact, siting them at the rear or least-visible side of the home wherever the floor plan allows.

🚗 Rear Garage Conversions — Lower Impact

Garrett Park's rear-yard detached garages — accessed from alleys behind the homes — are generally less visible from the primary street and thus have lower historic impact when converted to an ADU. A well-executed garage conversion that retains the garage's exterior character while creating a functional interior ADU is one of the most historically sensitive ADU paths available in Garrett Park. DMV-GC Group designs garage conversions to maintain the exterior appearance of the original structure wherever possible.

Town of Garrett Park Permit Process for ADUs

Like neighboring Kensington, Garrett Park is an incorporated town with its own permit authority — entirely separate from Montgomery County DPS. All ADU permits in Garrett Park must be obtained from the Town, not the County.

📋 Town of Garrett Park ADU Permits — What to Know

  • Permit authority: Town of Garrett Park, 4600 Oxford Street, Garrett Park, MD 20896. All building, electrical, and plumbing permits for properties within the town limits are issued by the Town — not by Montgomery County DPS.
  • Zoning compliance: While building permits come from the Town, zoning requirements — ADU lot size minimum, size limits, setbacks — are governed by Montgomery County's ADU ordinance, which applies throughout unincorporated and incorporated county territory. DMV-GC Group verifies compliance with both the county's zoning requirements and the Town's building permit requirements before filing any application.
  • Plan review: The Town reviews submitted drawings before issuing permits. Required drawings include floor plan, egress window locations, separate entrance design, electrical layout, plumbing plan, and any structural modifications. DMV-GC Group prepares all required drawings in-house.
  • Historic compatibility: The Town's review for exterior modifications considers compatibility with Garrett Park's historic character. DMV-GC Group designs all exterior modifications — entrance additions, egress windows, garage conversion exteriors — to be compatible with the Victorian and Craftsman character of Garrett Park's homes, facilitating smoother Town review.
  • Certificate of Occupancy: Required before the ADU can be legally rented or occupied. Issued by the Town of Garrett Park after all required inspections pass. DMV-GC Group manages the COO process as part of every ADU project.
  • Jurisdiction verification: A very small number of properties near the Garrett Park boundary use Montgomery County DPS permits rather than Town permits. DMV-GC Group verifies the correct jurisdiction for every Garrett Park address using parcel boundary data before filing anything.
⚠️ Permit Warning: Contractors who file Garrett Park ADU permits with Montgomery County DPS receive county permits — but Town of Garrett Park inspectors will not conduct inspections for projects within the town limits under county authority. The result is uninspected work, no valid Town Certificate of Occupancy, and an ADU that cannot legally be rented. DMV-GC Group eliminates this risk by verifying jurisdiction at the start of every Garrett Park project.

ADU Construction in Garrett Park's Older Homes — Real Constraints, Honestly Assessed

Garrett Park's Victorian and Craftsman homes from the 1880s–1930s are architecturally irreplaceable — and they present specific construction realities for ADU projects that a contractor experienced only in newer suburban homes will not anticipate. DMV-GC Group assesses all of these during the free consultation, so there are no surprises mid-project.

🔧 Historic Home ADU Construction Realities in Garrett Park

  • Foundation type: Garrett Park's oldest homes — 1880s–1910s — commonly have rubble stone or early brick foundations. These are more porous than poured concrete and require interior moisture management rather than exterior waterproofing. The masonry quality and pointing condition must be assessed before any basement ADU is designed. Some foundations have been partially repaired or reinforced over the decades — the current condition, not the original construction, is what matters.
  • Basement ceiling height: Victorian basements were functional utility spaces, not living areas. Ceiling heights of 6'4"–7'4" are common — at or below the 7-foot minimum required for habitable ADU space. DMV-GC Group measures every square foot of ceiling height in Garrett Park basements during the consultation. Areas below the minimum cannot count as habitable ADU space. Where HVAC and plumbing rerouting can recover a few inches of clearance, we identify and price that option.
  • Galvanized plumbing: Homes built before 1950 in Garrett Park often retain galvanized steel supply lines — which corrode from the inside over decades, restricting flow and eventually failing. For a basement ADU where a tenant depends on water pressure, corroded galvanized supply lines must be replaced before the unit is rented. DMV-GC Group's licensed plumbers assess supply line condition and provide a replacement recommendation during the consultation for every pre-1950 Garrett Park home.
  • Electrical systems: Older Garrett Park homes may have original or early-replacement electrical panels with limited capacity for ADU circuits. A sub-panel for the ADU requires adequate capacity in the main panel — if the main panel is at or near capacity, an upgrade is required before ADU electrical work can be sized and installed. DMV-GC Group's licensed electricians assess panel capacity during the consultation.
  • Knob-and-tube wiring: Some of Garrett Park's oldest homes retain segments of original knob-and-tube wiring — an early 20th century wiring system that cannot be buried under insulation and is incompatible with modern circuit protection. Where knob-and-tube is present in the basement ADU zone, its scope and condition must be assessed and appropriate remediation designed before ADU electrical work proceeds.
  • Tree canopy and exterior access: Garrett Park's dense mature tree canopy — one of the community's defining characteristics — can affect equipment access to rear yards for any exterior foundation or drainage work. Delivery and equipment access routes must be planned before any exterior scope is scheduled. DMV-GC Group identifies access constraints during the site visit.
