ADU Construction in Kensington's Historic Homes — What's Different

Building an ADU in a Kensington Victorian or Craftsman home is not the same as building one in a 1990s colonial or a newer suburb. The specific characteristics of Kensington's older housing stock create real-world constraints and considerations that need to be assessed honestly before any ADU project is designed or priced.

📏 Basement Ceiling Height

Victorian and Craftsman basements in Kensington frequently have ceiling heights of 6'4"–7'2" — at or just above the 7-foot minimum required for habitable ADU space. This is the most common constraint in Kensington basement ADU projects. DMV-GC Group measures every square foot of ceiling height and identifies where HVAC rerouting can recover clearance before any ADU floor plan is designed. Areas that don't meet the minimum cannot be counted as habitable ADU space.

💧 Stone & Block Foundation Moisture

Kensington's oldest homes have rubble stone or early block foundation walls that manage moisture differently than modern poured concrete. A basement ADU in a home with an unaddressed moisture history will develop moisture problems after it's rented — and fixing them requires removing finished work. DMV-GC Group's moisture assessment is a required first step before any basement ADU design is finalized. The assessment is in writing, honest, and free.

🔧 Older Plumbing & Electrical

Homes built before 1950 in Kensington often have galvanized steel supply lines and older electrical systems. For a basement ADU where a tenant will rely on water pressure and electrical capacity, these systems must be assessed and — where degraded — updated before the ADU is occupied. DMV-GC Group's licensed plumbers and electricians assess all systems in the ADU zone during the initial consultation.

🏘️ Lot Size Constraints

Kensington's historic lots tend to be smaller than most Montgomery County communities — many between 6,000 and 9,000 sq ft. This directly affects ADU feasibility: detached ADUs require enough rear yard area to meet setback requirements while leaving the ADU footprint. On smaller Kensington lots, basement and garage conversion ADUs are typically the most practical path. DMV-GC Group confirms ADU feasibility for your specific lot before any design work begins.

Town of Kensington ADU Permit Process

Kensington's incorporation as a town within Montgomery County creates a permit jurisdiction that many contractors mishandle. All construction within the Town of Kensington's limits — including ADU projects — requires permits from the Town of Kensington, not from Montgomery County DPS. Filing with the wrong authority is one of the most common and costly contractor mistakes in Kensington ADU projects.

📋 Town of Kensington ADU Permit Process — What to Expect

  • Issuing authority: Town of Kensington Town Hall. All building, electrical, and plumbing permits for ADU projects within the town limits are issued by the Town — not by Montgomery County DPS.
  • Zoning compliance: Kensington properties must comply with Montgomery County's ADU ordinance (which governs the zoning rules — lot size minimum, ADU size limits, setbacks) even though building permits are issued by the Town. DMV-GC Group verifies compliance with both the county's zoning ordinance and the Town's building permit requirements before filing anything.
  • Plan review: The Town reviews submitted drawings before issuing ADU permits. Plans must show the proposed floor plan, egress window locations, separate entrance design, kitchen and bathroom layout, electrical layout, and any structural work. DMV-GC Group prepares all required drawings in-house.
  • Architectural compatibility: For attached additions and garage conversions that modify the exterior of a historic Kensington home, the Town's review may consider whether the proposed design is compatible with the character of the existing structure. DMV-GC Group designs all Kensington ADU additions to be architecturally compatible with Victorian and Craftsman aesthetics.
  • Certificate of Occupancy: A COO is required before the ADU can be legally occupied or rented. DMV-GC Group manages the COO application and all required inspections through the Town of Kensington process.
  • Properties outside town limits: A small number of Kensington-mailing-address properties fall outside the incorporated town limits — in unincorporated Montgomery County. These properties require Montgomery County DPS permits rather than Town permits. DMV-GC Group verifies the exact jurisdiction for every Kensington address using parcel boundary data before filing anything.
⚠️ Common Mistake: Contractors who file Kensington ADU permits with Montgomery County DPS receive a permit — but the Town of Kensington's inspectors will not conduct inspections for properties within the town limits. The ADU ends up uninspected, without a valid Certificate of Occupancy, and cannot legally be rented. DMV-GC Group eliminates this risk by verifying jurisdiction at the start of every Kensington project.

Kensington Detached Garage Conversions — What Makes Them Work

Kensington's Victorian and Craftsman homes frequently have detached garages built in the 1920s–1940s — separate structures behind the house, originally designed for car storage in an era when cars were new. Many of these garages have been used for storage for decades and have never been seriously evaluated as ADU candidates. Here is what makes a Kensington garage a strong — or weak — ADU conversion candidate:

🚗 Kensington Detached Garage — ADU Feasibility Assessment

  • Minimum square footage: A functional studio ADU requires approximately 300–350 sq ft of livable space — enough for a sleeping area, bathroom, and kitchenette. A one-bedroom ADU requires 450–550+ sq ft. Kensington's older single-car garages (typically 12'x20' = 240 sq ft) may be too small for a code-compliant ADU without expansion. Two-car garages (typically 20'x20' = 400 sq ft) are often a better candidate.
  • Ceiling height: The finished ceiling height of the ADU must meet the 7-foot minimum for habitable space. Older garages with low-pitched roofs may have insufficient interior height for a standard finished ceiling — especially after adding insulation and drywall to the ceiling. DMV-GC Group measures the existing interior height during every garage assessment.
  • Foundation condition: Older Kensington garages are often built on a concrete slab with no formal foundation — or on rubble stone piers. The slab condition (cracks, settlement, moisture) must be assessed before any interior finishing. Plumbing for a bathroom requires cutting through the slab for drain installation.
  • Structural condition: Garages from the 1920s–1940s used wood framing that may have deteriorated where it contacts grade — sill plates, bottom plates, and wall sheathing near the ground. A structural assessment before conversion planning is essential for any garage of this age.
  • Utility connections: Electrical service to the garage (if present) must be evaluated for capacity — a dedicated sub-panel is required for an ADU. Water and sewer connections to the garage are rarely present and must be run from the main house — the distance affects cost significantly.
  • Town of Kensington setbacks: The existing garage must meet the Town of Kensington's setback requirements for an ADU use — or a variance may be needed if the structure is non-conforming. DMV-GC Group confirms setback compliance for every Kensington garage before any conversion is designed.
