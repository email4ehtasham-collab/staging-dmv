Types of ADUs We Build in Washington, DC

DC's rowhouse housing stock — primarily built between the 1880s and 1940s across Northwest, Northeast, and Southeast DC — creates specific ADU opportunities that differ from suburban Maryland. Here are the ADU types that are most practical in DC's rowhouse neighborhoods:

Most Common DC ADU Type

English Basement ADU Conversion

The English basement — the semi-below-grade level of a DC rowhouse with its own entrance below the main stoop — is the dominant ADU type in DC's rowhouse neighborhoods. It is often the most practical because the separate entrance is typically already in place, the level is partially above grade giving natural light from rear windows, and ceiling heights in English basements commonly reach 7'4″–8'6″. A legal English basement ADU requires a separate entrance, egress window compliance in sleeping areas, full kitchen, full bathroom (with ejector pump where needed), fire-rated ceiling separation, and a DC Certificate of Occupancy. Rents $1,700–$3,000/month depending on neighborhood and size.

Typical Cost: $90,000 – $150,000
Full Below-Grade Basement

Rowhouse Basement ADU Conversion

Some DC rowhouses have a fully below-grade basement below the English basement level — a sub-basement with limited ceiling height. Where ceiling heights meet the 7-foot minimum and the space is large enough for habitable use, this level can also be converted to a legal ADU unit. More commonly, the English basement is the ADU and the sub-basement serves as storage and mechanical. DMV-GC Group assesses the full vertical stack of every DC rowhouse consultation to identify which level is the right ADU candidate.

Typical Cost: $85,000 – $140,000
Where Rear Structure Exists

Carriage House / Rear Structure ADU

Some DC properties — particularly in Capitol Hill, Georgetown, and older NW DC neighborhoods — have rear carriage houses or outbuildings accessed from the alley behind the home. Where these structures have sufficient square footage and are structurally sound, conversion to a legal ADU is feasible. Rear alley-accessed structures have minimal street-facing historic impact. DC DOB permits and COO required. DLCP license required for all DC construction.

Typical Cost: $95,000 – $160,000
For Detached Homes

Attached In-Law Suite / New ADU Structure

DC's rowhouse form — attached on both sides, built to the lot line — makes new construction additions and detached ADU structures impractical for most rowhouse properties. For DC's detached single-family homes — more common in upper NW DC and parts of SE and NE — attached in-law suite additions and in some cases detached ADU structures are feasible. DMV-GC Group assesses DC home type and lot configuration during the free consultation to identify what is actually buildable on your specific property.

Typical Cost: $120,000 – $200,000+

English Basement ADUs in DC — The Most Valuable Rowhouse Investment

The DC English basement ADU conversion is the highest-ROI renovation available to most DC rowhouse owners — and one of the most consequential renovations that can go wrong if handled by a contractor without DC-specific experience. Here is what makes a successful English basement ADU conversion in DC:

🏠 What Qualifies as a Legal DC ADU

A legal English basement ADU in DC requires — at minimum — a separate entrance, egress window compliance in sleeping areas, a full kitchen (range, refrigerator, sink, counter space), a full bathroom (toilet, sink, shower or tub), fire-rated ceiling separation between the ADU and the main dwelling (typically 1-hour assembly), smoke and CO interconnected detectors, minimum 7-foot ceiling height in habitable rooms, and a DC Certificate of Occupancy. DMV-GC Group designs every English basement ADU to meet all of these requirements before construction begins.

🚿 Ejector Pump — When It's Required

DC rowhouse basements and English basements are frequently at or below the elevation of the city sewer line — meaning waste from below-grade bathroom fixtures cannot drain by gravity. DMV-GC Group's licensed plumbers assess the relative elevation of the existing drain invert versus the proposed bathroom floor on every DC ADU project before any plumbing is designed. Where an ejector pump is required, we size and install it correctly — sealed fiberglass pit, proper discharge line, separate vent stack, and battery backup for below-grade rental units where the homeowner may be away during power outages.

⚡ Separate Electrical Meter

A separate Pepco electric meter for the ADU is recommended for any DC English basement rental — allowing the tenant to pay their own electric bill, simplifying accounting, and adding value at resale. DMV-GC Group coordinates meter separation with Pepco as a standard part of most DC English basement ADU projects — handling all required electrical permit work and utility coordination in-house.

🔥 Fire-Rated Ceiling Separation

The floor-ceiling assembly between the ADU and the main dwelling must achieve the required fire rating — protecting both the tenant below and the owner above. In DC rowhouses this typically means a 1-hour rated assembly, achieved through proper drywall specification on the basement ceiling. DMV-GC Group specifies and documents the correct fire separation assembly on every DC ADU project for DC DOB plan review compliance.

DC ADU Zoning Rules — What Homeowners Need to Know

Washington DC has its own ADU regulations under the DC Zoning Regulations — distinct from Maryland's Montgomery County ADU ordinance and applicable only within the District. Key rules for DC homeowners:

📋 DC ADU Zoning Rules — Key Requirements

  • ADU types permitted in DC: DC zoning permits ADUs in residential zones as accessory dwelling units within or attached to an existing single-family home, or in a separate accessory structure on the same lot. English basement conversions in rowhouses — the most common DC ADU type — fall under the "within the existing structure" category.
  • ADU size limits: DC's ADU regulations limit the size of an accessory dwelling unit, with specific limits varying by zone. In most DC residential zones, the ADU cannot be larger than a set percentage of the gross floor area of the primary dwelling. DMV-GC Group verifies the applicable size limit for your specific DC zoning designation during the consultation.
  • One ADU per lot: DC zoning permits one ADU per single-family lot — the same limitation as Maryland's county ADU ordinances.
  • Owner-occupancy in DC: Unlike Montgomery County's ADU ordinance which requires owner-occupancy, DC's ADU regulations do not impose an owner-occupancy requirement for all ADU types. However, homeowners should verify the current DC zoning regulation applicable to their specific zone and ADU type during the consultation — regulations are subject to change.
  • DC DOB permits required: All ADU construction in DC requires permits from the DC Department of Buildings (formerly DCRA). DMV-GC Group files all required DC DOB building, electrical, and plumbing permits in-house. DC DOB permit processing for ADU projects typically takes 4–8 weeks before construction can begin.
  • Certificate of Occupancy mandatory: A DC DOB Certificate of Occupancy is required before any ADU can be legally rented or occupied. An unpermitted basement unit in DC without a COO is illegal to rent, creates insurance gaps, and is subject to fines and required correction. The COO process involves DC DOB final inspections of all building systems.
  • DLCP license required for all construction: Every contractor performing any home improvement or construction work in DC must hold a valid DLCP license. Verify your contractor's DLCP license at DC's licensing portal before signing any contract.

HPRB Historic Districts — How They Affect DC ADU Construction

A significant portion of DC's rowhouse neighborhoods are designated historic districts — meaning exterior modifications that affect the historic character of homes require review by the DC Historic Preservation Review Board (HPRB) or a Certificate of No Objection from the State Historic Preservation Office before DC DOB permits are issued.

🏛️ HPRB Review for DC ADU Projects — What Triggers It

  • Interior ADU work — no HPRB required: Finishing a basement, installing a kitchen, adding a bathroom, framing walls — all interior ADU work proceeds with standard DC DOB permits regardless of historic district status. HPRB reviews exterior changes, not interior renovations.
  • Egress window in rear — typically no HPRB: An egress window cut in the rear foundation wall — not visible from the public street — typically does not require HPRB review. DMV-GC Group sites all DC ADU egress windows at the rear of the property wherever the floor plan allows, specifically to avoid triggering historic review.
  • New front facade opening — HPRB review likely: Any new opening in the front facade of a rowhouse in a historic district — including a new egress window at the English basement level visible from the street — requires HPRB review or a Certificate of No Objection before DC DOB permits are issued.
  • English basement entrance modifications: Significant changes to the existing English basement entrance — the door, surround, or stoop configuration visible from the street — may require HPRB review depending on the specific historic district and the nature of the change.
  • DC historic districts where ADU projects most commonly encounter HPRB: Capitol Hill HD, Georgetown HD, Dupont Circle HD, Logan Circle HD, U Street HD, Strivers' Section HD, Takoma DC HD, 16th Street Heights HD.
  • Certificate of No Objection path: For minor changes, a faster "Certificate of No Objection" from the State Historic Preservation Office may be available rather than a full HPRB hearing. DMV-GC Group identifies which path applies and pursues the most efficient route for each specific project.
  • Timeline impact: HPRB review adds 4–8 weeks to the pre-construction timeline. DMV-GC Group initiates DC DOB permit applications and any required HPRB submissions simultaneously wherever possible to minimize total timeline.
⚠️ Design Strategy: DMV-GC Group's standard approach for DC ADU projects in historic districts is to design egress windows at the rear of the property from the outset — avoiding front facade modifications that would trigger HPRB review. This keeps the ADU project on a straightforward DC DOB permit path without historic review delays in the vast majority of cases.
