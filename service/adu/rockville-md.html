City of Rockville vs Montgomery County — Which Permits Your ADU?

Rockville's incorporated city limits create the same permit dual-jurisdiction that Gaithersburg homeowners face — but with Rockville-specific neighborhood boundaries. Getting this wrong means permits issued by the wrong authority, uninspected work, and no valid Certificate of Occupancy.

🏛️ City of Rockville Permits

  • Issuing authority: City of Rockville, Department of Community Development, 111 Maryland Avenue
  • Covers: Properties within the incorporated city limits
  • Neighborhoods typically in City: King Farm, Fallsgrove, West End, Twinbrook, Rockville Town Center area, College Gardens
  • City has its own plan review and inspection staff — separate from county
  • Zoning compliance still governed by Montgomery County ADU ordinance
  • King Farm and Fallsgrove HOA ARC approval required before city permits filed

📋 Montgomery County DPS Permits

  • Issuing authority: Montgomery County Department of Permitting Services
  • Covers: Unincorporated areas with Rockville mailing addresses
  • Areas typically in County: Parts of Congressional, Traville, areas north of Norbeck Road, some Rockville Pike corridor properties
  • Montgomery County ADU ordinance and building code apply
  • Some county-jurisdiction Rockville properties still have HOAs — verified separately
  • Montgomery County COO required before ADU occupancy
⚠️ Common Mistake: Rockville and Gaithersburg are the two Rockville Pike corridor cities where contractors most commonly file permits with the wrong authority. DMV-GC Group verifies jurisdiction using parcel boundary data at the start of every Rockville project — not mailing address assumption. This verification is free and takes less than one business day.

King Farm & Fallsgrove — Why Basement ADUs Work Especially Well Here

King Farm and Fallsgrove's late 1990s–2000s construction gives their basements specifications that make ADU conversion more practical and cost-effective than in older Rockville housing stock — similar to the advantages we outlined for Gaithersburg's Kentlands and Lakelands:

🏗️ Newer Construction ADU Advantages in King Farm & Fallsgrove

  • 8'–9' ceiling heights: Comfortable, open-feeling ADU spaces well above the 7-foot code minimum — eliminating ceiling height as a constraint and creating basement apartments that feel like proper living spaces rather than cramped utility areas.
  • Poured concrete foundations: More moisture-resistant than the concrete block found in older Wheaton and Silver Spring homes. Interior moisture management is simpler and less costly in King Farm and Fallsgrove homes.
  • Existing plumbing rough-ins: A significant proportion of King Farm and Fallsgrove homes were built with basement bathroom rough-ins — a drain stub-out in the slab. Completing an existing rough-in saves $6,000–$12,000 versus cutting new drain trenches through the slab. DMV-GC Group checks for rough-ins during every newer-construction Rockville consultation.
  • 200-amp electrical panels: Standard in 1990s–2000s construction — providing adequate capacity for ADU sub-panels without main panel upgrades in most cases.
  • Modern framing and insulation: 2x6 exterior wall framing with proper insulation is standard in King Farm and Fallsgrove — meaning basement perimeter walls require less intervention before finish work than older homes with minimal insulation.
  • Wider basement stairs: Newer construction typically provides 36"–42" stair widths — better for ADU access than the 28"–32" stairs found in older mid-century homes.
