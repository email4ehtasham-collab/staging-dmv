North Bethesda's Two Housing Eras — Different Addition Approaches

North Bethesda's housing stock spans two meaningfully different eras, each with its own structural profile, addition potential, and design approach:

🏠 1950s–1970s Ranches & Colonials — The Core Addition Market

  • Neighborhoods: Luxmanor, Tilden Woods, Garrett Park Estates, Strathmore, Old Georgetown Estates
  • Typical home: 1,400–2,400 sq ft ranch or split-level on a generous lot; often 0.3–0.7 acres
  • Most common addition scope: Second-story addition converting ranch to two-story; master suite addition; rear family room expansion
  • Structural conditions: Original electrical panels often 100-amp — typically upgraded during addition scope; load-bearing walls at non-obvious locations in split-level plans; crawlspace or slab foundation assessed before design
  • Addition potential: High — generous lots, favorable setbacks, homes structurally well-suited to vertical expansion
  • Why add on: Lot size and neighborhood quality far exceed what is available at comparable prices elsewhere in MoCo; adding on preserves the location at a fraction of moving cost

🏢 Newer Townhomes & Pike & Rose Corridor — Limited But Targeted Addition Scope

  • Neighborhoods: Pike & Rose corridor, White Flint area, newer attached-home communities
  • Typical home: 1,800–2,800 sq ft townhome or attached home, 3–4 stories; built 2000s–2020s
  • Most common addition scope: Rooftop deck addition, lower-level expansion, bump-out of main living level; HOA review required in most townhome communities
  • Structural conditions: Modern construction with engineered lumber framing; structural additions require engineering review of existing floor and roof systems
  • Addition potential: Moderate — constrained by townhome lot size and HOA covenants but targeted expansions are achievable
  • HOA: Most Pike & Rose area townhome communities have HOA covenants requiring ARC approval for exterior modifications — managed in-house by DMV General Contracting Group

1

Free In-Home Consultation — Zoning Review, Housing Era Assessment, Feasibility

We visit your North Bethesda home and assess which addition type fits your lot, your home's structural era, and your household's needs. For older ranches and split-levels, we identify load-bearing wall locations, assess the electrical panel, and check the foundation. For newer townhomes, we identify HOA requirements and applicable setbacks. Full MoCo zoning and setback review included. No charge, no obligation.

2

Architectural Design & 3D Renderings

Our in-house architects develop full design documentation — floor plans, exterior elevations, interior layout, and 3D renderings. For ranch-to-two-story additions, the new second-story roofline is designed to read as originally two-story, not as a floor added on top of a ranch. Construction team reviews every design choice in parallel to confirm buildability.

3

Structural Engineering — Foundation to Roof

Our in-house structural engineers assess the existing foundation and bearing wall structure, calculate loads, and specify all beams, headers, and connections. For second-story additions, foundation adequacy is the first assessment. All structural documents stamped and included in the MoCo DPS permit package.

4

MoCo DPS Permit Submission — 2425 Reedie Drive

We prepare and submit the complete MoCo DPS permit package and manage all county correspondence. MoCo DPS review typically runs 6–10 weeks for room additions and 8–12 weeks for second-story additions. You do not contact the county at any point.

5

Foundation, Framing & Exterior Shell

Construction begins with site preparation and structural framing. For second-story additions, the existing roof is removed and second-story framing rises from the original bearing walls. All framing inspected by MoCo DPS before closing. Exterior clad in materials that match the existing home.

6

Mechanical Rough-In, Interior Finishes & Final Inspections

Licensed electricians, plumbers, and HVAC technicians extend all systems — including electrical panel upgrade to 200-amp where required. All rough-in inspected by MoCo DPS. Interior finishes and the connection to the existing home completed. All final inspections scheduled, punch list walkthrough, and written warranty documentation.
