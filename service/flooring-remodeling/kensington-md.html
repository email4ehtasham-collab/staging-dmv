Kensington's Older Homes — Subfloor Realities Every Homeowner Should Understand

Before choosing a floor, it's essential to understand what's underneath it. Kensington's older homes were built with construction methods that differ fundamentally from modern homes — and the right flooring choice depends on your subfloor type.

😀 Diagonal plank subfloors

Found in many homes built before 1950. These provide a strong, stable base but require specific installation techniques — especially if you're installing tile, which needs a decoupling membrane to prevent cracking.

😀 Original hardwood over joists

¾-inch strip hardwood nailed directly over joists — the classic Kensington setup. These floors can almost always be refinished rather than replaced, saving thousands.

⚠️ Pine or softwood subfloors

Found in some 1920s–40s homes, particularly in bedrooms and closets. Softer woods dent and scratch easily, and may indicate there was carpet over them. More prep needed before installing new flooring.

😀 Concrete slab (first-floor or basement areas)

For slab-on-grade installations, moisture testing is essential before any flooring goes down. LVP or engineered wood is the recommended solution for these areas.

Why it matters: The single biggest mistake we see is homeowners selecting the floor first and ignoring the subfloor. Selecting the right flooring for your subfloor saves you from costly failures 6–24 months after installation.

The key factor is remaining wood thickness above the tongue. Original ¾-inch strip hardwood needs at least 3/16" above the tongue to safely sand without compromising the mechanical connection. DMV-GC Group checks remaining thickness during the free in-home consultation using a thickness gauge. If sufficient thickness remains and the wood is structurally sound (not cupped, rotted, or buckled), refinishing is almost always the right choice — more authentic and significantly less expensive than replacement.

The most common cause of tile failure in older Kensington homes is installation directly over diagonal plank subfloor boards without an uncoupling membrane. Diagonal plank subfloors have joints between boards that move seasonally — when the boards expand and contract, that movement telegraphs through a rigid tile installation and cracks the tiles or grout joints. An uncoupling membrane (such as Schlüter DITRA) absorbs this movement and prevents it from reaching the tile above. DMV-GC Group installs uncoupling membrane as a standard part of every tile project on older Kensington subfloors — the step that determines whether tile lasts 50 years or 2.

Yes — LVP is often the best choice for first-floor rooms in Kensington homes with crawlspace foundations where moisture is a concern. Because LVP is 100% waterproof and dimensionally stable regardless of humidity, it will not cup, buckle, or squeak in response to seasonal moisture changes. DMV-GC Group recommends a vapor barrier under LVP in crawlspace homes and uses an embedded moisture meter to check humidity levels in the wood subfloor before installation.
