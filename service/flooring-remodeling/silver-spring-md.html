Silver Spring's Split-Levels Need a Two-Zone Flooring Strategy — Upper Level and Lower Level Are Not the Same Problem

Silver Spring's most common housing type — the 1950s–70s split-level in Four Corners, Woodside, Wheaton Hills, and along Colesville Road — presents a flooring challenge that is unique to this home configuration. The upper main level sits above grade: hardwood or engineered hardwood works well here, original floors may be refinishable, and the subfloor conditions are typical of postwar construction. The lower level is at or below grade: solid hardwood will cup and buckle from seasonal moisture, and the right flooring here is LVP or another moisture-tolerant material. A contractor who recommends solid hardwood throughout a Silver Spring split-level — upper and lower — without distinguishing between the two zones is setting the homeowner up for flooring failure on the lower level within a few years. DMV-GC Group assesses both zones independently and recommends the right material for each.

The Split-Level Two-Zone Flooring Strategy — Silver Spring's Most Important Flooring Decision

Silver Spring's 1950s–70s split-levels in Four Corners, Woodside, and Wheaton Hills are the community's most common single-family home type — and they present a flooring challenge that is specific to the split-level configuration. Understanding the two zones before any material is selected prevents the most common flooring mistake in Silver Spring: solid hardwood installed below grade, where it will fail.

🪵 Upper Main Level

Above Grade — Hardwood Zone
  • Living room, dining room, kitchen (main level)
  • Bedrooms on the upper floor
  • Above-grade — no seasonal moisture concern from below
  • Original hardwood often present — refinish if viable ($3–$6/sq ft)
  • New hardwood installation appropriate if original is gone
  • Engineered hardwood preferred over solid where crawlspace or slab is adjacent
  • LVP also appropriate for kitchen and high-traffic zones
  • Target: hardwood or engineered hardwood for main living areas

💧 Lower Level

At / Below Grade — LVP Zone
  • Family room, laundry, lower-level bedrooms
  • Frequently converted to ADU or rental unit
  • At or below grade — seasonal moisture from slab or below
  • Solid hardwood will cup, buckle, and squeak here — wrong choice
  • Engineered hardwood is marginal — still moisture-sensitive
  • LVP: 100% waterproof, floats over minor slab irregularities, realistic wood look
  • Porcelain tile also appropriate for lower-level bathrooms and laundry
  • Target: premium LVP throughout lower level
💡 The Right Visual Continuity: Homeowners often worry that LVP on the lower level will look mismatched with hardwood on the upper level. Premium LVP — specified in a wood-tone and plank width that complements the upper-level hardwood — reads as a natural continuation of the same floor when viewed through the stairwell. DMV-GC Group selects the LVP color and pattern to complement each specific upper-level hardwood stain for every Silver Spring split-level project.

Flooring Types We Install in Silver Spring, MD

Upper Level — First Choice

Hardwood Refinishing

The right first choice for Silver Spring's Four Corners and Woodside colonials with surviving original strip hardwood — typically 2.25-inch or 3-inch oak installed in the 1940s–60s. DMV-GC Group checks remaining wood thickness before recommending refinishing. When viable — and it often is — refinishing restores the original floor at $3–$6 per square foot, preserving the postwar character of Silver Spring's established neighborhoods at a fraction of replacement cost. Full stain color range from natural warm oak to rich dark walnut; matte and satin finishes most popular in Silver Spring's current renovation market.

Lifespan: Extends original floor 20–40 more years  |  Cost: $3–$6/sq ft
Upper Level — New Installation

Solid & Engineered Hardwood

When original hardwood is beyond refinishing or absent, new solid or engineered hardwood on the upper main level maintains the warmth and resale value that Silver Spring buyers expect at this price point. White oak in natural or warm medium stain is the most popular new hardwood choice in Silver Spring's renovation market. Engineered hardwood is the safer choice for upper-level rooms adjacent to slab or crawlspace where some moisture transmission is possible. Solid hardwood for confirmed dry above-grade installations with plywood subfloor.

Lifespan: 50–100 yrs (solid) · 25–40 yrs (engineered)  |  Cost: $8–$15/sq ft installed
Lower Level — Only Right Choice

Luxury Vinyl Plank (LVP)

LVP is the correct flooring for Silver Spring's split-level lower levels — full stop. 100% waterproof, dimensionally stable regardless of seasonal humidity swings, realistic wood look that can be matched to complement upper-level hardwood, comfortable underfoot, and durable under the heavy use that lower-level family rooms and ADU rental units receive. Premium LVP — COREtec, Shaw Floorté, Karndean — is genuinely difficult to distinguish from hardwood at casual inspection. For Silver Spring's growing ADU market, LVP is also the right choice for any converted lower-level rental unit where durability and easy replacement between tenants matters.

Lifespan: 20–30 yrs  |  Cost: $5–$10/sq ft installed
Kitchens & Bathrooms

Porcelain & Ceramic Tile

The top choice for Silver Spring bathrooms and kitchen floors — waterproof, easy to clean, and durable under active-household use. Modern large-format porcelain (18×18, 24×24) in wood-look or stone-look patterns is popular in Silver Spring kitchen renovations where a continuous material from kitchen to dining area is desired without the moisture risk of solid hardwood in the kitchen zone. Subway tile in white or soft grey is the most period-appropriate bathroom choice for Silver Spring's postwar homes. Uncoupling membrane required for tile installation on older diagonal plank subfloors in Four Corners' oldest homes.

Lifespan: 50+ yrs  |  Cost: $8–$18/sq ft installed
Bedrooms — Comfort

Carpet

Carpet remains a practical and comfortable choice for Silver Spring upper-level bedrooms — warm, quiet, and affordable. For lower-level bedrooms in split-levels (common in Silver Spring's below-grade ADU conversions), LVP is the better choice over carpet for moisture resilience and durability under rental turnover. DMV-GC Group installs carpet on upper levels and LVP or hard flooring on lower levels — matching material to zone, not applying the same material everywhere regardless of conditions.

Lifespan: 10–15 yrs  |  Cost: $4–$10/sq ft installed
Budget Option

Laminate

A cost-effective alternative for Silver Spring secondary spaces, rental units, and rooms where budget is the primary driver. Modern laminate is durable and available in realistic wood appearances. Not recommended for below-grade rooms or kitchens where moisture exposure is likely. A practical choice for Silver Spring upper-level bedrooms and home offices where a good-looking floor is needed at the most cost-effective price point.

Lifespan: 15–25 yrs  |  Cost: $4–$8/sq ft installed

Our Flooring Installation Process in Silver Spring

1

Free In-Home Consultation — Zone Assessment First

We visit your Silver Spring home, identify whether you have a split-level two-zone situation, assess existing floor condition and remaining thickness where refinishing is being considered, check below-grade moisture conditions for the lower level, and walk through material options with samples. For split-level homes, we discuss the upper-level and lower-level strategies separately before any material is specified.

2

Material Recommendation by Zone

We provide zone-specific material recommendations: refinishing recommendation for upper-level original hardwood if viable; new hardwood or LVP spec for upper level where refinishing is not viable; LVP specification for lower level with color and pattern selected to complement the upper-level floor visually. Full written estimate broken down by room and material, including subfloor prep costs identified during the assessment.

3

Subfloor Preparation

Upper level: any loose boards re-secured, soft spots patched, sanding sequence determined. Lower level: existing flooring removed, concrete slab cleaned and any high spots ground down, moisture barrier installed before LVP where slab moisture is present. Tile areas: uncoupling membrane or backer board installed. All subfloor prep completed before any finish material goes down.

4

Installation by Zone

Upper level: hardwood refinishing (sand, stain, finish coats) or new hardwood installation as specified. Lower level: LVP floating installation with proper expansion gaps and transitions at stairway. Bathrooms and kitchen: tile set with appropriate mortar and grout. Clean cuts, tight seams, correct transitions between all zones and materials throughout the home.

5

Trim, Transitions & Final Cleanup

Baseboards, shoe molding, stair nosings, and transition strips at all material changes — particularly at the split-level stairway between the hardwood upper level and the LVP lower level. Thorough cleanup of all debris and dust. Final walkthrough with you before we call the project complete.
