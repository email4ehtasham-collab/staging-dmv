Chevy Chase's Six Permit Jurisdictions — Confirmed by Parcel Before Any Work Begins

Two adjacent Chevy Chase homes with identical mailing addresses may file hardscaping permits at different offices depending on which village boundary their parcels fall within. A contractor who files by neighborhood name or zip code will get it wrong for a significant percentage of Chevy Chase properties. DMV General Contracting Group confirms the correct permit authority by parcel at every Chevy Chase hardscaping consultation.

🏛️ Village of Chevy Chase

Oldest and largest village. Own municipal permit office for all structural hardscaping — retaining walls, outdoor kitchens, driveway work. Not MoCo DPS.

🏛️ Chevy Chase Section 3

Separate incorporated village with its own permit office. Distinct from the Village — filing at the wrong office results in no permit issued.

🏛️ Chevy Chase Section 5

Another independent incorporated section with its own permit authority — separate from both the Village and Section 3.

🏛️ Village of Somerset

Small incorporated village within the Chevy Chase area. Own permit authority for all construction including structural hardscape elements.

🏛️ Village of Friendship Heights

Incorporated village bordering DC. Own permit office for hardscaping and construction work — not MoCo DPS, not DC DOB.

🏛️ Unincorporated MoCo

Some Chevy Chase-area properties fall outside all village limits. These file structural hardscaping permits with MoCo DPS at 2425 Reedie Drive, Wheaton.

Simple ground-level patios and walkways typically do not require a permit in any Chevy Chase jurisdiction. Structural elements — retaining walls over 30 inches, outdoor kitchens with gas or electrical connections, driveway work at public road connections, and raised patio footings — require permits filed at the correct village or county office. DMV General Contracting Group confirms permit requirements for your specific Chevy Chase project at the free consultation.

Period-Appropriate Hardscaping Materials for Chevy Chase

Chevy Chase's 1910s–1940s colonials, tudors, and georgian revivals represent some of the most architecturally significant residential properties in Montgomery County. The right hardscaping material complements this character; the wrong one contrasts with it in a way that is immediately visible to anyone who knows the community.

🪨 Natural Bluestone ✅ First Choice

The most popular estate hardscaping material for Chevy Chase's traditional colonials and georgian revivals — cool gray tone, natural texture, timeless character. Mortar-set in large-format slabs for formal terraces; irregular in sand-set for garden settings. Ages beautifully alongside Chevy Chase's mature plantings and formal gardens.

🪨 Limestone & Travertine ✅ Premium

Cut limestone for formal steps, terrace edging, and retaining wall cap stones; tumbled travertine for informal garden patio settings. Warm cream tones complement both tudor and georgian revival architectural profiles. Travertine used for outdoor kitchen areas and pool surrounds on Chevy Chase's larger estate properties.

🧱 Reclaimed or New Brick ✅ Period Authentic

The most architecturally authentic hardscaping choice for Chevy Chase's older colonials and tudor revivals with brick exterior details. Reclaimed brick from a comparable era delivers the most authentic character. Traditional patterns — running bond, herringbone — read as genuinely period rather than decorative imitation.

🪨 Fieldstone ✅ Retaining Walls & Garden Features

Local fieldstone for retaining walls, garden feature walls, and stone edging — the most natural and historically compatible hardscape material for Chevy Chase's established estate gardens. Dry-laid fieldstone settles naturally into the landscape over time, gaining a character that no engineered concrete block wall can replicate.

🧱 Concrete Pavers ⚠️ Estate Profile Only

Acceptable on Chevy Chase estate properties only in premium earth-tone profiles scaled appropriately to the estate setting. Large-format contemporary slabs in bright or unusual colors are not appropriate. Natural stone and brick are the preferred choices — concrete pavers are a budget consideration, not a design equal, in Chevy Chase's estate context.

🏗️ Stamped Concrete / Modern Porcelain ❌ Not Recommended

Contemporary stamped concrete patterns and large-format porcelain pavers are not compatible with Chevy Chase's estate architectural character. These materials read as modern additions that contrast with — rather than complement — the community's 1910s–1940s colonial and tudor streetscapes. Not recommended for any Chevy Chase hardscaping project.
