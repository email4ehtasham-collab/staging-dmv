Kensington's Unique Hardscaping Conditions

🌳 Mature Tree Root Zones — The Most Important Assessment Before Any Kensington Hardscape Excavation

Kensington's mature tree canopy — oaks, maples, elms, and specimen trees that in some cases pre-date the homes they shade — is one of the community's most valued characteristics. Mature tree root zones extend well beyond the drip line: a large oak with a 30-foot canopy spread may have significant roots extending 40–60 feet from the trunk. Hardscape excavation within that zone — even shallow base preparation for a patio or walkway — can sever major roots and permanently damage or kill the tree. DMV General Contracting Group assesses mature tree proximity and root zone extent before any Kensington hardscape layout is finalized. Patio and walkway positions are adjusted to maintain safe setbacks from significant roots where possible. Where hardscape coverage within a root zone is unavoidable and the homeowner accepts the proximity, permeable paver systems with a no-dig or minimal-dig installation method are specified to minimize root disturbance.

Town of Kensington Permit — 3710 Mitchell Street

Kensington hardscaping permits are NOT filed at Montgomery County DPS: Kensington is an incorporated municipality with its own permit office at 3710 Mitchell Street, Kensington, MD 20895 — entirely separate from Montgomery County DPS at 2425 Reedie Drive, Wheaton. Period-Appropriate Hardscaping Materials for Kensington

Kensington's pre-1950 bungalows, foursquares, and colonials have a distinct architectural character that is best served by hardscaping materials with roots in early American residential design — natural stone, brick, and wood. These materials complement the community's older homes, age well alongside mature plantings, and belong on a Kensington lot in a way that modern concrete slabs and porcelain tiles simply do not.

🪨 Irregular Flagstone ✅ First Choice

Natural irregular stone in a wide-joint sand or mortar setting — the most organic and period-appropriate hardscape surface for Kensington's craftsman bungalows and cottage-style homes. Natural variation in color and texture integrates seamlessly with established garden plantings. Wide joints planted with ground cover or gravel reinforce the naturalistic, settled character that suits Kensington's mature landscape settings.

🪨 Pennsylvania Bluestone ✅ Recommended

Uniform-cut or irregular bluestone — cool gray tone and natural texture complementing both craftsman and colonial revival profiles. More structured than irregular flagstone; appropriate for homes with more formal garden layouts or for front entry walks where a cleaner, more defined edge is preferred.

🧱 Brick ✅ Period Authentic

Reclaimed or new brick in running bond, herringbone, or basket weave — deeply embedded in early American residential character and architecturally compatible with Kensington's older colonials and foursquares. Reclaimed brick from a comparable era delivers the most authentic character. Warm tone, durable, and repairable.

🪨 Fieldstone ✅ Retaining Walls

Local fieldstone in dry-laid or mortared settings for retaining walls and garden feature walls — the most naturalistic and historically compatible retaining wall material for Kensington's established lots. Dry-laid fieldstone walls allow water to drain through the wall face, reducing hydrostatic pressure without engineered drainage systems.

🧱 Concrete Pavers ⚠️ Period Profile Only

If budget requires concrete pavers, only earth-tone tumbled or aged-profile units in smaller sizes read as period-compatible in Kensington. Large-format modern pavers in contemporary colors do not suit Kensington's pre-1950 architectural context. Even appropriate pavers are a compromise — natural stone and brick are the preferred choices.

🏗️ Stamped Concrete / Large Porcelain ❌ Not Recommended

Modern stamped concrete patterns and large-format porcelain pavers are not compatible with Kensington's pre-1950 housing character. These materials read as contemporary additions that contrast with — rather than complement — the community's craftsman and colonial architectural setting. Not recommended for Kensington properties.
