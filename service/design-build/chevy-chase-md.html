Chevy Chase Has Six Separate Permit Jurisdictions — More Than Any Other Community in Montgomery County — and Every One of Them Is Different from MoCo DPS in Wheaton

The community known as "Chevy Chase, Maryland" is not a single municipality. It is a collection of five incorporated villages — the Village of Chevy Chase, Chevy Chase Section 3, Chevy Chase Section 5, the Village of Somerset, and the Village of Friendship Heights — each with its own municipal government and its own building permit office, plus a surrounding area of unincorporated Montgomery County served by MoCo DPS in Wheaton. Six permit authorities govern a community that most people address with a single ZIP code and a single name. A contractor who assumes that Chevy Chase means "file with MoCo DPS" is wrong for every incorporated village — and a contractor who knows one village's permit process but not another's will get the boundary-area addresses wrong. Beyond the permit complexity: Chevy Chase's 1920s–1950s colonials, tudors, and cape cods are among the most architecturally significant residential homes in Maryland — estates with original old-growth hardwood floors, hand-crafted millwork, and historic details that require architects who understand what to preserve and what to upgrade.
