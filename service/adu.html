What Is an ADU? (Accessory Dwelling Unit)

An Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) — also called an in-law suite, granny flat, carriage house, or backyard cottage — is a secondary residential unit built on the same lot as a primary single-family home. ADUs are fully self-contained living spaces with their own bedroom, bathroom, and kitchen or kitchenette.

In Bethesda and throughout Montgomery County, ADUs are legal under the county's updated ADU ordinance and are growing in popularity among homeowners who want to generate rental income, house aging parents or adult children, or add significant value to their property.

ADU Zoning Rules in Bethesda & Montgomery County, MD

One of the most important parts of building an ADU in Bethesda is understanding Montgomery County's zoning requirements. DMV General Contracting Group's in-house architects and permit team are experts in local ADU regulations and handle the entire approval process for you. Here's a summary of the key rules:

Montgomery County ADU Zoning Quick Reference

Zones Allowed: R-60, R-90, R-200 and most single-family residential zones
Max ADU Size: Up to 1,200 sq ft (detached); up to 50% of primary home (attached)
Setbacks: Minimum 5 ft side/rear for detached ADU (varies by zone)
Owner Occupancy: Not required as of 2022 Montgomery County update
Parking: 1 additional space typically required
Permits Required: Building, electrical, plumbing, HVAC + zoning approval
⚠️ Important: ADU regulations in Montgomery County were updated in 2022 and continue to evolve. Lot size, zoning classification, and existing structure conditions all affect what is permitted on your specific Bethesda property. ADU vs. Home Addition — What's Right for Your Bethesda Home?

Bethesda homeowners sometimes ask whether they should build an ADU or do a traditional home addition. The key difference is independence: an ADU is a fully separate, self-contained dwelling unit with its own entrance, kitchen, and bathroom — it can be rented or occupied independently. A home addition simply expands the primary home's square footage without creating a separate living unit.

If your goal is rental income, housing a family member independently, or maximizing long-term property value, an ADU is the better investment. If your goal is simply more space within your existing home, a home addition or room expansion may be more appropriate. DMV General Contracting Group builds both — and can help you determine which option makes more sense for your specific Bethesda property during your free consultation.
