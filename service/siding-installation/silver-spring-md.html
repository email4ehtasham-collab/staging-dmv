Silver Spring Split-Levels — Siding Conditions Specific to This Housing Type

🏘️ What Silver Spring's Split-Level Exterior Profile Requires — That Most Siding Contractors Get Wrong

  • Multi-zone level transitions — the most common failure point: Silver Spring's split-level homes have exterior wall planes that step up and down at each floor level transition — the defining characteristic of split-level architecture. Where two siding zones meet at a horizontal transition (at a soffit, a band board, or a wall step), the flashing, caulking, and siding overlap detail must be executed correctly or water infiltrates the transition within 2–3 years. Inexperienced siding crews treat the split-level exterior as a series of independent wall sections and miss the transitions entirely. DMV General Contracting Group details every Silver Spring split-level transition — horizontal band boards, soffit returns, and wall plane offsets — with correct flashing and overlap before any siding panels are installed in those zones.
  • Aluminum siding over original siding — what's underneath matters: Many Silver Spring split-levels from the 1960s had aluminum siding installed directly over the original wood siding in the 1970s or 1980s — a common period practice that added a layer without removing the original. When that aluminum is removed, the condition of the original wood siding underneath determines whether it can serve as a substrate for new siding or must be removed as well before new installation. DMV General Contracting Group assesses this layering condition at the estimate and discusses the implications for scope and cost before any removal begins.
  • Open soffit conditions at split-level overhangs: Silver Spring split-levels commonly have unenclosed soffit areas at the level-change overhangs — open rafter tails rather than closed soffit panels. Re-siding these homes requires new soffit installation as part of the scope — both to properly terminate the new siding at the overhang and to improve the home's energy envelope. DMV General Contracting Group includes soffit assessment in every Silver Spring split-level siding estimate and provides soffit enclosure pricing when open rafter tails are present.
  • Original aluminum siding — lead paint and disposal: Silver Spring's original aluminum siding from the 1960s–1970s may have multiple layers of paint, including lead-based paint applied in the original factory finish or in subsequent field repaints before 1978. DMV General Contracting Group follows EPA RRP lead-safe disposal practices for aluminum siding removal where lead paint is possible based on the home's age and siding history.
  • Energy efficiency opportunity — insulated siding on older Silver Spring homes: Silver Spring's postwar split-levels and colonials were built with minimal wall insulation — often just 2×4 framing with no cavity fill or a thin fiberglass batt. Insulated vinyl or rigid foam under fiber cement during a full siding replacement adds meaningful R-value to the wall assembly without interior disruption — improving comfort and reducing heating and cooling costs significantly on Silver Spring homes where wall insulation has been a persistent weakness since original construction.

Signs Your Silver Spring Home Needs New Siding

🔧

Dented or Oxidized Aluminum

Dents, chalking white oxidation, or paint failure on original aluminum siding — the aluminum has reached end of serviceable life.

🔍

Cracking or Pulling Vinyl

Cracks along panel lengths or panels pulling away at seams — first-generation vinyl from the 1980s–90s is failing structurally.

🎨

Significantly faded siding color that no longer matches the home's trim and roof — beyond what cleaning or painting can restore.

💧

Noticeable cold air infiltration at exterior walls — original postwar siding without house wrap allows significant heat loss.

💨

Gaps at Level Transitions

Visible gaps or separating panels at split-level floor transitions — the most common infiltration point on Silver Spring split-levels.

New Siding & Energy Efficiency for Silver Spring's Postwar Homes

🌡️ Why energy efficiency matters more for Silver Spring siding than for newer homes: Silver Spring's 1950s–1970s split-levels and colonials were built before modern energy codes — many have 2×4 exterior framing with minimal or no wall insulation, no house wrap, and original siding that was installed directly over building paper that has since degraded. The total wall R-value on these homes is often R-4 to R-8 — far below the R-15 to R-21 of a modern 2×6-framed wall. A full siding replacement is the most practical opportunity to add continuous insulation to these walls without interior disruption: insulated vinyl adds R-2 to R-4; rigid foam under fiber cement adds R-3 to R-6. On a typical Silver Spring split-level with 900–1,200 sq ft of exterior wall area, that insulation improvement translates to meaningful annual energy savings and noticeably more comfortable interior walls in winter.
