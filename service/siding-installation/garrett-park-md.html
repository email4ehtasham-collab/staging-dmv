The Town of Garrett Park Permit — What Siding Contractors Must Know

📋 Garrett Park Siding Permits — 4600 Oxford Street, Not MoCo DPS

The Town of Garrett Park issues its own building permits at 4600 Oxford Street — separate from Montgomery County DPS at 2425 Reedie Drive, Wheaton. Like-for-like siding replacement in Garrett Park generally does not require a permit. However, siding projects that involve structural sheathing repair, changes to window or door openings, or addition of continuous rigid insulation beneath new siding may cross the permit threshold — and those permits must be filed with the Town of Garrett Park at 4600 Oxford Street, not with MoCo DPS. Contractors who file with MoCo DPS for a Garrett Park Town-limit property face rejection and weeks of delay. DMV General Contracting Group assesses permit requirements at the estimate and files with the Town of Garrett Park when required. A small number of Garrett Park postal code addresses fall outside Town limits in unincorporated MoCo — for those, MoCo DPS is correct. We confirm jurisdiction by parcel before any permit-required scope begins.

The National Register Historic District — What It Means for Siding in Garrett Park

🏛️ Garrett Park's Historic District Character — Why Siding Choice Matters More Here Than Elsewhere

  • The designation does not prohibit non-historic materials — but the community does: Garrett Park's National Register Historic District designation governs exterior changes visible from public streets and informs what the Town's review will look favorably upon. While vinyl siding on a Garrett Park bungalow is not federally or locally prohibited, it creates visual incongruity in a streetscape where the architectural vocabulary has been maintained with unusual consistency since original construction. Garrett Park homeowners, neighbors, and buyers are acutely aware of this distinction — and the Garrett Park resale market prices historic character measurably. A siding choice that reads as incompatible with the original bungalow diminishes property value and the community's visual coherence.
  • Interior siding work is not reviewed by any historic authority: Siding installation is exterior work and is visible from the street — it is exactly the type of modification that the historic district designation was established to guide. There is no interior equivalent. If your Garrett Park bungalow needs new exterior siding, the material, profile, and color choice matter for the historic district's character and your property value in ways that interior renovations do not.

Period-appropriate replacement means matching this profile dimension, selecting a color consistent with the original or the community's historic palette, and choosing a material — cedar, James Hardie HardiePlank, or LP SmartSide — that reads as compatible with wood construction from the street. A wide 8-inch vinyl lap siding profile in a bright modern color on a 1930 Garrett Park Craftsman cottage is the opposite of period-appropriate.
  • DMV General Contracting Group's approach to Garrett Park siding: We bring period-appropriate material samples — HardiePlank in 4-inch and 6-inch exposure, LP SmartSide in the same profiles, cedar options — alongside honest comparisons of maintenance requirements and cost. Old-Home Siding Conditions in Garrett Park — What We Assess Before Pricing

⚠️ What Garrett Park's 80–100-Year-Old Siding Hides

  • Diagonal board sheathing and rot at bottom courses: Garrett Park's 1900s–1940s homes were sheathed with diagonal boards — individual boards installed at 45-degree angles behind the original siding. At the bottom courses where siding meets the foundation, moisture accumulation over 80–100 years commonly produces rotted, soft sheathing that cannot support new siding fasteners. DMV General Contracting Group assesses visible sheathing edges at estimate and discusses repair likelihood and cost range before finalizing pricing.
  • Knob-and-tube wiring in exterior wall cavities: Garrett Park homes built before 1940 frequently have original knob-and-tube wiring running through exterior wall cavities. When siding removal exposes knob-and-tube at exterior penetration points, adding insulation around active knob-and-tube is a fire hazard and voids homeowners insurance. Our crews identify knob-and-tube at removal and advise homeowners before proceeding with any insulated siding installation.
  • Lead paint on original siding: Garrett Park homes with original pre-1978 painted siding carry lead-based paint. Siding removal on these homes requires EPA RRP lead-safe work practices — containment, wet methods, HEPA vacuum cleanup, and proper disposal. DMV General Contracting Group follows RRP lead-safe practices on all Garrett Park siding projects where lead paint is possible based on the home's age.
  • Window and door trim rot: Original wood window casings and door surrounds on Garrett Park's oldest homes develop rot at bases and sill areas — rot that becomes visible when siding is removed. New siding installed against rotted window trim will fail at those joints within 2–3 years. We assess and include trim repair in scope where needed rather than installing new siding against rotted existing wood.

Signs Your Garrett Park Bungalow Needs New Siding

🍄

Rot at Bottom Courses

Soft, punky wood at siding bases — 80–100 years of moisture contact has compromised the original wood.

🎨

Paint Failing in 2–4 Years

Paint peeling or bubbling shortly after repainting — the siding substrate has failed beyond what paint can restore.

🔍

Splitting & Checking

Horizontal cracks along wood grain — century-old cedar or pine boards reaching end of serviceable life.

💧

Interior Plaster Water Stains

Moisture staining on interior plaster walls — active infiltration through failed siding joints or sheathing.

🌡️

Cold Wall Surfaces

Exterior walls cold to the touch in winter — original siding without house wrap allows significant heat loss.

🏚️

Chalked or Faded Paint

Severely chalked paint surface that no longer holds color — the siding itself has degraded beyond surface restoration.
