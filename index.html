The Job

What Does a Residential General Contractor Do for Your Remodel?

A residential general contractor is a licensed builder who runs a home remodel from start to finish: design, permits, trades, schedule, inspections and the final walkthrough. Instead of hiring a plumber, an electrician, a framer and a tile setter one at a time, you sign one contract and deal with one company.

As your general contractor, we handle four jobs:

01

Plan and design

Our in-house architecture and engineering team turns your goals into drawings and a written scope before any demolition starts.

02

Permits and inspections

We file the applications, answer the reviewers' comments and book every inspection with the county, the city or DC.

03

Trades and schedule

We line up carpenters, electricians, plumbers and HVAC technicians in the right order, so no trade sits waiting on another.

04

Quality and handover

We check each stage against the plans and the building code, walk the finished space with you and start your warranty.

When a project needs both design and construction, our design-build remodeling service keeps the architect, the engineer and the builder on the same team.

Verified

Licensed in Maryland and DC

We hold Maryland Home Improvement Commission (MHIC) license #156178 and DC Department of Licensing and Consumer Protection (DLCP) license #410526000209. In Maryland, only MHIC-licensed contractors may sign home improvement contracts with homeowners, and doing this work without a license is a crime. You can check our number, or any contractor's, in the Maryland Department of Labor's MHIC license search.

Protected

Insured and Covered by the Guaranty Fund

We carry the general liability insurance that the MHIC requires of every licensed home improvement contractor. Because we are licensed, our clients can also file a claim with the Maryland Home Improvement Guaranty Fund if work goes wrong. The fund does not cover work by unlicensed contractors.

Cost and pricing

How Much Does a Home Remodel Cost in Maryland?

$60-$250+Per square foot for most Maryland remodels

In Maryland, most home remodels cost between $60 and $250 or more per square foot. Construction and labor costs in Maryland run higher than the national average, so your budget depends on your county, the size of your home and the scope of the work. Here is what each type of project typically costs:

Kitchen remodel

$30,000-$115,000

Typical total cost

A mid-range remodel usually runs $35,000-$75,000. Custom cabinets can take 30-40% of the budget.

Bathroom remodel

$15,000-$45,000

Typical total cost

Moving plumbing lines and custom tile work push the cost toward the top of the range.

Basement finishing

$45,000-$110,000

$30-$85 per sq ft

Adding a bathroom, a wet bar or egress windows raises the cost.

ADU

$150,000-$300,000

Typical total cost

A new detached unit. Garage and basement conversions usually cost less.

Home addition

$120,000-$325,000

$200-$400 per sq ft

A new foundation and roof extension drive most of the cost.

Whole-home renovation

$120,000-$600,000+

$100-$250 per sq ft

A full gut with structural changes or historic updates sits at the top of the range.

These are 2026 figures gathered from cost guides published by Maryland and DC remodelers. Your own price depends on the size of the space, the materials you choose, any structural work and the age of your home. After the consultation, you get a free written estimate for your project.

Is it cheaper to hire the trades yourself?

You can save the general contractor's fee, which usually runs 10 to 20 percent of the project, but you also take on the scheduling, the permits and the cost of any delay when one trade holds up the next.

One Contractor vs Separate Trades

Why Hire One General Contractor Instead of Separate Trades?

For any remodel that touches more than one trade or needs a permit, one general contractor is simpler: one contract, one schedule and one party responsible for the result.

Who is responsible if something goes wrong?

One general contractor (design-build)One company, under one contract

Best for

One general contractor (design-build)Remodels with several trades or a permit

A contractor and a general contractor are not always the same thing. A contractor can be a single trade, such as a plumber or an electrician. A general contractor holds the main contract with you and hires and manages all of the trades on the job.
