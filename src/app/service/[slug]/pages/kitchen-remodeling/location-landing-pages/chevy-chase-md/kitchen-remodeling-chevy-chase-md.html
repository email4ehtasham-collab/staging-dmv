Renovating in Chevy Chase's Distinct Villages

Unlike most Montgomery County communities, "Chevy Chase" is not a single municipality — it is a collection of separately incorporated villages and special taxing areas, each with its own local government, and in some cases, its own permitting or design review requirements layered on top of Montgomery County's process. Understanding this structure is essential to a smooth renovation, and it's an area where local experience makes a real difference.

🏛️ Chevy Chase's Incorporated Villages — What Homeowners Should Know

  • Chevy Chase Village — one of the oldest and most architecturally distinguished areas, with many homes dating to the early 1900s. Some exterior modifications may require additional village-level review beyond standard county permits.
  • Town of Chevy Chase (Section 3, Section 4, etc.) — a separately incorporated municipality with its own local government, distinct from unincorporated Chevy Chase areas.
  • Martin's Additions — a small incorporated village with its own council, located between Chevy Chase Village and Bethesda.
  • Section 5 and other unincorporated sections — fall under standard Montgomery County jurisdiction without additional village-level review.
  • Interior kitchen renovations that don't change a home's exterior typically follow standard Montgomery County permit processes regardless of which village a home sits in
  • Exterior changes — additions, bump-outs, new windows visible from the street — may require additional review depending on the specific village
  • DMV-GC Group identifies which jurisdiction applies to your specific Chevy Chase property and manages all required permits and reviews accordingly

Kitchen Remodeling for Chevy Chase's Historic Architecture

Chevy Chase was developed beginning in the 1890s as one of the area's earliest planned suburbs, connected to Washington DC by streetcar along Connecticut Avenue. Much of the original housing stock — particularly in Chevy Chase Village and Section 3 — reflects the architectural styles popular during that era of development.

Colonial Revival homes are among the most common in Chevy Chase — typically featuring symmetrical facades, formal floor plans, and kitchens that were historically positioned at the rear of the home, often smaller than modern expectations. Renovations frequently involve reconfiguring adjacent spaces — a butler's pantry, breakfast room, or rear porch — to expand the kitchen footprint while maintaining the home's formal proportions.

Tudor Revival and Craftsman homes in Chevy Chase often feature substantial original millwork, built-in cabinetry, and distinctive window proportions. Kitchen renovations in these homes benefit from custom cabinetry that echoes the home's original woodwork — DMV-GC Group's design team works to match trim profiles, wood species, and hardware styles to the home's period character.

Mid-century and contemporary homes — found throughout Chevy Chase's later-developed sections — typically offer more renovation flexibility, with open-concept conversions and full gut renovations more straightforward than in the area's oldest housing stock.
