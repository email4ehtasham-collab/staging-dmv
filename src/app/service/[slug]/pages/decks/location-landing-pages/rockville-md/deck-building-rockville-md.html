Which one governs your specific property depends on your parcel's location, not your mailing address.

Building a Deck in King Farm, Rockville — What to Know

King Farm is Rockville's most active single-family deck market — a 2000s master-planned community with generously sized lots, newer colonial construction, and an active HOA ARC. Here is what makes King Farm deck projects specific:

🏘️ King Farm Deck Project — Key Considerations

  • City of Rockville permits: King Farm is within the incorporated City of Rockville — all deck permits are filed with City DPS at 111 Maryland Avenue, not with Montgomery County. City permit processing for decks typically runs 3–5 weeks for plan review before construction can begin.
  • King Farm HOA ARC — required first: The King Farm HOA's Architectural Review Committee must approve the deck design before the City permit is filed. ARC submission requires site plan, elevation drawings, material specifications, and color samples. DMV-GC Group prepares all required documentation and submits to the King Farm ARC. We run permit preparation in parallel so the City application is ready to file the day ARC approval is received.
  • ARC-approved materials and colors: The King Farm ARC has specific preferences regarding deck material and color compatibility with the home's exterior. Composite decking in warm earthy tones — complementing King Farm's colonial architectural palette — is the most commonly approved direction. DMV-GC Group works within the ARC's design standards while selecting the best available composite product within those parameters.
  • Lot size advantage: King Farm's single-family lots are larger than most planned community developments in Montgomery County — allowing deck footprints of 280–420 sq ft in many cases without lot coverage issues. This is a meaningful advantage over Kentlands or Lakelands where smaller lots constrain deck footprint.
  • Newer construction rough-ins: King Farm's 2000s colonials were often built with sliding door or French door openings designed for future deck addition — meaning the structural connection point and door threshold height are already in place. This reduces deck connection complexity compared to retrofitting an older home without a planned deck opening.
  • Foundation type: King Farm homes are predominantly poured concrete foundation — a favorable ledger attachment substrate compared to the concrete block foundations found in Rockville's 1950s–60s stock. Standard ledger attachment per structural drawings, with proper flashing at the ledger-foundation interface.
