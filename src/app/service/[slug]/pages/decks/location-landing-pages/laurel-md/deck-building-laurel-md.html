Laurel's Three Permit Jurisdictions — Which One Governs Your Deck

Understanding which permit authority governs your specific Laurel address is the first and most consequential step in any Laurel deck project. Here is what each jurisdiction covers:

🏛️ City of Laurel

City DPS — 8103 Sandy Spring Rd
  • Incorporated city with its own permit authority
  • Address: 8103 Sandy Spring Road, Laurel, MD 20707
  • Old Laurel / historic core: City permits
  • Laurel town center area: City permits
  • Many neighborhoods along US-1 corridor: City permits
  • City has own plan review, inspection schedule
  • Verify by parcel — city boundary is not obvious from address

📋 Montgomery County

MoCo DPS — West Laurel
  • Unincorporated MoCo west of the city
  • West Laurel: Montgomery County DPS
  • Montpelier: Montgomery County DPS
  • Hammond area (west): Montgomery County DPS
  • Same MoCo DPS process as Rockville, North Bethesda
  • BGE utility territory (west of city)
  • COO required before occupancy

📋 Prince George's County

PG County DPS — East Laurel
  • Unincorporated PG County east of the city
  • Laurel Lakes: Prince George's County DPS
  • Russett: Prince George's County DPS
  • South Laurel neighborhoods: PG County DPS
  • Pepco utility territory (east of city)
  • PG County permit process differs from MoCo
  • COO required before occupancy
⚠️ Laurel Jurisdiction Warning: Laurel is the only community in the DMV-GC Group campaign area where a single city's postal addresses span three different permit authorities across two different counties. Contractors who routinely work in Montgomery County or Prince George's County individually may not know that Laurel requires them to work with the other county — or with the City — depending on the specific parcel. Types of Decks We Build in Laurel, MD

Laurel's housing spans multiple decades and styles — from 1960s colonials to 1990s planned community townhomes — each with different deck opportunities:

Old Laurel & Laurel Lakes — Most Common

Elevated Colonial Rear Deck

Laurel's 1960s–80s colonials in Old Laurel, Laurel Lakes, and along the US-1 corridor have rear elevations typically 2–5 feet above grade — accessed from a sliding door or French door at the main living level. A composite rear deck of 240–360 sq ft with wood or cable railing and stairs to the yard is the dominant project type across Laurel's single-family neighborhoods. Permit filed with City of Laurel (for city-limit properties), MoCo DPS (for Montpelier and West Laurel), or PG County DPS (for Laurel Lakes and South Laurel) — confirmed before design begins.

Typical Cost: $26,000 – $52,000
Montpelier & West Laurel

Split-Level Elevated Deck

Montpelier and West Laurel's 1970s–80s split-levels have main living floors 4–6 feet above rear grade — same elevated deck opportunity as Wheaton, Silver Spring, and North Bethesda. Composite elevated deck with stairs, optional under-deck drainage system for the 4–6 ft clearance below. Montgomery County DPS permits for West Laurel and Montpelier — same MoCo process DMV-GC Group manages for all our MoCo communities. BGE utility territory for any gas line work.

Typical Cost: $30,000 – $58,000
Russett & Planned Communities

Townhome Compact Rear Deck

Russett and other Laurel planned community townhomes have compact rear yards — typically 15–25 feet of usable depth — and HOA oversight in many cases. A compact composite rear deck of 140–220 sq ft maximizes the usable outdoor space within the lot's constraints and HOA guidelines. Prince George's County DPS permits for Russett and South Laurel planned communities. HOA coordination included where applicable. DMV-GC Group assesses rear yard dimensions and HOA requirements during the consultation before any design begins.

Typical Cost: $16,000 – $32,000
For Larger Rear Yards

Multi-Level Deck with Pergola & Features

Laurel's larger-lot single-family homes — particularly in Montpelier, West Laurel, and some Old Laurel properties near the Patuxent River corridor — support multi-level deck builds with pergola shade structure, outdoor kitchen integration, and cable railing. These are Laurel's most ambitious deck projects, on par with what we build in Silver Spring and Wheaton. Permit authority confirmed before design; HOA coordination included where applicable.

Typical Cost: $48,000 – $85,000

What is the best deck material for Laurel homes?
Composite decking — Trex, TimberTech, or Azek — is the most popular and practical choice for Laurel's mid-range single-family and townhome market. Pressure-treated is a practical choice for cost-conscious projects on homes where the resale timeline is longer. Ipe hardwood is available for premium Montpelier and Old Laurel properties where the investment level warrants it.
