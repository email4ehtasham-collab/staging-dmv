Small-Lot Deck Design in Kensington — Making the Most of What's There

Kensington's lots — typically 5,000–8,000 sq ft — are among the smaller in Montgomery County. Rear yard setback requirements and existing mature trees further constrain the buildable footprint. Within these constraints, design efficiency is the primary skill that determines whether a Kensington deck feels generous or cramped. Here are the approaches DMV-GC Group uses to maximize every square foot:

🪑

Built-In Seating

Integrated bench seating along one or two deck edges eliminates the need for bulky outdoor furniture that consumes half the deck area. A well-designed bench — with optional storage beneath — seats 6–8 guests comfortably in the same space where four chairs would fit nothing else.

🌿

Integrated Planter Boxes

Planter boxes built into the railing or deck edge define the space, add privacy, bring greenery onto the deck, and avoid the visual clutter of freestanding containers crowding a smaller deck. On a 200 sq ft Kensington deck, planters frame the space without consuming any of it.

📐

Right-Sized Proportions

A deck that is the right size for the lot and the home looks better than a deck that maxes out every inch of the setback envelope. DMV-GC Group designs Kensington decks to proportions that feel natural on the property — not to an arbitrary maximum.

🌳

Tree-Aware Layout

Kensington's rear yards often have mature trees whose root zones limit where footings can be placed. DMV-GC Group identifies significant trees and their root zones during the consultation and designs the deck layout to avoid root conflicts — sometimes using longer joist spans to clear root zones without impacting the deck surface plan.

🚪

Multiple Door Access

Where the home's rear layout allows, designing deck access from both the kitchen and the main living area makes the deck feel more integrated into daily life — not an occasionally visited appendage but a genuine extension of the home's interior flow.

💡

Lighting for Evening Use

Step lighting, post cap lights, and low-voltage string lights extend the usable hours of a Kensington deck significantly — important when the deck's limited square footage means you want to use every hour of good weather it provides.

Choosing the Right Deck Material for Kensington's Historic Homes

Kensington's 1920s–40s bungalows and craftsman homes have a specific architectural vocabulary — horizontal lines, natural materials, covered porches, and painted trim. The deck material and railing style you choose either reinforces that vocabulary or works against it. Here is an honest guide:

🟤 Composite Decking (Recommended)

Trex or TimberTech in warm brown tones reads naturally on Kensington's bungalows — it does not look conspicuously modern or suburban. The 25–30 year lifespan and no-stain maintenance make it the most practical choice for most Kensington homeowners. Specify a natural wood-grain texture rather than a smooth contemporary profile for the best fit with older homes. Pair with painted wood railings for a cohesive bungalow aesthetic.

🌲 Pressure-Treated / Cedar (Traditional)

Painted pressure-treated or stained cedar is the most historically consistent material for Kensington's bungalows — it is what these homes would have had originally, and it looks correct when painted to match the home's trim color. Requires sealing or staining every 2–3 years to prevent weathering. The lower initial cost relative to composite is offset by higher long-term maintenance. Best choice where the client specifically wants the traditional painted wood look.

🔩 Railing: Wood Baluster (Recommended for Bungalows)

Painted square-baluster wood railing — whether pressure-treated or composite post-and-rail systems — is the most architecturally appropriate railing style for Kensington's craftsman and bungalow homes. Simple, sturdy, period-appropriate. Painted to match the home's trim color it reads as intentional and integrated rather than as a generic builder deck component.

⚙️ Railing: Cable or Glass (Use with Caution)

Horizontal cable railing and frameless glass — popular on contemporary and mid-century modern homes — can look incongruous on Kensington's 1920s–30s craftsman homes. Where a client specifically wants cable or glass railing, DMV-GC Group designs the overall deck aesthetic to ensure the railing choice reads as an intentional modern contrast rather than a stylistic mismatch.

Town of Kensington Building Permits — What Homeowners Need to Know

Kensington is an incorporated Town in Montgomery County with its own municipal government and building permit authority — distinct from Montgomery County DPS. Many Kensington homeowners — and some contractors — mistakenly file deck permits with Montgomery County rather than with the Town.

📋 Town of Kensington Permit Process for Decks

  • Permit authority: Town of Kensington, 3710 Mitchell Street, Kensington, MD 20895. All building permits for properties within the town limits are filed here — not with Montgomery County DPS.
  • What requires a permit: Any new deck attached to the home, any deck over 30 inches above grade, any deck over 200 sq ft, and any structural deck replacement or rebuild. Routine deck repair and minor maintenance typically does not require a permit — but any structural modification does.
  • Town vs County jurisdiction: The Town of Kensington's boundaries do not cover all addresses with a Kensington mailing address. Some properties on the periphery of the community are in unincorporated Montgomery County and require MoCo DPS permits. DMV-GC Group verifies the correct permit authority for every Kensington-area address using parcel boundary data before any application is filed.
  • Setback requirements: The Town of Kensington has specific rear and side yard setback requirements for accessory structures. On Kensington's smaller lots — typically 5,000–8,000 sq ft — the rear setback in particular can meaningfully constrain the usable deck depth. DMV-GC Group confirms the applicable setback requirements and calculates the maximum buildable footprint for your specific lot during the consultation.
  • Structural drawings: Town of Kensington deck permits require structural drawings. DMV-GC Group's in-house team prepares all required structural documentation for permit submission.
  • Inspection schedule: The Town conducts required footing and framing inspections during construction. DMV-GC Group schedules and manages all required Town of Kensington inspections as a standard part of every project.
⚠️ Permit Jurisdiction Alert: Filing a deck permit with Montgomery County DPS when your property is within the Town of Kensington — or vice versa — produces an invalid permit and no valid inspection record. DMV-GC Group has verified this distinction on every Kensington project we have managed and catches jurisdiction errors before any permit is filed.
