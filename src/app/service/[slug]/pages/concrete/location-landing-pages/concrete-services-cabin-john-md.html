Concrete Finish Options for Cabin John, MD Homeowners

🧹 Broom Finish

A practical textured finish offering slip resistance and a clean appearance. A strong choice for driveways and walkways.

🪨 Exposed Aggregate

Reveals decorative stone aggregate for added texture and a natural appearance, especially useful around pools and slopes.

🎨 Stamped Concrete

Imprints patterns such as slate, cobblestone, brick and wood while allowing integral color for a decorative finish.

🖌️ Colored / Integral Color

Pigment is mixed into the concrete for consistent color throughout the slab and can be combined with stamping.

✨ Salt Finish

A subtle textured surface created by pressing rock salt into wet concrete and removing it after curing.

🔲 Smooth / Trowel Finish

A hard smooth finish commonly used for garage floors and interior slabs where an ultra-smooth surface is appropriate.

Frequently Asked Questions — Concrete Services in Cabin John, MD

How much does a concrete driveway cost in Cabin John, MD?

Typical installed pricing is approximately $8–$12 per sq ft for standard broom-finished concrete, $10–$15 for exposed aggregate, and $14–$22 for stamped decorative concrete. A 600 sq ft driveway may therefore range from roughly $5,000 to $13,200 depending on finish and site conditions.

Do I need a permit for concrete work in Cabin John, MD?

Permit requirements depend on the scope and site conditions. Structural concrete, certain retaining walls, grading/drainage changes and other regulated work can require permits. DMV General Contracting Group reviews the project requirements before work begins and coordinates required permitting.

Concrete or pavers — which is better?

Both can perform well. Concrete generally has a lower installed cost and provides a seamless surface, while pavers offer individual-unit repairability and a premium appearance. Stamped concrete can provide decorative patterns at a lower cost than many natural-stone applications.

How long does concrete take to cure?

Concrete continues gaining strength for weeks. Light foot traffic is commonly possible after 24–48 hours, while vehicle traffic is typically kept off for at least 7 days. Full design strength is generally evaluated at 28 days, with weather and project specifications affecting the schedule.
