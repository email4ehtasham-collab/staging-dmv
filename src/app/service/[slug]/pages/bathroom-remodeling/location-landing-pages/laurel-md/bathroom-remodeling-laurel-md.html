Laurel's Three-Jurisdiction Permit System — What Homeowners Need to Know

Laurel is one of the few communities in Maryland where a single city name covers properties in three separate permit jurisdictions. This creates real confusion for homeowners — and for contractors who don't know the area well. Filing a permit with the wrong jurisdiction causes costly delays and sometimes compliance problems that must be corrected at the homeowner's expense.

🗺️ Understanding Laurel's Permit Jurisdictions

  • City of Laurel — properties within the incorporated City of Laurel boundaries (roughly the historic downtown core and surrounding neighborhoods) require permits through Laurel's own Department of Public Works. Many Laurel residents with a "Laurel, MD" mailing address are actually outside the city limits.
  • Montgomery County — properties in the northwest portion of greater Laurel that fall within Montgomery County (communities like North Laurel and parts of Burtonsville) require permits through Montgomery County's Department of Permitting Services (DPS).
  • Prince George's County — properties in the eastern and southeastern portions of greater Laurel that fall within Prince George's County require permits through Prince George's County's Department of Permitting, Inspections and Enforcement (DPIE).
  • What determines your jurisdiction — your property's county and whether it's within the incorporated city limits. A "Laurel, MD 20707" or "Laurel, MD 20708" mailing address does not definitively tell you which jurisdiction applies — only the parcel map does.
  • DMV-GC Group verifies the correct permit jurisdiction for your specific Laurel address before filing anything — using parcel data and county boundary mapping, not assumptions based on zip code.
