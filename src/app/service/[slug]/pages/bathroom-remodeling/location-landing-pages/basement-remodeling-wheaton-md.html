A legal basement ADU in Wheaton requires specific components to receive a Montgomery County Certificate of Occupancy:

🏘️ Montgomery County Legal Basement ADU — Required Components

  • Certificate of Occupancy (COO): The document that legally permits the basement to be occupied as a dwelling unit — required for legal rental in Montgomery County. DMV-GC Group manages the COO application and all inspections as a standard part of every ADU project.
  • Egress window in every sleeping area: Minimum 5.7 sq ft opening, 24" height, 20" width, maximum 44" sill height. Required in any room designated as a bedroom. DMV-GC Group handles all concrete cutting and installation.
  • Separate entrance: A dedicated exterior door — either an existing side/rear door or a newly cut opening with steps and landing. Many Wheaton split-levels already have this.
  • Kitchen or kitchenette: Food preparation capability — at minimum a sink, counter space, and refrigerator. A kitchenette (countertop cooking) satisfies the requirement at lower cost than a full kitchen with range.
  • Full bathroom: Toilet, sink, and shower — licensed plumbing installation with Montgomery County inspection.
  • Fire-rated separation: The ceiling assembly between the ADU and main home must achieve a 1-hour fire rating — typically addressed through proper drywall specification on the ceiling and any shared walls.
  • Smoke and CO detectors: Interconnected in every room and near sleeping areas — verified by county inspector as part of the COO process.
  • 7-foot minimum ceiling height in all habitable rooms — Wheaton split-levels typically meet this without intervention.

Basement Moisture Assessment in Wheaton Homes

Wheaton's postwar housing stock — most built between 1950 and 1975 — includes basements constructed before modern moisture management practices were standard. The area's topography, with rolling terrain and clay-heavy soils common in central Montgomery County, means some Wheaton basements have more moisture exposure than equivalent homes in drier or sandier areas.

💧 What We Check Before Finishing Any Wheaton Basement

  • Foundation wall type: Most Wheaton homes from this era have poured concrete or concrete block foundations. Block foundations are more porous than poured concrete and more likely to show efflorescence or seepage at mortar joints.
  • Efflorescence and staining: White mineral deposits or water staining at the base of walls or along the floor-wall joint indicate historical moisture movement — needs to be addressed before finishing.
  • Sump pit and pump: Presence, age, cycling frequency, and battery backup status. Wheaton's clay-heavy soils can direct significant groundwater toward foundations during heavy rain events.
  • Window wells: Many Wheaton homes have below-grade basement windows with window wells. If well drains are clogged, window wells pond water during heavy rain and seep through window frames — a common and easily addressed moisture source.
  • Grading: Ground that has settled toward the foundation over decades is one of the most common correctable moisture sources in Wheaton's older homes. Regrading costs $800–$2,500 and prevents significantly more expensive moisture damage.
  • DMV-GC Group provides a written moisture assessment with findings and specific recommendations before any Wheaton basement renovation is designed or priced.
