Bathroom Remodeling for Chevy Chase's Historic Housing Stock

More than anything else, Chevy Chase's residential character is defined by its grand early-streetcar-suburb homes — large Colonial Revivals, Tudors, Queen Anne Victorians, and foursquares built mostly between the 1890s and 1930s, with mid-century and newer infill mixed in. These are substantial, high-end homes, but their bathrooms are frequently original, retrofitted, or modest relative to the house. Understanding how they were originally plumbed, wired, and framed — and how to renovate them sensitively at a high level of finish — is essential to a successful bathroom project, and it's where DMV-GC Group's experience makes a real difference.

🛁 What Makes Chevy Chase Bathrooms Different

  • Bathrooms retrofitted into older homes — many homes predate modern plumbing, so original baths are tucked into awkward or undersized spaces that benefit from a smart, high-end redesign
  • Few baths for the home's size — large early-1900s houses often have surprisingly few bathrooms, so adding an ensuite, powder room, or basement bath is a high-return upgrade
  • Aging plumbing & wiring — galvanized supply lines, cast-iron drain stacks, and outdated electrical are common in homes this age and often need updating during a remodel
  • Plaster walls & older subfloors — period construction calls for careful demolition and the right substrate prep so new tile and waterproofing last
  • High-end, period-sensitive finishes — Chevy Chase homeowners expect luxury results that modernize systems while honoring a home's classic character
  • A patchwork of jurisdictions — "Chevy Chase" spans several villages and towns, some (like Chevy Chase Village) with their own permit, architectural, and tree review we account for from the start
