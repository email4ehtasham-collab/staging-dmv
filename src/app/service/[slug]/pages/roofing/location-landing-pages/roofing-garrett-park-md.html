Garrett Park's Historic Homes — What We Find When We Inspect These Roofs

🏛️ Roofing Conditions Specific to Garrett Park's 1900s–1940s Housing Stock

Garrett Park's bungalows, craftsman foursquares, and colonial revival homes were built in the era before modern plywood sheathing, synthetic underlayments, and architectural shingles existed. When we inspect roofs on these homes, we commonly find conditions that require honest assessment before any replacement recommendation is made:

  • Original wood plank sheathing: Many Garrett Park homes have original 1×6 or 1×8 diagonal wood board sheathing beneath existing shingles rather than modern OSB or plywood. Wood plank sheathing is generally still sound after decades of service, but gaps between boards affect how new shingles are fastened and whether solid sheathing is needed before new installation.
  • Complex roofline geometry: Garrett Park's craftsman bungalows and foursquares frequently have multiple roof planes, dormers, eyebrow windows, and prominent chimneys — all of which create more flashing and transition points than a simple gable roof. Each transition is a potential leak source and requires individual attention during both inspection and replacement.
  • Century-old chimney conditions: Chimneys on Garrett Park's oldest homes often have deteriorated mortar joints, cracked chimney crowns, and failed or absent step and counter-flashing — making the chimney the most common source of roof leaks in these properties. Chimney assessment is a standard part of every Garrett Park roof inspection.
  • Original slate or early asphalt: Some Garrett Park homes still have original slate roofing — either fully intact, partially repaired, or overlaid with asphalt. Slate assessment requires evaluation of whether the remaining slate is repairable, whether replacement slates can be matched, and whether full replacement with modern materials is more practical.
  • Ventilation conditions: Garrett Park's older homes were built without modern attic ventilation systems. Inadequate ventilation accelerates shingle aging and can contribute to ice dam formation in winter. Ventilation assessment is included in every Garrett Park roof inspection.

Roof Repair vs. Roof Replacement in Garrett Park, MD

Roof Repair

Repair is appropriate for Garrett Park homes when damage is localized — a flashing failure at a chimney, a small section of damaged shingles after a storm, or a valley that needs resealing. Many Garrett Park roofs with otherwise sound shingles have chimney or flashing issues that can be corrected without full replacement.

Roof Replacement

Replacement is warranted when shingles are at or past their service life, when granule loss is widespread, when multiple leaks recur despite repairs, or when the underlying sheathing condition warrants full access. For Garrett Park's oldest homes, a roof that has not been replaced in 25+ years warrants honest assessment.

Professional Inspection First

For Garrett Park's historic homes, an inspection is particularly important before recommending repair versus replacement — the complexity of these roofs means that a single apparent leak may have multiple contributing sources that must all be identified to be effectively addressed.
