Why Wheaton Carpet Projects Need Split-Level Expertise

🏘️ The Wheaton Split-Level Carpet Case — What Most Contractors Get Wrong

  • Split-levels have two or three separate stair runs — not one: A typical Wheaton split-level connects four levels — entry, main living, upper bedrooms, and lower family room or basement — with two or three separate stair runs at different locations in the home. Each run is a distinct carpeting job: different step counts, different directions, sometimes different widths. A contractor who prices "stair carpeting" as a single line item on a Wheaton split-level without measuring each run independently is working from an incomplete estimate. DMV General Contracting Group measures every stair run individually, orders all runs from the same dye lot to ensure color consistency across the home, and installs from top level down to eliminate foot traffic over newly installed carpet.
  • Dye lot coordination across multiple rooms and stair runs matters: A whole-home carpet replacement on a Wheaton split-level involves carpet in four to six bedrooms, two or three stair runs, possibly a finished basement, and a lower-level family room — all of which must come from the same dye lot to read consistently under Wheaton's typical lighting conditions. Ordering rooms and stair runs from different production runs produces visible color inconsistency that becomes apparent within months as the carpet settles. We order all material for the full home before installation begins.
  • Finished basements need moisture-barrier pad — always: Wheaton's finished basements sit on below-grade concrete slabs that wick moisture from the ground regardless of how dry they appear. Standard carpet padding directly on a below-grade slab creates conditions for mold growth within 2–5 years in Maryland's humid climate — even without visible water intrusion. Moisture-barrier padding is standard practice on every Wheaton basement carpet project we install, not an optional upgrade. Homeowners who have had basement carpet go musty or moldy without obvious flooding almost always had standard padding installed on a below-grade slab.
  • Transitions and landings must be planned before installation begins: At each level of a Wheaton split-level, carpet transitions at landings, thresholds, and adjoining hard-surface floors must be planned and installed correctly for the finished result to look intentional. Transition strips between carpet and hardwood, tile, or LVP at landing entries; carpet wrap at stair nosings; and seam placement that avoids high-traffic areas at the bottom of stair runs — all of these details are planned at the estimate and executed during installation, not improvised on the job site.

Basement Carpet in Wheaton — Why Moisture-Barrier Pad Is Non-Negotiable

💧 Wheaton's finished basements sit on below-grade concrete slabs — a substrate that transmits moisture upward from the ground year-round, regardless of how dry the basement appears. Standard carpet padding directly on a below-grade concrete slab creates the conditions for mold growth in the padding within 2–5 years in Maryland's climate, even without any visible water intrusion or flooding. The symptoms are a musty basement smell, visible mold at the carpet edges, or carpet that begins to feel damp. DMV General Contracting Group installs moisture-barrier padding on every Wheaton basement carpet project — a product specifically engineered with a sealed, moisture-blocking bottom layer that prevents ground moisture transmission from the slab while providing the cushion and insulation of standard padding. If you have previously had basement carpet go musty without obvious flooding, standard padding on a below-grade slab is almost certainly the cause. Moisture-barrier pad is standard on every DMV-GC basement project in Wheaton — it is not an add-on or an upgrade conversation at installation.

Signs Your Wheaton Home Needs New Carpet

🔍

Matting at Stair Nosings

Carpet crushed flat at the edge of each stair step — the highest-wear point on a split-level home's stair run.

🎨

Fading or Color Inconsistency

Visible color fade near windows or along traffic paths — surface-dyed fibers losing color where original solution-dyed nylon would not.

🤧

Persistent Odor

Musty or pet odor that cleaning cannot eliminate — embedded in the pad or subfloor beneath the carpet surface.

🌊

Basement Dampness or Mold

Musty basement smell or mold at carpet edges — standard pad on concrete slab transmitting ground moisture upward.

🧹

Rippling or Buckling

Waves or loose areas in bedroom or stair carpet — the carpet has stretched beyond proper tension and needs re-stretching or replacement.

🪡

Fraying at Transitions

Carpet edges unraveling at doorways, stair nosings, or transition strips — structural failure that cannot be resolved by cleaning.

Free In-Home Estimate — Every Level and Stair Run Measured

We visit your Wheaton home and measure every room, every stair run, and the finished basement or lower-level family room individually. We assess subfloor conditions in each area, note the transition details at each landing and room threshold, identify whether moisture-barrier pad is needed (it always is in below-grade spaces), and confirm dye lot ordering requirements for the full home. You receive a room-by-room, stair-by-stair itemized estimate — not a single whole-home lump sum. No charge, no obligation.

2

Fiber & Color Selection — Whole-Home Dye Lot Coordinated

We bring samples of solution-dyed nylon, SmartStrand triexta, wool, and polyester options to your Wheaton home — showing colors in your actual lighting conditions on each level. We discuss fiber recommendations by room: nylon or triexta for high-traffic bedrooms and stair runs, wool for primary suites where feel matters most, and the appropriate fiber for your finished basement situation. All carpet for the full project is ordered from the same production run before installation begins to ensure dye lot consistency across levels.

3

Old Carpet Removal — All Levels Sequenced

Existing carpet and pad removed from all areas in the project scope — bedrooms, stair runs, basement, family room — and disposed of. Subfloor inspected at each level after removal: staples and tack strip nails pulled, subfloor surface assessed for squeaks, soft spots, and moisture damage, and minor repairs made before new pad is installed. For Wheaton split-levels doing a whole-home replacement, we sequence the removal and installation level by level to keep the home livable throughout the project.

4

Pad Installation — Moisture-Barrier Below Grade

Premium pad installed in all above-grade rooms. Moisture-barrier pad installed in all below-grade spaces — finished basement and any lower-level rooms on or below the concrete slab. Tack strips installed around all room perimeters and stair runs. New tack strips used on every project — we do not reuse old tack strips, which hold carpet tension poorly after the original carpet is removed.

5

Carpet Installation — Top Level Down, Stair Runs Last

Carpet installed from the top bedroom level downward — bedrooms first, then stair runs from top to bottom, then lower-level rooms and basement last. This sequence means newly installed carpet is never walked over during subsequent installation. Stair carpet installed with waterfall or wrapped method depending on stair profile and homeowner preference. Seams placed away from high-traffic paths. All transitions and threshold strips installed at landings and room entries.

6

Final Stretch, Trim & Walkthrough

All carpet power-stretched to proper tension — eliminating the rippling and buckling that develop when carpet is hand-stretched or knee-kicked only. All edges trimmed at baseboards. Transition strips secured at all hard-surface boundaries. Complete walkthrough on every level with you before project close — checking every seam, every stair nosing, every transition. Written warranty documentation provided.
