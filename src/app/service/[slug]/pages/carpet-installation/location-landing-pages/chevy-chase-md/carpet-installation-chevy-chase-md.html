Signs Your Chevy Chase Home Needs New Carpet

🔍

Matting at High-Traffic Areas

Permanently crushed pile at doorways, stair nosings, and hallway paths — fiber resilience lost beyond recovery by cleaning.

🎨

Fading in Traffic Lanes

Visibly lighter paths through rooms along daily routes — surface-dyed fiber losing color where solution-dyed would not.

🤧

Persistent Odor

Embedded musty or pet odor that professional cleaning does not resolve — deep in the pad below the surface.

🌊

Basement Dampness

Musty smell at basement carpet edges — standard pad on below-grade concrete transmitting ground moisture upward.

🧹

Rippling or Buckling

Waves or slack areas across bedroom or hallway carpet — stretched beyond tension and no longer lying flat.

🪡

Fraying at Stair Nosings

Unraveling carpet at stair step edges — structural fiber failure at the highest-wear point on a formal colonial staircase.

Premium carpet padding in all above-grade rooms. Moisture-barrier padding in any below-grade spaces — finished basements and lower-level rooms on concrete slab. On Chevy Chase's prewar construction, tack strip sizing and placement may differ from modern plywood subfloor specifications.

5

Carpet Installation — Formal Staircase Last, Trim Protected Throughout

Carpet installed in all bedroom and above-grade rooms first. Waterfall or wrapped installation depending on nosing profile and homeowner preference. All transitions, threshold strips, and landing details installed and secured.

6

Final Stretch, Trim & Walkthrough

All carpet power-stretched to proper tension. All edges trimmed cleanly at baseboards. Transition strips secured at all hard-surface boundaries.
