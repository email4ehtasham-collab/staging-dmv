🏠 The Garrett Park Historic Home Carpet Case — What Every Homeowner Should Know First

  • Original hardwood is likely under old carpet — and may be worth refinishing: Garrett Park's 1900s–1940s bungalows and colonial revivals were built when oak, fir, and wide-plank hardwood were standard floor material — wall-to-wall carpet came later, typically as an overlay installed in the 1950s through 1980s over original flooring that was in place from the beginning. When we remove old carpet from a Garrett Park bungalow, we assess the original flooring underneath: its species, width, condition, and refinishability. Some Garrett Park homeowners discover flooring in excellent refinishable condition that they would not have chosen to cover again had they known it was there. We bring that information to the homeowner honestly — even when it means our carpet project becomes a flooring refinishing referral instead.
  • Craftsman stair trim is not interchangeable with modern stair construction: Garrett Park's bungalow-era staircases carry original stringer profiles, hand-cut newel posts, and period baluster proportions that were designed for the architectural style of the home. Tack strip fasteners placed incorrectly on these staircases — at depths and angles calibrated for modern plywood stair construction — can split original stringer material or damage trim profiles that cannot be exactly replicated. DMV General Contracting Group uses stair installation methods specific to Garrett Park's bungalow-era construction: shallower tack strip placement, hand-fastening at critical trim locations, and a test-fit section on any stair where the woodwork's condition is uncertain before the full run is committed.
  • No historic review applies to interior carpet: The Town of Garrett Park's historic preservation framework — and the National Register Historic District designation itself — governs exterior modifications: siding, windows, additions, and changes visible from the public street. Carpet material, color, fiber, and style inside your Garrett Park home are entirely your choice with no Town review, no submission process, and no approval timeline. A Garrett Park homeowner in a National Register Historic District property can select any carpet color, any fiber type, and install it on their own schedule without any interaction with the Town of Garrett Park or any historic preservation authority.
  • Period-appropriate carpet choices complement Garrett Park's bungalow character: While no review governs interior carpet choices, many Garrett Park homeowners want carpet that feels appropriate to a 1930s Craftsman bungalow. Wool in warm earth tones — mushroom, oatmeal, sage, terracotta — reads naturally against Garrett Park bungalow woodwork and period trim. Solution-dyed nylon and triexta are available in the same color families for homeowners who want the period-appropriate color palette with better stain resistance. Signs Your Garrett Park Bungalow Needs New Carpet

🔍

Matting at High-Traffic Areas

Permanently crushed carpet at doorways, stair nosings, and hallway paths — fiber has lost its resilience beyond recovery.

🎨

Color Loss in Traffic Lanes

Visible lighter paths through rooms where foot traffic has faded surface-dyed fiber — uneven color that cleaning cannot restore.

🤧

Persistent Odor

Musty or embedded pet odor that cleaning does not resolve — deep in the pad or original subfloor beneath the carpet.

🧹

Rippling or Buckling

Waves or loose areas across bedroom or stair carpet — stretched beyond proper tension and no longer lying flat.

🪡

Fraying at Stair Nosings

Unraveling carpet edges at stair steps — structural fiber failure at the highest-wear point in a Garrett Park bungalow.

🏚️

Dated Color or Style

Carpet color or texture that no longer suits the home's character — a straightforward update with no historic review required.
