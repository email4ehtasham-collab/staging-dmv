Protecting Original Hardwood — Kensington's Most Common Carpet Question

🪵 Carpet over original hardwood in a Kensington Historic District home: Standard carpet installation uses tackless strips nailed or stapled around the room perimeter. Driven into a plywood subfloor, this is routine. Driven directly into original hardwood, it leaves small holes that complicate a future refinish. Before any Historic District installation, we identify the nailing surface in each room and, where original hardwood is the surface being fastened into, use lower-penetration tack strip alternatives positioned along existing fastener lines where possible — preserving the floor's condition and your future option to expose and refinish it. This single step is the biggest difference between a generic carpet installer and one experienced with Kensington's older housing stock.

Frequently Asked Questions — Carpet Installation in Kensington, MD

Does Kensington's Historic District require approval for interior carpet installation?
No. The Kensington Historic District, listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1980 and included in Montgomery County's Master Plan for Historic Preservation, requires county Historic Preservation Commission review for exterior changes to a home — additions, roofing, window replacement, and similar visible modifications. Interior finishes, including carpet material, color, and installation, are not subject to that review. The one thing worth doing before installation in a Historic District home is confirming whether original hardwood floors exist beneath any old carpet, since many Kensington Victorians retain original wood flooring that's worth protecting or preserving during the project. DMV General Contracting Group checks for this at every Historic District estimate.
How do you install carpet without damaging original hardwood floors in a Kensington Victorian?
Many homes in Kensington's Historic District — Victorian, Craftsman bungalow, Tudor, and Colonial Revival styles largely dating to the 1890s through the 1930s — have original hardwood flooring beneath carpet that was added at some point over the past century. Standard tackless-strip installation drives small nails or staples into the subfloor and, if driven through a hardwood floor rather than plywood subfloor, can leave permanent holes. Before installing, we identify whether the room has a plywood subfloor over the hardwood or the hardwood itself as the nailing surface, and where the hardwood is exposed, we use low-profile or adhesive-based tack strip alternatives that minimize penetration, positioned to land in the same fastener lines as any prior installation where possible. This preserves the option to refinish and expose the original hardwood later, which matters for resale value in Kensington's historic housing stock.
What's different about carpet installation in Rock Creek Woods versus Kensington's Historic District?
Rock Creek Woods is a distinct enclave of 74 Contemporary-style homes designed by architect Charles Goodman and built between 1958 and 1961, listed as its own historic district for its intact mid-century modern layout and architecture. These homes were designed around open floor plans and were typically built with concrete slab or simple plywood subfloors rather than the older joist-and-hardwood construction common in Kensington's 1890s-1930s Historic District homes. That means subfloor inspection in Rock Creek Woods is usually a simpler check for slab moisture and flatness, while Historic District homes require the extra step of checking for original hardwood before any tackless strip goes down. DMV General Contracting Group treats these as genuinely different projects rather than a one-size-fits-all installation.
