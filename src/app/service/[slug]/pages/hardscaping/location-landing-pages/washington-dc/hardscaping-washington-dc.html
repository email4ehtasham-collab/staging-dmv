HPRB — Historic Preservation Guidance for DC Hardscaping

🏛️ When Does DC Hardscaping Require HPRB Review?

Washington DC's Historic Preservation Review Board governs exterior modifications in DC's historic districts — Capitol Hill, Georgetown, Dupont Circle, Logan Circle, Kalorama, and others. For hardscaping specifically: rear garden hardscaping not visible from the street or alley generally does not require HPRB review and proceeds through standard DC DOB permitting. Features visible from the street or alley — front courtyard paving, retaining walls visible from the street, driveway additions, and rooftop elements visible from public ways — may require HPRB review or a Certificate of Appropriateness. Period-appropriate materials (brick, natural stone) are HPRB-compatible for most DC historic district hardscaping; large-format contemporary porcelain, stamped concrete, and decorative concrete block are generally not. DMV General Contracting Group discusses HPRB applicability with every DC historic district homeowner at the consultation — before any permit is filed, so there are no surprises in the DC DOB review process.
