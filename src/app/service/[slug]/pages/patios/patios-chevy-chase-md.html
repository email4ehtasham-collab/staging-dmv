Chevy Chase's Six Permit Jurisdictions — The Most Important Thing to Confirm Before Any Patio Work Begins

Two adjacent Chevy Chase homes with identical mailing addresses may file patio permits at different offices depending on which village boundary their parcels fall within. A contractor who files by neighborhood name or zip code rather than parcel location will get it wrong for a meaningful percentage of Chevy Chase properties — leaving homeowners with unpermitted work and the resale and insurance documentation gaps that creates. DMV General Contracting Group confirms the correct permit authority by parcel at every Chevy Chase consultation.

🏛️ Village of Chevy Chase

Oldest and largest Chevy Chase village. Own municipal permit office. All structural patio permits filed here for Village of Chevy Chase properties — not MoCo DPS.

🏛️ Chevy Chase Section 3

Separate incorporated village with its own permit office. Distinct from the Village of Chevy Chase — filing at the Village office for a Section 3 property will not result in a permit.

🏛️ Chevy Chase Section 5

Another independent incorporated section with its own permit authority. Separate from both the Village and Section 3 — each section is its own jurisdiction.

🏛️ Village of Somerset

Small incorporated village within the Chevy Chase area. Own permit authority for all construction including structural patio elements.

🏛️ Village of Friendship Heights

Incorporated village bordering DC. Own permit office for patio and construction work. Not the same as the DC neighborhood of the same name.

🏛️ Unincorporated MoCo

Some Chevy Chase-area properties fall outside all village limits. These file structural patio permits with MoCo DPS at 2425 Reedie Drive, Wheaton.

Simple ground-level patios: A basic ground-level patio without structural elements typically does not require a permit in any Chevy Chase jurisdiction. Structural elements — pergolas, outdoor kitchens with gas or electrical connections, retaining walls over a certain height, raised patio footings — require permits filed at the correct village or county office. Frequently Asked Questions — Patio Installation in Chevy Chase, MD

How many permit jurisdictions cover patio work in Chevy Chase, MD?

Six. Village of Chevy Chase, Chevy Chase Section 3, Chevy Chase Section 5, Village of Somerset, Village of Friendship Heights, and unincorporated Montgomery County each have separate permit offices. The correct office is confirmed by parcel location — not by mailing address, neighborhood name, or zip code. DMV General Contracting Group confirms the applicable jurisdiction at every Chevy Chase consultation.

What patio material is most appropriate for a Chevy Chase colonial or tudor?

Natural bluestone is the first choice for most Chevy Chase colonials and tudor revivals — the cool gray tone and natural texture complement both architectural profiles and age gracefully in Chevy Chase's established garden settings. Brick is an excellent alternative for homes with brick exterior details — reclaimed brick in a traditional pattern is the most period-authentic option. Modern large-format porcelain and stamped concrete are not recommended for Chevy Chase's estate architectural context.

My Chevy Chase lot has very large mature trees — can a patio still be designed around them?

Yes — Chevy Chase's century-old oaks and maples are often the most important features on the property, and patio design should work with them rather than against them. DMV General Contracting Group assesses mature tree root zones before any patio layout is finalized, adjusts the design to maintain safe setbacks from significant roots, and specifies permeable paving base systems where coverage within a root zone is unavoidable and accepted by the homeowner.

Do I need a permit for a simple ground-level bluestone patio in Chevy Chase?

A basic ground-level patio without structural elements — no pergola, no outdoor kitchen with gas or electrical, no retaining walls above a certain height — typically does not require a permit in any Chevy Chase jurisdiction. Adding structural elements to the patio scope does require the applicable village permit.
