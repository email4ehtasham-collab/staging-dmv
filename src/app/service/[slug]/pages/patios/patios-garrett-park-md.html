Garrett Park's Historic District — What It Means for Patio Design

🏛️ National Register Historic District — Patio Design Considerations

Garrett Park's listing on the National Register of Historic Places reflects the community's remarkably intact collection of early 20th century residential architecture. While patio installation in rear yards and not visible from the street generally does not trigger formal historic review, the community's character calls for design and material choices that respect — rather than contrast with — its historic setting:

  • Period-appropriate materials are the right starting point: Natural stone, brick, and wood have been part of Garrett Park's landscape for over a century. These materials age well, complement mature plantings, and read as belonging in a historic district. Modern large-format concrete slabs, contemporary porcelain tiles, and stamped concrete in bright or unusual colors do not.
  • Structures visible from the street warrant additional care: Front yard features, street-visible pergola structures, and prominently placed outdoor elements should be designed with the community's historic streetscape in mind — not just the individual home. Natural wood pergolas and stone or brick features read as consistent with Garrett Park's character; aluminum and vinyl structures generally do not.
  • Mature trees and root zones: Garrett Park's canopy — one of the community's defining characteristics — means mature tree root zones are present on most lots. Patio placement and excavation depth are assessed with root proximity in mind before any scope is finalized.
  • Town of Garrett Park permit at 4600 Oxford Street: All structural patio elements require Town of Garrett Park permits — not MoCo DPS. DMV General Contracting Group files at the correct office as standard practice for every Garrett Park project.

Period-Appropriate Patio Materials for Garrett Park

Garrett Park's historic district character is best served by materials that have always been part of American residential landscape design — stone, brick, and wood. These materials complement the community's 1900s–1940s homes, age gracefully alongside mature plantings, and belong in a National Register Historic District in a way that contemporary materials simply do not.

🪨 Natural Flagstone ✅ Most Recommended

Irregular Pennsylvania flagstone, bluestone, or quartzite set in a wide-joint sand or mortar bed — the most organically appropriate patio material for Garrett Park's garden-oriented lots. Natural variation in color and texture integrates seamlessly with established plantings and mature canopy. Wide joints planted with creeping thyme or moss reinforce the naturalistic character.

🪨 Pennsylvania Bluestone ✅ Recommended

Uniform-cut or irregular bluestone — cool gray tone and natural texture that complement both craftsman and colonial revival architectural profiles. More formal than irregular flagstone; appropriate for homes with more structured garden layouts or for rear terraces adjacent to the home's primary door.

🧱 Reclaimed or New Brick ✅ Recommended

Running bond, herringbone, or basket weave patterns in reclaimed or new brick — deeply embedded in early American residential character and architecturally compatible with Garrett Park's homes. Reclaimed brick from a comparable era delivers the most authentic character. Warm tone, durable, repairable.

🪨 Fieldstone ✅ Naturalistic Settings

Local fieldstone in dry-laid or mortared settings — the most naturalistic and least formal option for Garrett Park's garden settings. Well suited to homes with naturalistic landscape designs and lots where an organic, settled character is the goal.

🧱 Concrete Pavers ⚠️ Period Profile Only

If budget requires concrete pavers, only earth-tone tumbled or aged-profile units in smaller sizes read as period-compatible in Garrett Park. Large-format modern pavers in contemporary colors are not appropriate. Even with the right pavers, natural stone and brick are the preferred choices for the historic district context.

🏗️ Stamped Concrete / Porcelain ❌ Not Recommended

Modern stamped concrete patterns and large-format porcelain pavers are not compatible with Garrett Park's National Register Historic District character. These materials read as contemporary additions that contrast with — rather than complement — the community's historic architectural setting.

Does Garrett Park's historic district status affect my patio design choices?

It should — even where formal historic review is not required. Garrett Park's National Register Historic District character is best served by period-appropriate materials: natural stone, brick, and wood. Modern large-format porcelain, stamped concrete in contemporary patterns, and aluminum or vinyl structures contrast with — rather than complement — the community's historic character. What patio material is most appropriate for a Garrett Park craftsman bungalow?

Irregular natural flagstone in a wide-joint sand or mortar setting is the most period-appropriate and visually compatible choice — it integrates with the bungalow's craftsman character and existing garden plantings as if it has always been there. Brick in running bond or herringbone is an excellent alternative, particularly for homes with brick exterior details. My Garrett Park lot has large mature trees — will that affect patio installation?

Yes — root zone proximity is assessed at every Garrett Park patio consultation. Mature oak, maple, and elm root zones extend well beyond the drip line and can be damaged by excavation within that zone. DMV General Contracting Group adjusts the patio layout to maintain safe setbacks from significant roots where possible, and specifies permeable paving base systems where patio coverage within a root zone is unavoidable and the homeowner accepts the root proximity.
