R-200 Zoning & Permitting for Potomac Home Additions

📋 Potomac Permit Process — What Every Addition Requires

  • R-200 zoning setback review — first step before any design: Potomac is predominantly zoned R-200 — requiring minimum lot size of approximately 2 acres, front setback of 50 feet, side setbacks of 25 feet per side, and rear setback of 35 feet. These setbacks define the maximum buildable envelope within which the addition must fit. On most Potomac estate lots with ample rear and side depth, R-200 setbacks rarely constrain addition size — but the review is required before design begins to confirm the buildable area and any FAR (Floor Area Ratio) limits. DMV General Contracting Group performs this review at the free consultation.
  • HOA Architectural Review Committee (ARC) — required before MoCo DPS permit in many Potomac communities: Many Potomac neighborhoods — planned communities, subdivisions, and estates with recorded covenants — require HOA ARC approval for any exterior modification including additions, before the Montgomery County DPS permit can be filed. The ARC submission typically includes architectural drawings, exterior material specifications, color samples, and a site plan showing the addition footprint relative to the property. DMV General Contracting Group prepares and manages the full HOA ARC submission in-house, including attending ARC meetings where required. We sequence the HOA submission to complete before MoCo DPS filing so approval of one does not delay the other.
  • Montgomery County DPS building permit — filed at 2425 Reedie Drive, Wheaton: All Potomac building permits — structural, electrical, plumbing, HVAC — are filed with Montgomery County DPS. Potomac is unincorporated Montgomery County with no separate municipal permit office, unlike Kensington or Garrett Park. MoCo DPS addition permit review typically takes 8–12 weeks for Potomac estate-scale projects. DMV General Contracting Group's in-house permit team prepares the complete submission package — architectural drawings, structural calculations, trade permit applications — and manages all county correspondence through approval.
  • Structural engineering — required for all load-bearing additions: All Potomac additions involving load-bearing wall removal, second-story additions, or large room additions require stamped structural engineering documents as part of the MoCo DPS permit package. DMV General Contracting Group's in-house structural engineers perform all load calculations, specify all beams and connections, and provide stamped drawings — no outside engineering firm needed or billed separately.
  • Trade permits — electrical, plumbing, HVAC: Each trade permit (electrical, plumbing, HVAC) is pulled separately from MoCo DPS and requires its own rough-in inspection before walls close. DMV General Contracting Group's in-house licensed electricians, plumbers, and HVAC technicians pull and manage their own trade permits, coordinate inspections, and ensure no rough-in work is closed without the required county sign-off.
