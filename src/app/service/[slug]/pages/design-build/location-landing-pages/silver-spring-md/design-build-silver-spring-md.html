Silver Spring's Split-Level Homes — Design-Build Challenges & Opportunities

Silver Spring's 1950s–1970s split-level housing stock — one of the most prevalent home types in the community — creates a specific set of structural and design conditions that affect every major renovation and addition project. Our in-house architects and structural engineers address these before design is finalized:

⚠️ What Split-Levels Create — Without In-House Expertise

  • Compartmentalized floor plans that feel small despite adequate square footage
  • Load-bearing walls at unexpected locations — discovered by GCs mid-demolition
  • Multi-level floor plates that complicate addition design and structural coordination
  • Below-grade lower levels that need LVP, not hardwood — missed in design
  • Heating and cooling systems sized for original layout — addition requires HVAC redesign
  • Electrical service at 100-amp that cannot support modern kitchen additions
  • Permit applications to MoCo DPS filed without structural drawings — rejected

✅ What In-House Design-Build Delivers for Silver Spring Split-Levels

  • Open-concept conversions designed with structural engineering confirming beam spans before demo
  • Additions designed around the multi-level floor plate — not against it
  • Below-grade zones specified in LVP; above-grade in hardwood — correct from the start
  • HVAC extension sized and designed as part of the addition scope, not added later
  • Electrical panel upgrade absorbed into renovation scope when walls are already open
  • MoCo DPS structural plan review package produced by the same engineers who designed the work
  • One contract, one budget, one project manager — no surprises, no blame-shifting
