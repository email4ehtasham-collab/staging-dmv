Gaithersburg's Two Permit Jurisdictions — What Every Contractor Must Know

Gaithersburg's permit landscape is one of the most commonly misunderstood in the I-270 corridor. The incorporated City of Gaithersburg and unincorporated Montgomery County coexist in the same zip code area, with different permit authorities governing different neighborhoods:

🏛️ City of Gaithersburg

City DPW Permit Office
  • Neighborhoods: Kentlands, Lakelands, historic downtown Gaithersburg, Maryland City areas within incorporated limits
  • Permit authority: City of Gaithersburg Department of Public Works
  • What this means: All building permits for additions, structural work, ADUs, and significant renovations in Kentlands and Lakelands must be filed with the City — not MoCo DPS
  • Additional layer: Kentlands ARC (Architectural Review Committee) reviews exterior modifications — additions, siding changes, new structures. Interior renovations are not reviewed by ARC
  • DMV-GC approach: File with City DPW; coordinate ARC submission for exterior addition scope where required

🏛️ Montgomery County DPS

MoCo DPS — 2425 Reedie Drive, Wheaton
  • Neighborhoods: Crown Farm, Washingtonian Woods, Montgomery Village, Quince Orchard, North Potomac areas with Gaithersburg addresses
  • Permit authority: Montgomery County DPS at 2425 Reedie Drive, Wheaton
  • What this means: Crown Farm and Washingtonian Woods permits go to MoCo DPS — not the City — despite sharing a Gaithersburg address
  • Additional layer: Crown Farm HOA ARC reviews exterior modifications on Crown Farm properties. MoCo zoning governs setback and lot coverage
  • DMV-GC approach: Confirm parcel jurisdiction at consultation; file with MoCo DPS; coordinate Crown Farm HOA ARC submission for exterior scope
📋 The Kentlands-Crown Farm boundary: The boundary between City of Gaithersburg and unincorporated MoCo runs through the area between Kentlands and Crown Farm — some streets near this boundary require parcel-level address lookup to confirm the correct permit authority. DMV-GC Group confirms jurisdiction at the consultation by checking the specific parcel address before any design work begins. This prevents the permit rejection and resubmission delay that occurs when a contractor assumes jurisdiction without confirming it.
