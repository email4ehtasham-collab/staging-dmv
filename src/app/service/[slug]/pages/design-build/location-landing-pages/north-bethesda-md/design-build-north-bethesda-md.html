North Bethesda's Two Housing Types — Why the Design-Build Approach Differs Completely

North Bethesda's housing stock splits sharply between two construction types that require fundamentally different design-build expertise. Getting this distinction wrong produces design errors that surface during construction — not before it:

Pike & Rose · White Flint · Galvan Condos

Concrete Slab Construction
  • Flooring: LVP or engineered hardwood over underlayment only — solid hardwood cannot be nailed or glued to concrete slab without failure risk
  • Plumbing: Cannot be relocated below the slab without core drilling — layout changes require above-slab routing through walls and ceilings
  • Electrical: Modern panels and wiring standard — no old-home electrical surprises
  • Condo board: Renovation scope and timing often require condo board or HOA approval before permit is filed
  • Permit: MoCo DPS at 2425 Reedie Drive, Wheaton — plus condo association review process
  • Opportunity: Kitchen gut, bathroom expansion, open-concept conversion, flooring replacement — all achievable within slab constraints

Wildwood · Luxmanor · Tilden Woods Single-Family

Plywood Subfloor Construction
  • Flooring: Solid hardwood, engineered hardwood, LVP — all eligible above grade; LVP only below grade
  • Structural additions: Rear additions, second-story additions, and ADU construction feasible on lot sizes typically 8,000–15,000 sq ft
  • Construction era: Mix of 1950s–1980s colonials and split-levels (old-home assessment needed) and 1990s–2000s newer colonials (first-generation renovation opportunity)
  • Permit: MoCo DPS at 2425 Reedie Drive, Wheaton — same as condos, but addition and ADU scope requires structural engineering drawings
  • Old-home conditions: Older Wildwood homes may have 100A panels, galvanized plumbing, or load-bearing walls at non-obvious locations — all assessed at consultation
  • Opportunity: Full design-build renovation spectrum — additions, ADUs, open-concept, whole-home gut, basement finishing

A contractor who does not know that Wildwood single-family homes have plywood subfloors will underestimate the addition potential or miss the load-bearing wall conditions that affect open-concept conversion design. Can Pike & Rose and White Flint condos get hardwood flooring?
No — not solid hardwood. Pike & Rose, White Flint Station, and similar North Bethesda condo buildings are built on concrete slab. Solid hardwood flooring requires nailing or gluing to a wood subfloor — neither installation method works correctly on concrete slab, and both fail within a few years. The correct flooring options for slab-construction condos are LVP (luxury vinyl plank) or engineered hardwood with an appropriate underlayment. Both can achieve the look and feel homeowners want from hardwood without the installation failures that solid hardwood on slab produces. DMV-GC Group specifies this correctly from the first consultation — not after flooring has been installed and is already failing.
How does the condo board approval process work alongside the MoCo permit?
Many North Bethesda condo associations require homeowner approval of renovation scope before or alongside the MoCo DPS permit process. This approval governs working hours, elevator use for material delivery, debris removal procedures, noise-generating work restrictions, and in some buildings, specific finish material requirements. DMV-GC Group prepares the condo board renovation application — scope description, schedule, material specs, noise mitigation plan — and submits it to the association in parallel with MoCo DPS permit preparation, so both move simultaneously rather than sequentially.
