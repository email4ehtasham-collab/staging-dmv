Rockville's Permit Jurisdictions — City of Rockville vs MoCo DPS

📋 Two Permit Authorities — Which One Governs Your Rockville Address?

  • City of Rockville — 111 Maryland Avenue: The City of Rockville is an incorporated municipality that issues its own building permits at 111 Maryland Avenue, Rockville, MD 20850. Properties within the city limits — including King Farm, Fallsgrove, Rockville Town Center, and most of the established Rockville neighborhoods along Veirs Mill Road and Twinbrook Parkway — file with the City for all building permits: additions, ADU construction, structural modifications, significant kitchen and bathroom renovations, electrical and plumbing work that crosses the permit threshold. This is the correct authority for the large majority of Rockville homeowners.
  • MoCo DPS — 2425 Reedie Drive, Wheaton: Properties in unincorporated Montgomery County with Rockville mailing addresses file with MoCo DPS, not the City. The Congressional area northeast of the city core includes parcels that fall outside incorporated Rockville limits. Some addresses near the city boundary are not identifiable from the street address alone — parcel-level lookup is the only reliable confirmation. DMV-GC Group performs that confirmation for every Rockville project before any permit-required scope begins.
  • Why this matters for your design-build project: Filing with the wrong authority — City when it should be MoCo, or MoCo when it should be City — results in rejection, resubmission, and weeks of delay on a project timeline. On an addition or ADU project with a 6–8 week permit review period, that error compresses an entire construction season. We confirm jurisdiction before filing, not after rejection.
  • Both authorities served correctly: DMV-GC Group's in-house permit team files with the City of Rockville permit office at 111 Maryland Avenue for city-limit projects and with MoCo DPS at 2425 Reedie Drive for unincorporated county parcels. Homeowners never contact either permit office during their project — we manage all correspondence, review responses, and inspection scheduling from initial filing through permit close.

Rockville's Two Construction Eras — Different Projects, Different Expertise

Rockville's housing stock spans more than six decades of construction, and the design-build approach differs meaningfully between the two dominant eras:

King Farm, Fallsgrove & Newer Rockville

Built 1999–2010
  • Standard plywood subfloors throughout — no old-home surprises for tile or hardwood installation
  • 200-amp electrical panels — sufficient for modern kitchen appliances and EV chargers
  • Copper supply plumbing — no galvanized replacement needed
  • 20–25-year-old builder finishes — first major renovation opportunity: kitchens, bathrooms, basement
  • Colonial floor plans with formal dining/living rooms — open-concept conversion most requested scope

Built 1950s–1970s

  • Diagonal plank subfloors in oldest homes — uncoupling membrane required under tile
  • Significant addition and open-concept potential — structural engineer walk required first
  • City of Rockville or MoCo DPS depending on address — Congressional area requires parcel confirmation
  • Strong renovation ROI — postwar Rockville homes are priced below King Farm equivalents
