Wheaton's Mid-Century Electrical Conditions — What We Find at the Free Estimate

Wheaton's housing stock is concentrated in the 1950s–1970s construction window that produced the most common residential electrical issues in Montgomery County. Here is what our electricians assess at every Wheaton free estimate:

🚨 Federal Pacific Stab-Lok Panels — Heavily Concentrated in Wheaton's 1960s–1970s Construction

Federal Pacific Electric was among the most commonly specified panel manufacturers for Montgomery County residential construction in the 1960s and 1970s — and Wheaton's concentration of homes from this exact era means Federal Pacific panels appear frequently throughout the community. The Stab-Lok breaker design has been identified as a fire hazard: breakers fail to trip during overloads at a rate that significantly exceeds acceptable safety standards, allowing circuits to carry current past safe amperage without the protection the breaker is supposed to provide. A Wheaton home with a Federal Pacific panel may have had it in place for 50+ years without an obvious incident — the failure mode is not that the panel fails constantly, but that it fails to protect when protection is needed. Maryland insurance carriers have become significantly more aggressive about Federal Pacific panels in recent years, with many requiring replacement documentation before policy renewal. DMV General Contracting Group identifies Federal Pacific panels at every Wheaton free estimate and replaces them with full 200A upgrades — permitting through MoCo DPS at 2425 Reedie Drive and providing complete inspection documentation for insurance carriers.

🔶 Aluminum branch circuit wiring in Wheaton's 1965–1973 homes: Wheaton has a significant number of homes built in the aluminum wiring era — the 8-year window when copper prices spiked and aluminum was substituted for branch circuit wiring throughout the country. Aluminum wiring loosens at outlet and switch connections over time, creating arcing that is a leading fire cause in homes of this vintage. Wheaton homeowners who know their home was built between 1965 and 1973 should request an aluminum wiring assessment at the free estimate — it cannot be confirmed without opening outlet boxes, but it is the single most important electrical safety check for homes of that specific vintage. CO/ALR device remediation ($2,500–$5,500 for most Wheaton homes) or full copper rewiring ($9,000–$19,000+) are the two remediation paths DMV General Contracting Group presents with honest cost and risk comparisons at every Wheaton estimate.
