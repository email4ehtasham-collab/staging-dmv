New Siding & Energy Efficiency for Wheaton's Postwar Homes

🌡️ The Wheaton energy upgrade opportunity: Wheaton's 1950s–1970s split-levels and colonials were built with 2×4 exterior framing and no continuous wall insulation — a standard for the era that produces wall assemblies with R-values of R-4 to R-8 in today's assessment. Full siding replacement is the only practical opportunity to add continuous insulation to these walls from the exterior without removing interior drywall. Insulated vinyl provides R-2 to R-4 of continuous foam. Rigid foam under James Hardie provides R-3 to R-6. On a typical Wheaton split-level, that insulation layer eliminates the thermal bridging at each stud — the primary heat loss pathway in 2×4 framing — improving both comfort and annual energy costs. MoCo DPS permit is not required for standard siding replacement, but rigid foam addition may trigger a permit in some cases — our permit team advises on this at the estimate.

What is the difference between standard vinyl and insulated vinyl for Wheaton homes?
Standard vinyl siding has no foam backer — it installs directly over the house wrap and provides no additional insulation value.
