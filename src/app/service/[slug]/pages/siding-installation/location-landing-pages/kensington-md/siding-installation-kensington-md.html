What Kensington's Old Siding Hides — What We Assess Before Pricing

⚠️ Old-Home Siding Conditions in Kensington's 1920s–1940s Bungalows

  • Sheathing moisture damage at bottom courses: Kensington's original bungalows frequently have diagonal board sheathing — individual boards installed at 45 degrees beneath the original siding. At the bottom courses, where siding meets the foundation, moisture accumulation over decades commonly produces soft, rotted sheathing boards that cannot support new siding fasteners. This condition is not visible until the old siding is removed. DMV General Contracting Group assesses visible sheathing condition at the estimate appointment and discusses the likelihood and typical cost of sheathing repair before a final price is given — preventing the mid-project discovery that produces change orders homeowners were not expecting.
  • Knob-and-tube wiring in wall cavities: Kensington bungalows built before 1940 frequently have original knob-and-tube wiring running through the wall cavities behind the exterior siding. When siding removal exposes knob-and-tube wiring at exterior outlet locations, fixture mounting points, or wall cavity penetrations, this matters particularly if insulated siding is planned: adding spray foam or rigid insulation around active knob-and-tube wiring is a fire hazard and typically voids homeowners insurance coverage for the affected circuits. Our crews identify knob-and-tube at penetrations during siding removal and advise homeowners before proceeding with insulated siding installation over affected wall sections.
  • Diagonal board sheathing — different fastening requirements: Diagonal board sheathing requires different fastening patterns and techniques than modern OSB or plywood sheathing. James Hardie fiber cement and LP SmartSide have specific fastener requirements for diagonal board substrates. Our installers know these requirements — they do not assume every Kensington bungalow has modern sheathing and apply the same fastening pattern they use on a 2005 colonial in Gaithersburg.
  • Original wood siding with lead paint: Kensington bungalows with original pre-1978 wood siding may have lead-based paint on the siding surface. Siding removal on these homes requires lead-safe work practices — containment, proper disposal, and HEPA vacuuming of debris. DMV General Contracting Group follows EPA RRP (Renovation, Repair and Painting) lead-safe practices on all Kensington siding projects where lead paint is possible based on the home's age and paint history.
  • Window and door trim conditions: Original wood window casings and door surrounds on Kensington bungalows frequently have rot at the bases and sill areas — rot that becomes visible when siding is removed. New siding installed against rotted window trim will fail at the joint within 2–3 years. DMV General Contracting Group assesses trim condition at the estimate and includes trim repair or replacement in the scope where needed, rather than installing new siding against existing rotted wood and leaving the homeowner with a siding failure at every window within a few years.

Signs Your Kensington Bungalow Needs New Siding

🍄

Rot at Bottom Courses

Soft, punky wood at the base of siding boards where they meet the foundation — moisture has penetrated behind the bottom courses.

🎨

Paint Failing Every Few Years

If exterior paint is peeling or bubbling within 2–4 years of repainting, the siding substrate — not the paint — has failed.

🔍

Cracking & Splitting Boards

Horizontal cracks along board grain lines and split ends — original cedar or wood boards near end of serviceable life.

💧

Interior Moisture Stains

Water stains on interior wall plaster or drywall beneath exterior siding — active infiltration behind the siding envelope.

🌡️

Drafts at Exterior Walls

Noticeable cold air infiltration at exterior wall surfaces — original wood siding without house wrap allows significant air leakage.

🏚️

Faded or Chalking Paint

Severely chalked or faded paint that no longer holds color — the siding surface has degraded beyond what paint can restore.

Does the Town of Kensington require a permit for siding replacement?
Like-for-like siding replacement in Kensington generally does not require a permit. What happens if knob-and-tube wiring is found behind the siding?
If knob-and-tube wiring is visible at exterior penetrations during siding removal, we document and advise the homeowner before proceeding with insulated siding installation over those sections. Adding spray foam or rigid insulation around active knob-and-tube wiring is a fire hazard and typically voids homeowners insurance for affected circuits. In most cases, the right next step is an electrician assessment of the affected circuits — determining whether the wiring is still active, whether it can be safely insulated, or whether it should be replaced before insulated siding is installed. We do not skip this step to move the project forward faster.
