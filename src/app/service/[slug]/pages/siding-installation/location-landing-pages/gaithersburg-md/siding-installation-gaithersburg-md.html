Why Gaithersburg Siding Projects Need Local Jurisdiction Expertise

🏛️ The Gaithersburg Permitting & HOA Case — Explained Honestly

  • Gaithersburg is genuinely split between two permitting authorities: Unlike most Montgomery County communities, Gaithersburg is an incorporated municipality with its own Permitting & Inspections department at City Hall. Homes inside the official city limits are permitted by the City. But zip codes like 20877, 20878, 20879, 20882, and 20886 also cover pockets of unincorporated county land — parts of North Potomac, Darnestown, Washingtonian, and Montgomery Village — where Montgomery County DPS is the actual authority. A homeowner filing with the wrong office loses weeks. We confirm jurisdiction against the property record before submitting anything.
  • Kentlands and Lakelands add a second layer — HOA architectural review: Both communities were built as Traditional Neighborhood Developments with active architectural review boards that govern exterior materials, siding profiles, and approved color palettes. A siding replacement that would need no sign-off in Olde Towne or Diamond Farm requires a full ARC submission — material spec sheets, color samples, sometimes a site plan — in Kentlands or Lakelands, and incomplete submissions are a common cause of stalled projects in these neighborhoods.
  • Gaithersburg's housing stock spans two very different eras: Older sections like Olde Towne, Diamond Farm, and Washingtonian Woods date to the 1960s–1980s and carry original aluminum or first-generation vinyl that's well past its service life. Kentlands and Lakelands, built from the 1990s onward, have newer siding that's typically failing for different reasons — factory finish fade, seam separation, or storm damage — rather than age alone. The right material recommendation and the right permitting path depend on knowing which Gaithersburg you're actually in.
  • The energy case still applies to Gaithersburg's older homes: Gaithersburg's postwar-era split-levels and colonials were framed with minimal wall insulation, the same as much of inner Montgomery County. Insulated vinyl or James Hardie with a rigid foam backer adds continuous R-value to these walls during the same project that replaces the aging siding — compounding the curb appeal upgrade with real annual energy savings.

Frequently Asked Questions — Siding Installation in Gaithersburg, MD

Does my Gaithersburg siding project need a City permit or a County permit?
It depends on your exact address. Gaithersburg is an incorporated city, and homes within the official city limits pull permits through City of Gaithersburg Permitting & Inspections at 31 South Summit Avenue rather than Montgomery County DPS. However, many addresses with a Gaithersburg mailing address and zip code — including parts of North Potomac, Darnestown, Washingtonian, and Montgomery Village — sit in unincorporated Montgomery County and are permitted through MoCo DPS in Wheaton instead. Like-for-like siding replacement often does not require a permit either way, but sheathing repair, insulation additions, or soffit and fascia work can trigger the requirement under whichever jurisdiction actually governs the property. DMV General Contracting Group verifies which authority applies before filing anything, and handles the filing and inspections in-house.
Do Kentlands and Lakelands require HOA approval for new siding?
Yes. Kentlands and Lakelands are governed by active architectural review boards that require homeowners to submit siding material, profile, and color selections for approval before installation begins — a step that does not exist in older, non-HOA Gaithersburg neighborhoods like Olde Towne, Diamond Farm, or Washingtonian Woods. Rejected or incomplete submissions are a common cause of project delays in these communities. DMV General Contracting Group prepares Kentlands and Lakelands ARC submission packages with manufacturer spec sheets and color samples that match each community's approved materials list, so the review moves through cleanly on the first pass.
What is the difference between standard vinyl and insulated vinyl for Gaithersburg homes?
Standard vinyl siding has no foam backer — it installs directly over the house wrap and provides no additional insulation value. Insulated vinyl has a rigid foam backer permanently bonded to each panel, adding R-2 to R-4 of continuous insulation to the wall assembly. For Gaithersburg's older split-levels and colonials with minimal original wall insulation, insulated vinyl is the significantly better choice — it improves comfort, reduces energy costs, and is generally pre-approved under Kentlands and Lakelands HOA guidelines as well. The price difference on a full Gaithersburg home is typically $1,500–$3,000.
