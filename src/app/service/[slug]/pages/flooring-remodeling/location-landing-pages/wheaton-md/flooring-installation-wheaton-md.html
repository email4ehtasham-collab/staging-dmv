Wheaton's Two-Zone Flooring Strategy — Upper Level vs Lower Level

Every Wheaton split-level flooring project starts with the same fundamental question: which level are we flooring, and what are the moisture conditions there? The answer determines the material — not aesthetic preference alone.

🪵 Upper Main Level

Above Grade — Hardwood Zone
  • Living room, dining room, main kitchen
  • Upper bedrooms and hallway
  • Above grade — not in contact with slab moisture
  • Original hardwood often present and refinishable
  • New hardwood appropriate where original is absent
  • Engineered hardwood preferred where any moisture uncertainty exists
  • LVP a strong practical option for kitchen and high-traffic areas
  • Hardwood adds the most resale value here — buyers expect it

💧 Lower Level

At / Below Grade — LVP Zone
  • Family room, laundry, lower-level bedrooms
  • ADU / rental unit space — active in Wheaton
  • On or adjacent to concrete slab — moisture present seasonally
  • Solid hardwood here = cupping, buckling, squeaking within seasons
  • Engineered hardwood: marginal — still moisture-sensitive
  • LVP: 100% waterproof, floats over slab, realistic wood look
  • Porcelain tile for lower-level bathrooms and laundry rooms
  • Durable wear layer essential for ADU rental turnover
💡 ADU Lower Level — Wheaton's Most Requested Flooring Scope: Wheaton is one of Montgomery County's most active ADU markets. Lower-level conversions to rental units are common across Glenmont, Forest Estates, and Wheaton Hills. For every ADU conversion, DMV-GC Group installs premium LVP throughout the lower level — waterproof for below-grade conditions, durable for rental turnover, and visually warm enough to make the ADU feel like a quality living space rather than a finished basement. Flooring Types We Install in Wheaton, MD

Upper Level — First Choice

Hardwood Refinishing

The right first recommendation for Wheaton's 1950s–70s colonials and split-level upper levels with surviving original strip hardwood. Wheaton's postwar housing frequently has original 2.25-inch or 3-inch oak that has remained under carpet for decades — structurally sound but cosmetically worn. DMV-GC Group checks remaining wood thickness above the tongue before recommending refinishing. When viable, restoring the original floor at $3–$6 per square foot is far more cost-effective than replacing it at $8–$15 per square foot — and produces a floor with the authentic character of old-growth oak that modern replacements cannot fully replicate.

Lifespan: Extends original floor 20–40 more years  |  Cost: $3–$6/sq ft
Upper Level — New Installation

Solid & Engineered Hardwood

When original hardwood is beyond refinishing or absent on the upper main level, new solid or engineered hardwood is the right replacement. White oak in natural to warm medium stain is the most popular current choice in Wheaton's renovation market. For upper-level rooms where there is any crawlspace moisture concern below — less common in Wheaton's slab-founded split-levels than in Kensington's crawlspace homes, but present in some properties — engineered hardwood is the more dimensionally stable choice over solid. Solid hardwood is fully appropriate for confirmed dry above-grade installations with standard plywood subfloor.

Lifespan: 50–100 yrs (solid) · 25–40 yrs (engineered)  |  Cost: $8–$15/sq ft installed
Lower Level & ADU — The Only Right Choice

Luxury Vinyl Plank (LVP)

LVP is the correct flooring for every Wheaton split-level lower level and ADU conversion — not a compromise, not a budget choice, the technically correct specification for the conditions. 100% waterproof against the below-grade slab moisture that Wheaton's split-level lower levels seasonally experience. Dimensionally stable regardless of humidity. Floating installation tolerates minor slab irregularities without adhesive. Available in highly realistic wood looks matched to complement the upper-level hardwood. Durable wear layer resists the tenant foot traffic that ADU rental units generate. DMV-GC Group specifies COREtec, Shaw Floorté, or Karndean at 12mil or greater wear layer for ADU units and active households.

Lifespan: 20–30 yrs  |  Cost: $5–$10/sq ft installed

Full written estimate by room and zone, including all required subfloor prep. No surprises mid-project when conditions are discovered during demolition.

3

Subfloor Preparation

Upper level: loose boards re-secured, soft spots patched, sanding sequence determined. Lower level: existing flooring removed, slab surface cleaned, high spots ground down, moisture barrier installed beneath LVP where slab moisture testing indicates it is needed. Tile areas: appropriate waterproofing membrane and backer installed. All prep completed before any finish material is specified or ordered for that zone.

4

Installation by Zone

Upper level: hardwood refinishing (sand, stain approval, finish coats with dry time) or new hardwood installation with proper nail or staple schedule. Lower level: LVP floating installation with correct expansion gaps, seam staggering, and perimeter transitions. Bathrooms: tile set with waterproofing membrane, appropriate mortar, and grout. Stairway between levels: matching stair nosings and transitions at zone boundaries.

5

Trim, Transitions & Final Cleanup

Baseboards, shoe molding, stair nosings, and material transition strips installed throughout — particularly clean transitions at the stairway between hardwood upper level and LVP lower level. Final cleanup of all debris, sawdust, and finish residue. Walkthrough with you before close-out — every detail reviewed before we call the project complete.

Why does solid hardwood fail on Wheaton split-level lower levels?
The lower level of a Wheaton split-level sits on or directly adjacent to a concrete slab. Concrete slabs transmit ground moisture upward seasonally — summer humidity causes the slab to release moisture vapor, which solid hardwood absorbs and swells from. Winter dryness causes contraction. This seasonal cycle causes solid hardwood to cup (edges rise), buckle (boards lift from subfloor), and develop persistent squeaks that cannot be corrected without full removal and replacement. This is a material mismatch with the conditions, not an installation quality issue. LVP is 100% waterproof and dimensionally stable regardless of below-grade moisture — the technically correct specification for Wheaton split-level lower levels.
What LVP is best for a Wheaton ADU rental unit?
For ADU rental units, DMV-GC Group specifies commercial-grade LVP with a wear layer of 12mil or greater — COREtec Plus, Shaw Floorté Pro, or equivalent. The thicker wear layer handles the heavier foot traffic and furniture dragging that rental use generates, and resists the surface scratching that thinner residential-grade LVP shows within a few years of rental occupancy. The investment in a 12mil wear layer versus an 8mil residential product is typically $0.50–$1.50 per square foot — worthwhile for a rental floor that needs to last through multiple tenant cycles without replacement.
Can my original Wheaton split-level hardwood be refinished?
Often yes — Wheaton's 1950s–70s split-levels frequently have original 2.25-inch or 3-inch strip oak on the upper main level that has survived under carpet in reasonable condition. DMV-GC Group checks the remaining thickness above the tongue during the free consultation. If sufficient thickness remains and the wood is structurally sound, refinishing is the right choice at $3–$6 per square foot — far less than the $8–$15 per square foot cost of replacement, and more authentic to the home's original character.
