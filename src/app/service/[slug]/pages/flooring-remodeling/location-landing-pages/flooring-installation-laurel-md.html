Laurel's Split-Level Housing — The Two-Zone Flooring Strategy

Laurel's 1960s–80s split-level homes — one of the most prevalent housing types in the community — require a flooring approach that varies by level. This is not a preference; it is a structural and moisture reality.

🏠 The Split-Level Two-Zone Rule for Laurel: Above-grade main level and upper bedrooms → solid hardwood (where subfloor and moisture support it) or engineered hardwood. Below-grade lower-level family room or finished basement → LVP, always. Solid hardwood installed below grade in a Laurel split-level will cup, buckle, and gap as it absorbs moisture from the slab below. The floor will fail within two to three seasons regardless of installation quality. LVP is 100% waterproof and moisture-immune. The visual continuity between wide-plank oak on the main level and matched-tone wide-plank LVP on the lower level creates a seamless whole-home look without the moisture risk that solid hardwood below grade always carries in Laurel's split-level and colonial construction.

Our Flooring Installation Process in Laurel

1

Free In-Home Consultation — Jurisdiction, Subfloor & Zone Assessment

We confirm your Laurel address jurisdiction (City, MoCo, or PG County), assess existing floors and subfloor conditions on each level, check remaining hardwood thickness where refinishing is being considered, identify the below-grade lower-level zone requiring LVP, and for townhome projects obtain the applicable HOA flooring rules. We bring stain samples for refinishing decisions and wide-plank oak samples for upgrade projects. No cost, no obligation.

2

Two-Zone Material Recommendation

Above-grade recommendation first: refinishing if original floor is viable and the goal is stain color refresh; wide-plank white oak if plank width upgrade is desired or original floor is beyond refinishing. Below-grade recommendation: premium LVP matched in tone to the above-grade hardwood for visual continuity at the stairway transition. For townhomes: LVP with HOA-compliant acoustic underlayment throughout above-grade hard floor areas. Written itemized estimate per zone.

3

Subfloor Preparation by Zone

Above-grade: squeak sources addressed, plywood levelness checked, any soft spots patched. Diagonal plank subfloor (in oldest Laurel homes) identified and uncoupling membrane specified for any tile areas. Below-grade: slab surface cleaned and leveled where needed, moisture barrier specified. For townhomes: HOA acoustic underlayment product confirmed and delivered before installation day.

4

Installation by Zone

Above-grade: hardwood acclimated 3–7 days, then nail-down wide-plank oak or refinishing as specified. Below-grade: LVP floated with moisture barrier and acoustic underlayment where required. Tile: thin-set with uncoupling membrane where subfloor warrants it. Stair treads installed to coordinate the visual transition between lower LVP and upper hardwood. Clean cuts at all thresholds and material transitions.

5

Trim, Transitions & Final Walkthrough

New baseboards, shoe molding, transition strips at all material changes including the critical lower-level-to-stairway transition. Stair nosings and treads coordinated with main-level hardwood. Complete cleanup. Final walkthrough before close-out and written warranty documentation.

Why can't I install solid hardwood on the lower level of my Laurel split-level?
Laurel split-level lower levels are below grade — they sit on or adjacent to a concrete slab that has direct contact with ground moisture. Solid hardwood is hygroscopic and absorbs moisture from below, causing it to swell seasonally, cup at board edges, gap in dry weather, and develop persistent squeaks over time. This failure is not a workmanship issue — it is what happens when solid hardwood is placed in a below-grade environment. LVP is 100% waterproof and unaffected by slab moisture. For Laurel split-levels, DMV-GC Group specifies LVP on every below-grade lower level without exception, and matches the LVP color tone to the above-grade hardwood for visual continuity at the stair transition.
