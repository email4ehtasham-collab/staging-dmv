Gaithersburg's Newer Construction Subfloor Advantage

One of the most practical but least-discussed flooring advantages of Gaithersburg's planned community homes is their subfloor. Understanding what your subfloor type means for your flooring project saves time, money, and mid-project surprises.

✅ What Gaithersburg's 1990s–2010s Construction Gives You

  • Standard plywood subfloor throughout: Kentlands, Lakelands, Crown Farm, and Washingtonian Woods homes were built with ¾-inch tongue-and-groove plywood subfloor panels — the modern standard that any flooring type can be installed on without additional preparation. No diagonal plank boards, no gaps, no soft spots from age. A relatively flat, stable substrate from the first day of the flooring project.
  • No uncoupling membrane required for tile (in most cases): Unlike Kensington's diagonal plank subfloors where tile installation requires an uncoupling membrane to prevent cracking at the plank joints, Gaithersburg's plywood subfloors support direct tile installation with appropriate adhesive mortar in most cases. This reduces tile project cost and complexity compared to older homes.
  • Solid hardwood can be nail-down installed on all above-grade floors: Plywood subfloor allows standard nail-down or staple-down solid hardwood installation without the moisture or structural concerns that older construction sometimes presents. Wide-plank white oak nail-down on the main living level is straightforward for Gaithersburg's newer homes.
  • Below-grade floors still need LVP: The subfloor advantage applies to above-grade floors only. Lower-level finished spaces in Gaithersburg split-levels and walkout colonials are still on or adjacent to concrete slab — LVP remains the correct waterproof specification for any below-grade finished floor, regardless of the plywood subfloor advantage above grade.
  • Builder-grade flooring is the starting point, not the finish line: Most Gaithersburg planned community homes were delivered with builder-grade carpet in living areas and basic hardwood or vinyl in kitchens. The clean plywood subfloor means upgrading to wide-plank white oak or premium LVP is a straightforward process — no extensive subfloor prep, no hidden surprises, and a finished result significantly above the builder standard.

HOA Flooring Rules in Gaithersburg's Attached Communities

Interior flooring changes — replacing carpet with hardwood or LVP — do not require HOA Architectural Review Committee approval in most Gaithersburg communities because they are not visible exterior modifications. However, Gaithersburg's attached townhome communities and multi-story condo buildings have a different concern: noise transmission.

🏘️ The Noise Transmission Issue: In Kentlands, Lakelands, and other attached townhome communities where homes share party walls and stacked floor-ceiling assemblies with neighbors, hard flooring (hardwood, LVP, tile) on upper levels transmits impact noise — footsteps, dropped objects, chair movement — to the unit or home below more readily than carpet does. Some Gaithersburg condo associations and HOA-governed buildings address this by requiring a minimum STC-rated underlayment beneath any hard flooring installed on upper stories, or by limiting hard flooring installation to ground-level floors only. DMV-GC Group verifies the applicable flooring rules for every Gaithersburg attached townhome and condo project before materials are ordered — and specifies the correct acoustic underlayment where required. This is not a reason to avoid hardwood or LVP in attached Gaithersburg communities; it is a specification detail that, when handled correctly, keeps the installation compliant and the neighbor relationship intact.

Refinishing vs Replacing Your Gaithersburg Hardwood — The Honest Assessment

Gaithersburg's Kentlands and Lakelands homes from the late 1990s–early 2000s were commonly delivered with 2.25-inch strip hardwood in medium or dark stain. After 20–25 years, this original hardwood falls into one of two categories:

🪵 Still Refinishable: Most Kentlands and Lakelands original hardwood from 1995–2005 has not been refinished before and retains sufficient thickness for one full refinish. At $3–$6 per square foot versus $10–$18 for wide-plank white oak replacement, refinishing with a stain color change is the smarter financial choice when the floor is structurally sound. A medium warm tone or natural stain over the existing strip hardwood completely transforms the room at roughly one-third the cost of replacement. DMV-GC Group brings stain samples to your home before any commitment.

📌 When Replacement Makes Sense: If you want to change plank width — from the original 2.25-inch strip to 5-inch wide-plank white oak — refinishing cannot achieve that. Wide-plank flooring is one of the most impactful visual upgrades in Gaithersburg's renovation market precisely because the wider planks create a fundamentally different feel than the original strip hardwood, regardless of stain color. If plank width upgrade is the goal, full replacement is the path. DMV-GC Group presents both options honestly with exact costs for each so you can make an informed decision.
