What Garrett Park's 1900s–1940s Construction Means for Your Flooring Project

Working in Garrett Park's historic homes requires understanding the construction conditions that age has created. Every one of the following characteristics is something DMV-GC Group assesses before any material is specified or ordered.

🪵 Old-Growth Hardwood — What Makes It Special

The original strip hardwood in Garrett Park's 1900s–1940s bungalows was cut from old-growth forests — trees that grew slowly over centuries, producing wood with tight annual rings, high density, and extraordinary dimensional stability compared to modern plantation-grown lumber. A 1920s Garrett Park bungalow's original oak or fir floor is not just old — it is made from material that is no longer commercially available at any price. When that floor is structurally sound and has remaining thickness for sanding, refinishing it is not just cost-effective — it preserves something genuinely irreplaceable.

📐 Diagonal Plank Subfloor

Garrett Park's pre-1950 homes have diagonal plank subfloors — 1×6 or 1×8 boards laid at 45 degrees over the floor joists, the pre-plywood construction standard. This subfloor type is structurally sound but has board-to-board joints that move seasonally. Tile installation over diagonal plank subfloor without an uncoupling membrane leads to cracked tiles within a few years. DMV-GC Group installs Schlüter DITRA or equivalent uncoupling membrane as a standard step on every tile project in Garrett Park homes — the same protocol as in Kensington's equally aged housing stock.

💧 Crawlspace Moisture

Garrett Park's wooded, shaded lots — many of them adjacent to the old rail corridor green space — create crawlspace conditions that can be more humid than comparable lots in less shaded neighborhoods. Crawlspace moisture causes solid hardwood on the first floor to cup and squeak regardless of installation quality. DMV-GC Group checks crawlspace moisture before specifying first-floor hardwood. Where moisture is elevated, engineered hardwood or LVP is the honest recommendation, even when solid hardwood would be more authentic to the period. Flooring that fails within two seasons is never the right choice.

🔍 Remaining Hardwood Thickness

Original ¾-inch strip hardwood loses a thin layer with each sanding. After 80–100 years and potentially one or two previous refinishes, remaining thickness above the tongue may be ½ inch, 3/8 inch, or less. DMV-GC Group checks remaining thickness with a gauge before recommending refinishing — we never recommend sanding a floor that does not have sufficient material remaining. When thickness is marginal, we advise on options: a light screen-and-recoat that removes only the finish layer (not wood), versus a full sand that would deplete the remaining thickness.

🔧 Squeaky Floors — What Causes Them

Many Garrett Park bungalows have floors that squeak — and many homeowners assume refinishing will fix the problem. It will not. Squeaks in older Garrett Park homes come from seasonal wood movement at the board-to-subfloor interface, loose subfloor boards, or gaps that have opened between subfloor boards over decades. DMV-GC Group addresses squeak sources before any refinishing begins — re-securing loose subfloor boards from below where crawlspace access allows, and using appropriate fasteners or construction adhesive at squeak points identified from above. A refinished floor that still squeaks is a job half done.

🏠 Renovation Context — Broader Scope Awareness

Garrett Park's oldest homes — those built before 1940 — may also have galvanized plumbing and knob-and-tube wiring that is being addressed as part of a broader renovation. When flooring is installed as part of a kitchen or bathroom remodel that also involves plumbing or electrical work, sequencing matters: flooring goes in after rough-in work but before finish work. DMV-GC Group coordinates flooring with all other trades in-house when the project scope includes multiple systems, ensuring the flooring is not damaged by subsequent tradespeople or installed in the wrong sequence.

When original hardwood has insufficient remaining thickness, significant rot or water damage, or is absent — new hardwood is the right replacement for Garrett Park's historic rooms.   • The reason to choose authentic flooring is not regulatory — it is practical: The original hardwood in a Garrett Park historic home adds value, authenticity, and character that no regulation requires but that every informed buyer recognizes. Addressing Squeaky Floors in Garrett Park Bungalows — Before Refinishing Begins

Squeaky floors are among the most common complaints in Garrett Park's historic homes — and among the most misunderstood. A refinishing job that does not address the squeak sources first leaves a beautiful-looking floor that still creaks underfoot. Here is what DMV-GC Group does before any sanding begins:

🔧 Squeak Assessment & Pre-Refinishing Repair: We identify squeak locations by walking the floor with the homeowner and mapping each one. Where crawlspace access is available, we can access the subfloor from below to re-secure loose boards and add blocking where needed — the most effective and least invasive squeak repair method. Where crawlspace access is not sufficient, we use a combination of long screws driven through the subfloor into the joists from above (countersunk and filled before sanding) and construction adhesive at seasonal-gap locations. Squeaks from seasonal wood movement — boards expanding and contracting at their edges — are minimized but not always eliminable in 100-year-old construction; we are honest about what is treatable and what is a permanent characteristic of the building's age.

Will refinishing remove all the character from my original Garrett Park floors?
No — when done correctly, refinishing restores the floor's appearance while preserving what makes it distinctive. The grain, density, and patina of old-growth oak or fir are properties of the wood itself, not the finish on top. Sanding removes the worn finish and a thin layer of wood surface — the old-growth character remains in every board. A natural or warm stain with a matte or satin finish (rather than the high-gloss polyurethane of the 1990s) actually reveals the wood's grain and character more authentically than the heavy finish coats that were applied in previous decades. Many Garrett Park homeowners are surprised at how much more beautiful their original floors look after refinishing than they imagined.
Does Garrett Park's historic district prevent me from changing my flooring?
No. Garrett Park's National Register Historic District designation governs exterior changes visible from public streets, not interior renovations. You can install any flooring material inside your home without historic review or approval. When flooring is part of a broader renovation requiring a building permit, the Town of Garrett Park issues permits at 4600 Oxford Street — DMV-GC Group handles Town permit submissions for applicable renovation scopes. Flooring-only projects typically do not require a building permit.
Why does DMV-GC Group check crawlspace moisture before recommending hardwood in Garrett Park?
Garrett Park's wooded, shaded lots create crawlspace humidity levels that can be higher than comparable properties in less shaded neighborhoods. Elevated crawlspace moisture causes solid hardwood on the first floor to absorb moisture, swell in summer, contract in winter, and over time cup, buckle, and develop persistent squeaks. This failure mode is not related to installation quality — it is a material mismatch with the actual conditions. Engineered hardwood is more dimensionally stable under these conditions; LVP is entirely moisture-immune. DMV-GC Group checks crawlspace moisture before specifying any first-floor flooring material in Garrett Park and recommends the material that will actually perform well in that home's specific conditions.
