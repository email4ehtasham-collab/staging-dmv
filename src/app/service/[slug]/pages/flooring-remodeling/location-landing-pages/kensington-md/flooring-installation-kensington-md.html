Kensington's Older Homes — Subfloor Realities Every Homeowner Should Understand

The subfloor conditions in Kensington's 1920s–50s housing stock are meaningfully different from newer construction — and they affect which flooring materials can be installed, how they must be installed, and what subfloor prep is required before installation begins. DMV-GC Group assesses subfloor condition on every Kensington project before any material is specified.

📐 Diagonal Plank Subfloor

Pre-1950 Kensington homes were built with diagonal plank subfloors — 1×6 or 1×8 tongue-and-groove boards laid at 45 degrees over the floor joists, rather than the plywood panels used in all post-1950 construction. This subfloor type is structurally strong but has gaps between planks, occasional soft spots from age, and a surface that is not perfectly flat by modern standards. Solid hardwood and engineered hardwood can be installed over diagonal plank subfloors with the correct nail or staple schedule. LVP floats over minor irregularities. Tile requires a concrete backer board or uncoupling membrane to prevent cracking over the movement of the plank joints below.

🔍 Remaining Hardwood Thickness

Original strip hardwood in Kensington's bungalows was typically installed at ¾-inch thickness — the same as modern solid hardwood. After 80–100 years and potentially one or two previous refinishes, the remaining thickness above the tongue may be ½-inch, 3/8-inch, or less. DMV-GC Group checks remaining thickness before recommending refinishing. If sufficient thickness remains, refinishing is the right choice. If not, the options are replacement or a skim-coat approach that removes minimal material. We never recommend refinishing floors that don't have sufficient thickness for a safe sand.

💧 Crawlspace Moisture

Many of Kensington's pre-1950 homes have crawlspace foundations rather than full basements — and crawlspace moisture can significantly affect flooring performance on the first floor. High crawlspace humidity causes solid hardwood to cup, squeak, and buckle over time regardless of how well the flooring was installed. DMV-GC Group checks crawlspace moisture conditions before specifying first-floor flooring materials — where moisture is elevated, engineered hardwood (more dimensionally stable than solid) or LVP (entirely moisture-immune) is the honest recommendation over solid hardwood.

🔧 Low Crawlspace Clearance

Some Kensington homes — particularly the smallest 1920s bungalows — have very low crawlspace clearances that limit or prevent standard floor sanding equipment access from below for any subfloor work. DMV-GC Group identifies access constraints during the pre-installation assessment and adjusts the work approach accordingly — sometimes working from above through lifted floorboards rather than below through an inaccessible crawlspace. This requires experience with older construction that most flooring-only contractors lack.

How do I know if my Kensington bungalow's original hardwood can be refinished?
The key factor is remaining wood thickness above the tongue — original ¾-inch strip hardwood needs at least 3/16" above the tongue to safely sand without compromising the mechanical connection. DMV-GC Group checks remaining thickness during the free in-home consultation using a thickness gauge. If sufficient thickness remains and the wood is structurally sound (not cupped, rotted, or buckled), refinishing is almost always the right choice — more authentic and significantly less expensive than replacement.
Why does tile crack in older Kensington homes?
The most common cause of tile failure in older Kensington homes is installation directly over diagonal plank subfloor boards without an uncoupling membrane. Diagonal plank subfloors have joints between boards that move seasonally — when the boards expand and contract, that movement telegraphs through a rigid tile installation and cracks the tiles or grout joints. An uncoupling membrane (such as Schlüter DITRA) absorbs this movement and prevents it from reaching the tile above. DMV-GC Group installs uncoupling membrane as a standard part of every tile project on older Kensington subfloors — the step that determines whether tile lasts 50 years or 2.
Is LVP a good choice for a Kensington home with a crawlspace?
Yes — LVP is often the best choice for first-floor rooms in Kensington homes with crawlspace foundations where moisture is a concern. Because LVP is 100% waterproof and dimensionally stable regardless of humidity, it will not cup, buckle, or squeak in response to crawlspace moisture the way solid hardwood can. For rooms where the natural wood look is important, premium LVP is genuinely difficult to distinguish from hardwood at casual inspection. DMV-GC Group checks crawlspace moisture during the consultation and advises honestly on whether solid hardwood, engineered hardwood, or LVP is appropriate for each room.
