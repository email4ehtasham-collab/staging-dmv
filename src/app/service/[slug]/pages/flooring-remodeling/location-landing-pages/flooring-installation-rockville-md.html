Rockville's Two Housing Eras — Two Different Flooring Starting Points

The right flooring approach for a Rockville home depends almost entirely on which construction era it represents. Here is the distinction that shapes every recommendation DMV-GC Group makes in Rockville:

🏗️ King Farm & Fallsgrove

2000s Planned Communities
  • Built 1998–2015 with standard plywood subfloors throughout
  • Original builder-grade carpet or narrow strip hardwood — 15–25 years old
  • Ready for a first significant flooring upgrade
  • Wide-plank white oak (5") is the most popular upgrade direction
  • LVP for kitchen and lower level where applicable
  • Large-format porcelain for primary bathrooms
  • No subfloor surprises — clean plywood, installation straightforward
  • Acoustic underlayment check for attached King Farm townhomes

  • Built 1950–1975 — diagonal plank subfloors in oldest homes

Flooring Types We Install in Rockville, MD

King Farm & Fallsgrove — Most Requested

Wide-Plank White Oak Hardwood

The dominant flooring upgrade in Rockville's 2000s planned communities. Wide-plank white oak — 5-inch planks in natural to warm medium stain with matte or satin finish — replaces original builder carpet or narrow strip hardwood in King Farm and Fallsgrove colonials and townhomes. Clean plywood subfloors make nail-down installation efficient and straightforward. The contemporary grain of white oak suits the traditional new urbanist character of King Farm and the colonial vocabulary of Fallsgrove equally well. Acoustic underlayment specified for attached King Farm townhomes where applicable. LVP complemented in kitchen and lower level to complete the whole-home package.

Lifespan: 50–100 years  |  Cost: $10–$18/sq ft installed
Twinbrook & Older Rockville — First Choice

Hardwood Refinishing

The right first recommendation for Rockville's postwar colonials and split-level upper levels with surviving original strip hardwood. Twinbrook's 1950s–60s housing frequently has original 2.25-inch or 3-inch oak that has been under carpet for years and retains sufficient thickness for a full refinish. DMV-GC Group checks remaining thickness above the tongue, addresses squeak sources before sanding, and offers the full stain color range. Current Rockville renovation trends favor a warm natural to medium tone with satin or matte finish — a significant improvement over the high-gloss dark finishes that were applied to many Rockville homes in the 1990s–2000s. Refinishing at $3–$6 per square foot versus $8–$15 for replacement is the economically clear choice when the floor supports it.

Lifespan: Extends original floor 20–40 more years  |  Cost: $3–$6/sq ft
Split-Level Lower Levels — Only Right Choice

Luxury Vinyl Plank (LVP)

LVP is the correct flooring for every Rockville split-level lower level — Rockshire, Twinbrook split-levels, and any below-grade finished space throughout the city. 100% waterproof, handles below-grade slab moisture without the cupping and buckling that solid hardwood develops within a few seasons at grade level. Available in wide-plank wood looks matched to complement the upper-level hardwood tone. Premium COREtec or Shaw Floorté specified for durability. Also the correct kitchen specification in both King Farm and Twinbrook homes where waterproofing at the sink zone matters more than material authenticity.

Lifespan: 20–30 years  |  Cost: $5–$10/sq ft installed
Primary Bathrooms & Kitchens

Porcelain & Ceramic Tile

Large-format porcelain for Rockville primary bathroom renovations — marble-look or concrete-look 24×24 in King Farm and Fallsgrove renovations; classic subway or hex tile for period-compatible bathrooms in Twinbrook's older colonials. For tile on diagonal plank subfloors in Twinbrook's oldest homes, uncoupling membrane is a mandatory step — the same protocol as in Kensington and Garrett Park. For tile on King Farm and Fallsgrove's plywood subfloors, direct installation is typically appropriate. DMV-GC Group coordinates tile specification with the subfloor type for every Rockville project.

Lifespan: 50+ years  |  Cost: $8–$18/sq ft installed
Bedrooms

Carpet

Premium carpet remains the practical choice for Rockville upper-level bedrooms — particularly in King Farm and Fallsgrove attached townhomes where carpet naturally manages noise transmission without acoustic underlayment specification. Quality nylon or wool pile in warm neutral tones suits Rockville's traditional and transitional interior palettes. For homeowners who want hardwood throughout including bedrooms, acoustic underlayment is specified for attached King Farm and Fallsgrove townhomes, and crawlspace moisture is confirmed manageable before solid hardwood is specified in Twinbrook upper-floor bedrooms.

Lifespan: 10–15 years  |  Cost: $4–$10/sq ft installed
Secondary Spaces

Engineered Hardwood & Laminate

Engineered hardwood for Rockville applications where solid hardwood's moisture sensitivity is a concern — upper-level rooms over crawlspace in Twinbrook's older homes, or main-level installations where any below-grade adjacency raises questions. More dimensionally stable than solid under humidity variation. Laminate for secondary bedrooms, home offices, and rental spaces where budget is the primary driver. Neither recommended for kitchens, bathrooms, or below-grade spaces. DMV-GC Group specifies engineered hardwood where the moisture assessment indicates it is the more appropriate choice over solid.

Lifespan: 25–40 yrs (engineered) · 15–25 yrs (laminate)  |  Cost: $6–$12 / $4–$8 per sq ft

Refinishing vs. Replacing Your Rockville Hardwood

For Twinbrook and older Rockville homeowners, this is the central flooring question. For King Farm and Fallsgrove homeowners with 2000s builder hardwood, a version of the same question applies — though the calculation differs.

🪵 Twinbrook & Older Rockville: Original 1950s–70s strip hardwood is often refinishable at $3–$6/sq ft — a fraction of the $8–$15/sq ft cost of new hardwood. DMV-GC Group checks thickness before recommending refinishing. When the floor is viable, refinishing is almost always the right call — more economical and more authentic than replacement with modern lumber.

📌 King Farm & Fallsgrove — Builder Strip Hardwood: Many 2000s Rockville planned community homes were delivered with 2.25-inch strip hardwood in a medium stain. This hardwood is typically 20+ years old and may have had one previous refinish. If a stain color change is all you want, refinishing is the cost-effective path. If you want wide-plank — 5-inch versus 2.25-inch — refinishing cannot change that, and replacement is required. DMV-GC Group presents both options with exact costs for each so the decision is clear before any work begins.

For split-level homes: above-grade material for upper level and LVP specification for lower level. Written itemized estimate by room before any commitment.

3

Subfloor Preparation by Era

King Farm / Fallsgrove: any levelness issues addressed on plywood subfloor, acoustic underlayment installed for attached townhomes where required. Twinbrook / older homes: loose plank boards re-secured, soft spots patched, squeak sources addressed, uncoupling membrane installed for tile on diagonal plank subfloor. All subfloor prep completed before any finish material is applied or installed.

4

Material Acclimation & Installation

Hardwood acclimated 3–7 days. Wide-plank oak nail-down with correct nail spacing for 5-inch planks. Original floor refinishing: drum and edge sand, stain application with approved color, multiple finish coats with dry time. LVP floating with correct expansion gaps. Tile with lippage-control system on large-format installations. Clean cuts and tight transitions throughout.

5

Trim, Transitions & Final Walkthrough

New baseboards, shoe molding, transition strips at all material changes, and stair nosings where applicable. Thorough cleanup. Final walkthrough with you before close-out — every seam, every transition, and every zone boundary reviewed before the project is called complete.

Frequently Asked Questions — Flooring in Rockville, MD

How much does flooring installation cost in Rockville, MD?
Flooring in Rockville typically costs $3–$18 per square foot depending on material and neighborhood. Hardwood refinishing for Twinbrook and older homes costs $3–$6 per square foot. Wide-plank white oak for King Farm and Fallsgrove runs $10–$18 per square foot. Standard hardwood runs $8–$14 per square foot. Premium LVP costs $5–$10 per square foot. Is my Twinbrook colonial's original hardwood refinishable?
Often yes. Twinbrook's 1950s–60s colonials frequently have original 2.25-inch or 3-inch strip oak that has been under carpet for decades and retains sufficient thickness for refinishing. DMV-GC Group checks remaining thickness above the tongue during the free consultation before any recommendation is made. When viable — and it often is — refinishing at $3–$6 per square foot is far less than replacement at $8–$15 per square foot, and produces a floor with the authentic character of the original material.
Should I install wide-plank oak or refinish my existing King Farm hardwood?
It depends on your goal. If you want a stain color change — lighter or warmer than the existing — refinishing the original 2.25-inch strip hardwood costs $3–$6 per square foot versus $10–$18 for wide-plank replacement. If you want a plank width change from 2.25-inch to 5-inch, refinishing cannot achieve that — full replacement is required. DMV-GC Group presents both options with exact costs and helps you identify which path delivers what you actually want. We bring stain samples to show you what a refinish would produce in your home's specific light before you commit to either path.
Does my King Farm or Fallsgrove HOA need to approve interior flooring changes?
Generally no — interior flooring changes are not exterior modifications and are not reviewed by King Farm or Fallsgrove's HOA Architectural Review Committees. However, if you are in an attached King Farm or Fallsgrove townhome, your HOA may have rules about acoustic underlayment under hard flooring on upper levels to limit noise transmission to neighbors below. DMV-GC Group checks the applicable HOA flooring rules for every attached townhome project before ordering materials, and specifies the correct acoustic underlayment where required — keeping the installation compliant without any action required from you.
