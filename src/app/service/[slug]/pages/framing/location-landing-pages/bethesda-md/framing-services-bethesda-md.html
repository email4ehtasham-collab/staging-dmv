How do you know if a wall is load-bearing in a Bethesda colonial?

Load-bearing walls in Bethesda's colonials run perpendicular to the floor joists, are positioned over or adjacent to the foundation, and carry the weight of the floor or roof above. In most Bethesda colonials, the wall between the kitchen and dining room and the wall at the center of the home running front-to-back are load-bearing. DMV General Contracting Group assesses load-bearing conditions before any wall removal is scoped or priced — we do not make assumptions about wall conditions in a home we haven't assessed in person.

What is temporary shoring and why is it needed for load-bearing wall removal?

Temporary shoring is a system of posts and beams installed on both sides of a load-bearing wall before any part of the wall is cut or removed. It transfers the structural load from the wall to the floor temporarily while the permanent beam is installed. Without temporary shoring, removing a load-bearing wall — even partially — can cause the structure above to shift or settle. DMV General Contracting Group installs temporary shoring as a standard step before every load-bearing wall removal, regardless of span or scope.
