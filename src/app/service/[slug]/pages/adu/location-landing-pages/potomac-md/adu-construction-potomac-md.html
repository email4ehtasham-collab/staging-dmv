What Is an ADU? — And Why Potomac Properties Are Ideal

An Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) — also called an in-law suite, guest cottage, carriage house, or backyard apartment — is a fully self-contained secondary residential unit built on the same lot as a primary home. An ADU has its own bedroom, bathroom, kitchen or kitchenette, and separate entrance — it functions as a complete independent living space.

In Potomac and throughout unincorporated Montgomery County, ADUs are permitted under the county's ADU ordinance on any qualifying single-family residential lot. And Potomac's estate properties are among the most favorable for ADU construction in the entire county — for a simple reason: the lots are large enough to accommodate a genuinely substantial detached ADU without crowding the primary residence, and the homes are valuable enough that the ADU addition creates proportionally significant value.

💡 Potomac ADU Opportunity: On Potomac's typical 1–5 acre estate lots, a well-designed detached ADU adds $150,000–$350,000+ to property value and generates $2,500–$4,500/month in rental income — making it one of the highest absolute-dollar-return investments available in the Montgomery County real estate market.

Montgomery County ADU Zoning Rules — What Potomac Homeowners Need to Know

Potomac is in unincorporated Montgomery County, so ADU construction is governed by Montgomery County's ADU ordinance. Here are the key rules that apply to Potomac properties:

📋 Montgomery County ADU Ordinance — Key Requirements for Potomac

  • Lot size minimum: The lot must be at least 6,000 sq ft to permit an ADU — essentially every Potomac single-family property qualifies, as most Potomac lots are significantly larger.
  • ADU size limit: An ADU cannot exceed 1,200 sq ft or 50% of the gross floor area of the primary dwelling, whichever is less. Most Potomac estates have main homes well over 4,000 sq ft, meaning the 1,200 sq ft cap is the binding limit — which still allows a generous 2-bedroom ADU.
  • Detached ADU setbacks: A detached ADU must comply with the rear and side yard setback requirements of the zoning district. Potomac's RE-2 and RE-1 zoning districts have larger setback requirements than denser suburban zones — but Potomac's large lots almost always provide ample room to meet them.
  • Lot coverage: The total impervious surface and building coverage limits of the zoning district apply. On Potomac's large estate lots, a detached ADU footprint rarely challenges these limits.
  • One ADU per lot: Only one ADU is permitted on each single-family lot in Montgomery County.
  • Owner-occupancy: The property owner must reside in either the primary dwelling or the ADU. Investment properties where neither unit is owner-occupied do not qualify for an ADU permit under the county's current ordinance.
  • Certificate of Occupancy: A COO from Montgomery County is required before the ADU can be legally occupied or rented. DMV-GC Group manages the COO application and all required inspections as a standard part of every ADU project.
  • Separate utilities: Separate electric metering is recommended for rental ADUs — DMV-GC Group coordinates meter separation with Pepco as part of most ADU projects. Water and sewer typically remain on the main home's account unless separate connections are specifically requested.
  • Design standards: Montgomery County's ADU ordinance requires the ADU to be architecturally compatible with the primary home. On Potomac estate properties, this compatibility standard often results in detached ADUs that are genuinely attractive structures — matching the materials and character of the main home. DMV-GC Group's architects design to this standard as a matter of course.
⚠️ HOA Note for Avenel and Other Planned Communities: Portions of Potomac — including Avenel — are governed by HOAs that may have additional architectural review requirements beyond Montgomery County's ADU ordinance. If your property is in an HOA community, ADU design and placement may require HOA Architectural Review Committee (ARC) approval before Montgomery County permits are filed. DMV-GC Group identifies any HOA review requirements during the consultation and advises on the most efficient path to approval.

ADU Design Quality in Potomac — Matching the Main Home

On a Potomac estate property where the main home has been built and finished to a high standard, an ADU that looks like an afterthought undermines both the property's aesthetic and its market value. 🏛️ Architectural Compatibility

Exterior materials, roof pitch, window proportions, and architectural details that match or complement the main home. A Colonial Revival estate does not get a modern flat-roof ADU. A contemporary home does not get a Victorian cottage. Architectural compatibility is both a Montgomery County requirement and a sound design principle.

🔧 Quality of Systems

Separate HVAC system sized for the ADU's square footage — not tapped from the main home's system. Independent electrical sub-panel. Plumbing to match the fixture quality of the price point. Insulation to current energy code. A quality ADU functions as a complete home, not a roughed-in afterthought.

🪟 Interior Finishes

Kitchen and bathroom finishes that match the rental or use context — a $3,500/month rental unit warrants stone countertops and quality fixtures. A caretaker's cottage warrants durable, practical materials. DMV-GC Group's design team specifies finishes appropriate to the intended use and price point of each Potomac ADU.

🌿 Site Integration

A detached ADU on a Potomac estate lot needs to be sited and landscaped in a way that feels intentional — not dropped in the rear yard without context. We assess the existing landscape, drainage, trees, and sight lines during the design phase to site the ADU where it looks like it belongs.
