Chevy Chase, Maryland is not one jurisdiction — it is a mosaic of five incorporated villages (Chevy Chase Village, Town of Chevy Chase, Village of Chevy Chase Section 3, Village of Chevy Chase Section 5, and Village of Martin's Additions), plus unincorporated areas governed by Montgomery County. Types of ADUs We Build in Chevy Chase, MD

Chevy Chase's Colonial Revival basements have specific characteristics — plaster or poured concrete foundations, ceiling heights ranging from 6'8" to 8'0" depending on construction era, and older systems in pre-1950 homes — that DMV-GC Group assesses honestly during the consultation. A well-executed Chevy Chase basement ADU can be a genuinely elegant unit that suits the character of the home above.

Many of Chevy Chase's older homes have detached garages — sometimes built as true carriage houses — at the rear of the property. Where these structures have sufficient square footage and ceiling height, conversion to a studio or one-bedroom ADU is feasible. A rear-yard carriage house conversion has lower impact on the street-facing historic character than a front-facing addition. These ADUs often command premium rents in Chevy Chase given their detached character and often charming architectural proportions. Chevy Chase's Five Incorporated Villages — Which One Governs Your Property?

This is the first and most critical question for any Chevy Chase ADU project. The five incorporated villages of Chevy Chase each have independent municipal governments and issue their own building permits. The unincorporated remainder of the Chevy Chase community uses Montgomery County DPS. Knowing which authority applies to your specific parcel — not just your mailing address — is non-negotiable before any permit work begins.

ADU Construction in Chevy Chase's Historic Homes — What to Assess Before Designing

Chevy Chase's 1900s–1940s Colonial Revival and Tudor homes are architecturally exceptional — and they present specific construction realities for ADU projects that require honest pre-design assessment. DMV-GC Group addresses all of these during the free consultation.

🔧 Historic Home ADU Assessment — Chevy Chase Specific

  • Foundation type and era: Homes built before 1920 in Chevy Chase commonly have rubble stone or early brick foundations — more porous than poured concrete and requiring interior moisture management. Homes from the 1920s–1940s more commonly have poured concrete or concrete block. The foundation type determines the moisture management approach for any basement ADU.
  • Basement ceiling height: Chevy Chase basement ceiling heights vary meaningfully by construction era — 1910s–1920s homes often have 6'6"–7'4" heights, while 1930s–1940s construction more commonly reaches 7'4"–8'0". DMV-GC Group measures every square foot during the consultation. Areas below the 7-foot minimum cannot count as habitable ADU space.
  • Plaster walls and ceilings: Chevy Chase's older homes typically have original plaster walls — not drywall. Plaster is heavier than drywall and requires different fastening for any work affecting the plaster on the floors above the ADU. Where the ADU ceiling is a plaster floor-ceiling assembly, the fire separation strategy must account for plaster's specific characteristics.
  • Galvanized plumbing: Pre-1950 Chevy Chase homes frequently retain original or partial galvanized steel supply lines. DMV-GC Group's licensed plumbers assess supply line condition and water pressure during the consultation for every older Chevy Chase home — addressing any degraded galvanized lines before the ADU is designed for tenant use.
  • Electrical panel capacity: Older Chevy Chase homes may have 100-amp or sub-100-amp panels. An ADU sub-panel requires adequate main panel capacity. Where the existing panel is at capacity, an upgrade is required before ADU electrical work is sized. DMV-GC Group's licensed electricians assess panel capacity during the consultation.
  • Carriage house structural condition: Chevy Chase's older carriage houses and detached garages — built in the 1910s–1940s — may have deteriorated sill plates, under-sized framing, and foundation conditions that require structural attention before interior conversion. A structural assessment of any older Chevy Chase carriage house is essential before conversion design begins.
  • Finish quality expectations: Chevy Chase homes are among the most valuable in Montgomery County. An ADU that looks incongruously low-quality relative to the main home reduces both rental appeal and property value. DMV-GC Group specifies finishes for Chevy Chase ADU projects appropriate to the premium position of these homes in the market.
